P01 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, p. 272

At $t = 0$ a $12$ V dc source is switched in, in series with a $4$ $\Omega$ resistor $R_1$, to node A. From node A, a $2$ H inductor goes to the bottom rail, and a $6$ $\Omega$ resistor $R_2$ goes to node B; from node B, a $0.5$ F capacitor goes to the bottom rail. All elements are initially unenergized.

Let $v_C$ be the capacitor voltage and $v_A$ the node-A voltage.

KCL at node B: $C\frac{dv_C}{dt} = \frac{v_A - v_C}{R_2}$, hence $v_A = v_C + R_2 C\, v_C' = v_C + 3\,v_C'$.

KCL at node A, with $i_L$ the (downward) inductor current: $\frac{v_A - 12}{4} + i_L + \frac{v_A - v_C}{6} = 0$. Since $v_A - v_C = 3\,v_C'$, this becomes $i_L = \frac{12 - v_C}{4} - 1.25\,v_C'$. Using $\frac{di_L}{dt} = v_A/L = (v_C + 3\,v_C')/2$ and eliminating $i_L$ gives the second-order equation
$v_C'' + 1.4\,v_C' + 0.4\,v_C = 0$.

$\alpha = 0.7$, $\omega_0 = \sqrt{0.4} \approx 0.632$ rad/s: overdamped, roots $s_1 = -0.7 + 0.3 = -0.4$ and $s_2 = -0.7 - 0.3 = -1$.

Final value: at dc the inductor is a short, so $v_A(\infty) = 0$ and $v_C(\infty) = 0$. Thus $v_C(t) = A e^{-0.4t} + B e^{-t}$.

Initial conditions: $v_C(0^+) = 0$, $i_L(0^+) = 0$. KCL at A at $0^+$: $\frac{v_A - 12}{4} + \frac{v_A}{6} = 0 \Rightarrow 5v_A = 36 \Rightarrow v_A(0^+) = 7.2$ V, so $v_C'(0^+) = \frac{v_A(0^+) - v_C(0^+)}{R_2 C} = \frac{7.2}{3} = 2.4$ V/s.

$A + B = 0$ and $-0.4A - B = 2.4$ give $A = 4$, $B = -4$.

(a) $v_C(t) = 4(e^{-0.4t} - e^{-t})$ V.

(b) $i_L(t) = \frac{12 - v_C}{4} - 1.25\,v_C' = 3 + e^{-0.4t} - 4e^{-t}$ A (check: $i_L(0) = 0$, $i_L(\infty) = 12/4 = 3$ A).

(c) $v_C'(t) = 0 \Rightarrow 4e^{-t} = 1.6e^{-0.4t} \Rightarrow e^{0.6t} = 2.5 \Rightarrow t_p = \frac{\ln 2.5}{0.6} \approx 1.53$ s, where $v_C(t_p) \approx 1.30$ V.
Answer: $v_C(t) = 4(e^{-0.4t} - e^{-t})$ V; $i_L(t) = 3 + e^{-0.4t} - 4e^{-t}$ A; $v_{C,\max} \approx 1.30$ V at $t_p \approx 1.53$ s.
P02 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 8.9, p. 310

A source-free parallel RLC circuit has $L = 10$ mH, $C = 1$ $\mu$F, and a resistor $R$ adjusted so that the characteristic roots are $-8000 \pm j6000$ rad/s. The initial conditions are $v(0) = 10$ V and $i_L(0) = 40$ mA.

$\alpha = 8000$ Np/s, $\omega_d = 6000$ rad/s, so $\omega_0 = \sqrt{\alpha^2 + \omega_d^2} = 10000$ rad/s, consistent with $\omega_0 = 1/\sqrt{LC} = 1/\sqrt{(0.01)(10^{-6})}$.

(a) $R = \frac{1}{2C\alpha} = \frac{1}{2(10^{-6})(8000)} = 62.5$ $\Omega$.

(b) KCL at $0^+$ (all branch currents defined downward from the top node): $i_C(0^+) = -i_R(0^+) - i_L(0^+) = -\frac{10}{62.5} - 0.04 = -0.20$ A, hence $\frac{dv}{dt}\big|_{0^+} = \frac{i_C(0^+)}{C} = -2.0 \times 10^5$ V/s.

(c) $B_1 = v(0) = 10$. Differentiating, $v'(0^+) = -\alpha B_1 + \omega_d B_2$, so $-2.0 \times 10^5 = -80000 + 6000\,B_2 \Rightarrow B_2 = -20$.

Thus $v(t) = e^{-8000 t}(10\cos 6000 t - 20\sin 6000 t)$ V.

(d) $i_L(t) = -\frac{v}{R} - C\frac{dv}{dt} = e^{-8000 t}(0.04\cos 6000 t + 0.22\sin 6000 t)$ A (check: $i_L(0) = 40$ mA, $i_L(\infty) = 0$).
Answer: $R = 62.5$ $\Omega$; $dv/dt|_{0^+} = -2.0 \times 10^5$ V/s; $B_1 = 10$, $B_2 = -20$; $v(t) = e^{-8000t}(10\cos 6000t - 20\sin 6000t)$ V; $i_L(t) = e^{-8000t}(0.04\cos 6000t + 0.22\sin 6000t)$ A.
P03 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 8.10, p. 311

A source-free parallel RLC circuit has $L = 0.4$ H and $C = 10$ $\mu$F, and its resistance is adjusted for critical damping. The initial energy stored in the circuit is 25 mJ, distributed equally between the inductor and the capacitor, with $v(0) = 50$ V and $i_L(0) = 0.25$ A.

$\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{0.4 \times 10^{-5}}} = 500$ rad/s. (Consistency with the given energy: $\tfrac{1}{2}Cv^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}(10^{-5})(50)^2 = 12.5$ mJ and $\tfrac{1}{2}Li_L^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}(0.4)(0.25)^2 = 12.5$ mJ, total 25 mJ.)

(a) Critical damping: $\alpha = \omega_0 = 500$ Np/s, so $R = \frac{1}{2C\alpha} = \frac{1}{2(10^{-5})(500)} = 100$ $\Omega$.

(b) $v(t) = (A_1 + A_2 t)e^{-500 t}$ with $A_1 = v(0) = 50$. KCL at $0^+$: $i_C(0^+) = -\frac{50}{100} - 0.25 = -0.75$ A, so $v'(0^+) = -75000$ V/s. Then $v'(0^+) = A_2 - 500 A_1 \Rightarrow A_2 = -75000 + 25000 = -50000$, and $v(t) = (50 - 50000\,t)e^{-500 t}$ V.

(c) $i_L(t) = i_L(0) + \frac{1}{L}\int_0^t v\,dt = (0.25 + 250\,t)e^{-500 t}$ A (equivalently $i_L = -v/R - C\,v'$; check $i_L(0) = 0.25$ A, $i_L(\infty) = 0$).

(d) $i_R(0^+) = v(0)/R = 50/100 = 0.5$ A.
Answer: $R = 100$ $\Omega$; $v(t) = (50 - 50000\,t)e^{-500t}$ V; $i_L(t) = (0.25 + 250\,t)e^{-500t}$ A; $i_R(0^+) = 0.5$ A.

📖 Adapted from Hayt 8e, Examples 9.10-9.11, p. 355

In the two-loop RLC circuit shown, a current source $4u(t)$ A feeds node A and a $5$ A current source feeds node B; $R = 30$ $\Omega$ connects A to B, $L = 3$ H connects A to the bottom rail, and $C = 1/27$ F connects B to the bottom rail. For $t < 0$ the circuit is in dc steady state.

(a) For $t < 0$ the $4u(t)$ source is off (open circuit). In dc steady state the inductor is a short and the capacitor an open, so the $5$ A source drives $i_L(0^-) = 5$ A through the shorted inductor and $i_R(0^-) = -5$ A through $R$, giving $v_R(0^-) = -150$ V. With $v_A(0^-) = 0$ (shorted inductor), $v_C(0^-) = v_B(0^-) = v_A(0^-) - v_R(0^-) = 150$ V. By continuity: $i_L(0^+) = 5$ A, $v_C(0^+) = 150$ V.

At $t = 0^+$: KCL at A, $i_R(0^+) = 4 - i_L(0^+) = -1$ A; at B, $i_C(0^+) = i_R(0^+) + 5 = 4$ A. So $v_R(0^+) = -30$ V, $v_L(0^+) = v_C(0^+) + v_R(0^+) = 120$ V, and $v_C'(0^+) = i_C(0^+)/C = 4/(1/27) = 108$ V/s.

For $t > 0$ the natural response is found with the sources turned off (open circuits): the circuit reduces to one loop containing $L$, $R$ and $C$ in series, so $LC\,v_C'' + RC\,v_C' + v_C = 0$ with $\alpha = R/(2L) = 5$ Np/s and $\omega_0 = 1/\sqrt{LC} = 3$ rad/s: overdamped, roots $s_1 = -5 + 4 = -1$ and $s_2 = -5 - 4 = -9$.

Final values ($t \to \infty$, C open, L short): the $5$ A source is then balanced by $i_R(\infty) = -5$ A, so $i_L(\infty) = 4 - i_R(\infty) = 9$ A and $v_C(\infty) = -v_R(\infty) = 150$ V.

(b) $v_C(t) = 150 + A e^{-t} + B e^{-9t}$: $A + B = v_C(0^+) - 150 = 0$ and $v_C'(0^+) = -A - 9B = 108$ give $A = 13.5$, $B = -13.5$. Hence $v_C(t) = 150 + 13.5\,e^{-t} - 13.5\,e^{-9t}$ V.

(c) $i_C(t) = C\,v_C'(t) = -0.5\,e^{-t} + 4.5\,e^{-9t}$ A and, from KCL at B, $i_L(t) = 9 - i_C(t) = 9 + 0.5\,e^{-t} - 4.5\,e^{-9t}$ A. Check: $i_L(0^+) = 5$ A and $di_L/dt|_{0^+} = v_L(0^+)/L = 40$ A/s.
Answer: $v_C(0^+) = 150$ V, $i_L(0^+) = 5$ A; $v_C(t) = 150 + 13.5\,e^{-t} - 13.5\,e^{-9t}$ V; $i_L(t) = 9 + 0.5\,e^{-t} - 4.5\,e^{-9t}$ A.

📖 Adapted from Hayt 8e, Example 9.10, p. 355

In the same two-source RLC circuit as the previous problem ($R = 30$ $\Omega$ between nodes A and B, $L = 3$ H from A to the rail, $C = 1/27$ F from B to the rail, sources $4u(t)$ A into A and $5$ A into B, dc steady state for $t < 0$), determine at $t = 0^+$ the first derivatives of all six branch variables: $\dfrac{di_L}{dt}$, $\dfrac{dv_C}{dt}$, $\dfrac{di_R}{dt}$, $\dfrac{dv_R}{dt}$, $\dfrac{di_C}{dt}$, and $\dfrac{dv_L}{dt}$.

P11 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 8.53, p. 314

A series RLC circuit consists of a DC voltage source, a resistor R = 100 Ω, an inductor L = 40 mH, and a capacitor C = 10 mF. The circuit has been in DC steady state for a long time with the source voltage at 200 V. At t = 0, the source voltage suddenly drops to 100 V and remains constant. Find the capacitor voltage v_C(t) for t ≥ 0.

1. Initial Conditions: For t < 0, the circuit is in DC steady state. The capacitor acts as an open circuit and the inductor as a short circuit. Thus, the entire source voltage appears across the capacitor: $v_C(0^-) = 200$ V. The inductor current is $i_L(0^-) = 0$ A. Due to continuity, $v_C(0^+) = 200$ V and $i_L(0^+) = 0$ A.
2. Final Values: For t → ∞, the circuit reaches a new DC steady state with the 100 V source. The capacitor voltage becomes $v_C(\infty) = 100$ V and the inductor current is $i_L(\infty) = 0$ A.
3. Circuit Parameters: The damping factor is $\alpha = \frac{R}{2L} = \frac{100}{2(0.04)} = 1250$ rad/s. The resonant frequency is $\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{0.04 \times 0.01}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{0.0004}} = 50$ rad/s.

1. Initial and Final States
For $t < 0$, the switch is open, resulting in $i(0^-) = 0$ and $v_C(0^-) = 0$. As $t \to \infty$, the circuit reaches steady state, where $i(\infty) = 0$ and $v_C(\infty) = 250$ V.

2. Energy in Inductor
The energy stored in an inductor is given by $W_L = \frac{1}{2} L i^2$. The net change in inductor energy is:
$$ \Delta W_L = \frac{1}{2}L i(\infty)^2 - \frac{1}{2}L i(0)^2 = 0 \text{ J} $$

3. Energy in Capacitor
The net change in capacitor energy is:
$$ \Delta W_C = \frac{1}{2} C v_C(\infty)^2 - \frac{1}{2} C v_C(0)^2 = \frac{1}{2}(0.01)(250)^2 - 0 = 312.5 \text{ J} $$

4. Energy in Resistor
The resistor dissipates energy. To determine the total energy dissipated, apply the principle of conservation of energy. The total energy supplied by the source equals the sum of the energy absorbed by the resistor, inductor, and capacitor:
$$ W_S = W_R + W_C + W_L $$

First, calculate the total energy supplied by the source. Since the source voltage $v_s$ is constant at 250 V, the energy supplied is:
$$ W_S = \int_0^\infty v_s i(t) \, dt = 250 \int_0^\infty i(t) \, dt = 250 [q(\infty) - q(0)] $$
The charge on the capacitor is $q = C v_C$. Thus:
$$ q(\infty) = C v_C(\infty) = 0.01 \times 250 = 2.5 \text{ C} $$
$$ q(0) = 0 \text{ C} $$
$$ W_S = 250 \times (2.5 - 0) = 625 \text{ J} $$

Substitute the known values into the conservation equation:
$$ 625 = W_R + 312.5 + 0 $$
$$ W_R = 312.5 \text{ J} $$

5. Results
(a) Inductor: 0 J (net)
(b) Resistor: 312.5 J
(c) Capacitor: 312.5 J
(d) Source: 625 J delivered
(e) Verification: $625 = 312.5 + 312.5 + 0$. Energy is conserved.
6. (Reference) The transient is overdamped: $\alpha = \frac{R}{2L} = \frac{1000}{3.2} = 312.5$ rad/s and $\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1.6 \times 0.01}} \approx 7.906$ rad/s, with roots $s \approx -0.10$ and $-624.90$ rad/s. The energy balance above does not depend on the damping type.
Answer: (a) $0\ \text{J}$; (b) $312.5\ \text{J}$; (c) $312.5\ \text{J}$; (d) $625\ \text{J}$; (e) Conserved

$$ \omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{20 \times 2}} \approx 0.158 \text{ rad/s}. $$

As $t \to \infty$, the inductor acts as a short and the capacitor as an open, so the full source current flows through the inductor: $i_1(\infty) = 10$ A.

General solution:
$$ i_1(t) = i_1(\infty) + A \cos \omega_0 t + B \sin \omega_0 t = 10 + A \cos 0.158 t + B \sin 0.158 t. $$

Applying $i_1(0^+) = 0$:
$$ 0 = 10 + A \implies A = -10. $$

The initial derivative follows from the inductor voltage; since $v_C(0^+) = 0$ V,
$$ \frac{di_1}{dt}(0^+) = \frac{v_L(0^+)}{L} = 0. $$
Differentiating and evaluating at $t=0$:
$$ \frac{di_1}{dt}(0^+) = -\omega_0 B = 0 \implies B = 0. $$

Substituting the constants:
$$ i_1(t) = 10 - 10 \cos 0.158 t = 10(1 - \cos 0.158 t) \text{ A} \quad (t > 0). $$
Answer: $i_1(t) = 10(1 - \cos 0.158t) \text{ A}$

Design a parallel RLC circuit with $L = 1$ H and $C = 1$ F that produces an underdamped response with a peak voltage of $2$ V. The second peak must be greater than $1$ V. Determine the resistance $R$.

For a parallel RLC circuit, the damping factor and resonant frequency are defined as $\alpha = \frac{1}{2RC}$ and $\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}} = 1$ rad/s, respectively. The system is underdamped when $\alpha < 1$. The step response for a 2 V input is given by:
$$v(t) = 2 + e^{-\alpha t}(A \cos \omega_d t + B \sin \omega_d t)$$
Applying the initial conditions $v(0) = 0$ and $\frac{dv}{dt}(0) = 0$ yields $A = -2$ and $B = \frac{2\alpha}{\omega_d}$.

The peaks of the response occur approximately at $t_n = \frac{n\pi}{\omega_d}$ for light damping. The second peak occurs at $t_2 \approx \frac{2\pi}{\omega_d}$. Evaluating the voltage at this time:
$$v(t_2) \approx 2 - 2 e^{-2\alpha \pi / \omega_d} \cos(2\pi) = 2 - 2 e^{-2\alpha \pi / \omega_d}$$
The condition requires that the second peak exceeds 1 V:
$$2 - 2 e^{-2\alpha \pi / \omega_d} > 1$$
$$1 > 2 e^{-2\alpha \pi / \omega_d}$$
$$e^{-2\alpha \pi / \omega_d} < 0.5$$
Taking the natural logarithm of both sides:
$$-2\alpha \pi / \omega_d < \ln(0.5) = -0.693$$
$$\frac{2\alpha \pi}{\omega_d} > 0.693$$
$$\frac{\alpha}{\omega_d} > \frac{0.693}{2\pi} \approx 0.11$$
Since $\omega_d \approx 1$, the damping factor must satisfy $\alpha > 0.11$. Combined with the underdamped condition $\alpha < 1$, a valid choice is $\alpha = 0.2$.

Solving for the resistance $R$ with $C = 1$ F:
$$\frac{1}{2R(1)} = 0.2$$
$$R = \frac{1}{0.4} = 2.5 \ \Omega$$
Answer: $R = 2.5 \, \Omega$
