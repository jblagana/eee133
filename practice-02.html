P01 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 6.2, p. 212

Consider a single inductor with inductance $L = 5 \text{ mH}$. The current through the inductor is given by $i(t) = 10(e^{-200t} - e^{-800t}) \text{ A}$ for $t \ge 0$, and $i(0^-) = 0$. Determine: (a) the initial voltage $v(0^+)$ across the inductor in the direction of the current; (b) the time $t > 0$ when the voltage is zero; (c) the expression for the power $p(t)$ delivered to the inductor; (d) the time $t_p$ when the power is maximum; (e) the maximum power value; (f) the time $t_w$ when the stored energy is maximum; (g) the maximum stored energy.

(a) The voltage across an inductor is $v(t) = L \frac{di}{dt}$. Differentiating $i(t)$:
$$ \frac{di}{dt} = 10(-200e^{-200t} + 800e^{-800t}) = -2000e^{-200t} + 8000e^{-800t} \text{ A/s} $$
At $t=0$, $\frac{di}{dt} = 6000 \text{ A/s}$. Thus:
$$ v(0^+) = 5 \times 10^{-3} \cdot 6000 = 30 \text{ V} $$

(b) Set $v(t) = 0$:
$$ 5 \times 10^{-3}(-2000e^{-200t} + 8000e^{-800t}) = 0 $$
$$ 4e^{-800t} = e^{-200t} $$
$$ 4 = e^{600t} $$
$$ t = \frac{\ln(4)}{600} = 2.31 \text{ ms} $$

(c)
$$ p(t) = v(t)i(t) = 5 \times 10^{-3}(-2000e^{-200t} + 8000e^{-800t}) \cdot 10(e^{-200t} - e^{-800t}) $$
$$ p(t) = 50(-2e^{-400t} + 5e^{-600t} - 2e^{-1000t}) \text{ W} $$

(d) To find max power, differentiate $p(t)$ with respect to $t$ and set to $0$. Let $x = e^{-200t}$.
$$ p(x) = 50(-2x^2 + 5x^3 - 2x^5) $$
$$ \frac{dp}{dx} = 50(-4x + 15x^2 - 10x^4) $$
Setting $\frac{dp}{dx}=0$ gives roots. The relevant root for $t>0$ is $x = 0.5$ (approx). Solving $10x^4 - 15x^2 + 4 = 0$, let $y=x^2$, $10y^2 - 15y + 4 = 0$.
$$ y = \frac{15 \pm \sqrt{225-160}}{20} = \frac{15 \pm 8}{20} $$
$y=1.15$ (invalid) or $y=0.35$.
$$ x = \sqrt{0.35} = 0.5916 $$
$$ t = -\frac{\ln(0.5916)}{200} = 2.63 \text{ ms} $$

(e) Max power:
$$ p(2.63 \text{ ms}) = 50(-2(0.35) + 5(0.5916)^3 - 2(0.5916)^5) $$
$$ p(2.63 \text{ ms}) = 50(-0.7 + 1.03 - 0.22) = 16.5 \text{ W} $$

(f) Energy:
$$ w(t) = 0.5 L i^2 $$
Max energy occurs when $\frac{di}{dt} = 0$, which is when $v(t)=0$. From (b), $t_w = 2.31 \text{ ms}$.

(g)
$$ i(2.31 \text{ ms}) = 10(e^{-0.462} - e^{-1.848}) = 10(0.63 - 0.157) = 4.73 \text{ A} $$
$$ w_{\text{max}} = 0.5 \cdot 5 \times 10^{-3} \cdot (4.73)^2 = 0.056 \text{ J} = 56 \text{ mJ} $$
Answer: (a) $30\text{ V}$; (b) $2.31\text{ ms}$; (c) $p(t) = 50(-2e^{-400t} + 5e^{-600t} - 2e^{-1000t})\text{ W}$; (d) $2.63\text{ ms}$; (e) $16.5\text{ W}$; (f) $2.31\text{ ms}$; (g) $56\text{ mJ}$
P02 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 6.8, p. 212

A 20 mH inductor is connected to a voltage source v(t) = 100 sin(5000t) V. The initial current is i(0) = -2 A. (a) Find the expression for the current i(t) for t >= 0. (b) Determine the subintervals within the first cycle (0 to 0.2 ms) where the inductor is absorbing power and where it is delivering power.

(a) The voltage-current relationship for an inductor is given by $v(t) = L \frac{di}{dt}$. Solving for the derivative of the current:

$$ \frac{di}{dt} = \frac{v(t)}{L} = \frac{100 \sin(5000t)}{0.02} = 5000 \sin(5000t) \text{ A/s} $$

Integrating with respect to time:

$$ i(t) = \int 5000 \sin(5000t) \, dt = -\frac{5000}{5000} \cos(5000t) + K = -\cos(5000t) + K $$

Applying the initial condition $i(0) = -2 \text{ A}$:

$$ -1 + K = -2 \implies K = -1 $$

Thus, the current is:

$$ i(t) = -1 - \cos(5000t) \text{ A} $$

(b) The instantaneous power is defined as $p(t) = v(t)i(t)$. Substituting the expressions for voltage and current:

$$ p(t) = 100 \sin(5000t) \left[ -1 - \cos(5000t) \right] $$

The inductor absorbs power when $p(t) > 0$. Since $100 > 0$, the condition for absorbing power is:

$$ \sin(5000t) \left[ -1 - \cos(5000t) \right] > 0 $$

The term $\left[ -1 - \cos(5000t) \right]$ is always negative because $\cos(5000t)$ ranges from $-1$ to $1$, making $-1 - \cos(5000t)$ range from $-2$ to $0$. For the product to be positive, $\sin(5000t)$ must be negative.

The period of the sinusoidal signal is:

$$ T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} = \frac{2\pi}{5000} \approx 1.257 \text{ ms} $$

Within the first cycle, $0 < t < 1.257 \text{ ms}$, the argument $5000t$ ranges from $0$ to $2\pi$. The sine function is negative in the interval $(\pi, 2\pi)$. Therefore, the inductor absorbs power when:

$$ \pi < 5000t < 2\pi \implies \frac{\pi}{5000} < t < \frac{2\pi}{5000} $$

Calculating the time boundaries:

$$ \frac{\pi}{5000} \approx 0.628 \text{ ms}, \quad \frac{2\pi}{5000} \approx 1.257 \text{ ms} $$

The inductor absorbs power for $0.628 \text{ ms} < t < 1.257 \text{ ms}$ and delivers power for $0 < t < 0.628 \text{ ms}$.
Answer: (a) $i(t) = -1 - \cos(5000t) \text{ A}$; (b) Delivering: $0 < t < 0.628 \text{ ms}$; Absorbing: $0.628 \text{ ms} < t < 1.257 \text{ ms}$
P03 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 6.14, p. 214

A capacitor with capacitance $C = 1 \mu F$ has a voltage $v(t) = 0$ for $t < 0$ and $v(t) = 20 e^{-5000t} \sin(10000t)$ V for $t >= 0$. (a) Find the initial capacitor current $i(0+)$ in the direction of the voltage drop. (b) Calculate the power delivered to the capacitor at $t = \pi/40$ ms. (c) Calculate the energy stored in the capacitor at $t = \pi/40$ ms.

(a) The current is given by $i(t) = C \frac{dv}{dt}$. The voltage is $v(t) = 20 e^{-5000t} \sin(10000t)$. Differentiating with respect to time yields:
$$ \frac{dv}{dt} = 20 \left[ -5000 e^{-5000t} \sin(10000t) + 10000 e^{-5000t} \cos(10000t) \right] $$
At $t = 0$,
$$ \frac{dv}{dt} = 20 \left[ 0 + 10000 \right] = 200,000 \text{ V/s} $$
Thus,
$$ i(0^+) = 1 \times 10^{-6} \cdot 200,000 = 0.2 \text{ A} = 200 \text{ mA} $$

(b) The time is $t = \frac{\pi}{40} \text{ ms} = \frac{\pi}{40000} \text{ s}$.
$$ 10000t = 10000 \left( \frac{\pi}{40000} \right) = \frac{\pi}{4} $$
$$ 5000t = 5000 \left( \frac{\pi}{40000} \right) = \frac{\pi}{8} $$
The voltage at this instant is:
$$ v\left(\frac{\pi}{40} \text{ ms}\right) = 20 e^{-\pi/8} \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right) = 20 e^{-0.3927} (0.7071) = 20(0.675)(0.7071) = 9.55 \text{ V} $$
The current is:
$$ i(t) = 1 \times 10^{-6} \cdot 20 e^{-5000t} \left[ -5000 \sin(10000t) + 10000 \cos(10000t) \right] $$
$$ i(t) = 20 \times 10^{-6} e^{-5000t} \left[ -5000(0.7071) + 10000(0.7071) \right] $$
$$ i(t) = 20 \times 10^{-6} e^{-\pi/8} (3535.5) = 0.0707 e^{-0.3927} = 0.0707(0.675) = 0.0477 \text{ A} $$
The instantaneous power is:
$$ p(t) = v(t) i(t) = 9.55 \cdot 0.0477 = 0.456 \text{ W} $$

(c) The energy is given by $w(t) = 0.5 C v^2$.
$$ w\left(\frac{\pi}{40} \text{ ms}\right) = 0.5 \cdot 1 \times 10^{-6} \cdot (9.55)^2 = 0.5 \times 10^{-6} \cdot 91.2 = 45.6 \text{ \mu J} $$
Answer: (a) $200\ \text{mA}$; (b) $0.456\ \text{W}$; (c) $45.6\ \mu\text{J}$
P04 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 6.15, p. 214

A capacitor with capacitance $C = 2 \mu F$ has zero voltage and zero current for $t < 0$. For $t \ge 0$, the current is $i(t) = 4 \cos(20000t)$ A. (a) Find the voltage drop $v(t)$ in the direction of the current. (b) Find the maximum instantaneous power delivered to the capacitor. (c) Find the maximum stored energy.

(a) The voltage is given by $v(t) = \frac{1}{C} \int_0^t i(\tau) \, d\tau$. Substituting the given values:
$$v(t) = \frac{1}{2 \times 10^{-6}} \int_0^t 4 \cos(20000\tau) \, d\tau$$
$$v(t) = 2 \times 10^6 \left[ \frac{4}{20000} \sin(20000\tau) \right]_0^t$$
$$v(t) = 2 \times 10^6 \cdot (2 \times 10^{-4}) \sin(20000t)$$
$$v(t) = 400 \sin(20000t) \, \text{V}$$

(b) The instantaneous power is $p(t) = v(t)i(t)$.
$$p(t) = 400 \sin(20000t) \cdot 4 \cos(20000t)$$
$$p(t) = 1600 \sin(20000t) \cos(20000t)$$
Using the trigonometric identity $\sin(2\theta) = 2 \sin(\theta) \cos(\theta)$:
$$p(t) = 800 \sin(40000t) \, \text{W}$$
The maximum value is $800 \, \text{W}$.

(c) The energy stored in the capacitor is $w(t) = \frac{1}{2} C v^2$.
$$w(t) = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2 \times 10^{-6} \cdot (400 \sin(20000t))^2$$
$$w(t) = 1 \times 10^{-6} \cdot 160000 \sin^2(20000t)$$
$$w(t) = 0.16 \sin^2(20000t) \, \text{J}$$
The maximum stored energy is $0.16 \, \text{J} = 160 \, \text{mJ}$.
Answer: (a) $v(t) = 400 \sin(20000t) \text{ V}$; (b) $800 \text{ W}$; (c) $160 \text{ mJ}$
P05 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 6.18, p. 214

A capacitor with capacitance $C = 100 \text{ nF}$ has an initial voltage $v(0) = -50 \text{ V}$. For $t \ge 0$, the current is $i(t) = 50 e^{-10000t} \text{ mA}$. (a) Find the energy stored in the capacitor at $t = 100 \mu\text{s}$. (b) Find the energy stored in the capacitor at $t = \infty$.

First, find $v(t)$.
$$v(t) = v(0) + \frac{1}{C} \int_0^t i(\tau) \, d\tau$$
Given $i(t) = 50 \times 10^{-3} e^{-10000t} \, \text{A}$.
$$\int_0^t 50 \times 10^{-3} e^{-10000\tau} \, d\tau = 50 \times 10^{-3} \left[ -\frac{1}{10000} e^{-10000\tau} \right]_0^t$$
$$= -5 \times 10^{-6} \left( e^{-10000t} - 1 \right) = 5 \times 10^{-6} \left( 1 - e^{-10000t} \right)$$
$$v(t) = -50 + \frac{1}{100 \times 10^{-9}} \cdot 5 \times 10^{-6} \left( 1 - e^{-10000t} \right)$$
$$= -50 + 50 \left( 1 - e^{-10000t} \right)$$
$$= -50 + 50 - 50 e^{-10000t}$$
$$= -50 e^{-10000t} \, \text{V}$$

(a) At $t = 100 \, \mu\text{s} = 1 \times 10^{-4} \, \text{s}$, $10000t = 1$.
$$v(100 \, \mu\text{s}) = -50 e^{-1} = -50(0.3679) = -18.39 \, \text{V}$$
$$w = \frac{1}{2} C v^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 100 \times 10^{-9} \cdot (-18.39)^2 = 50 \times 10^{-9} \cdot 338.2 = 16.91 \, \mu\text{J}$$

(b) As $t \to \infty$, $v(t) \to 0$.
$$w(\infty) = 0 \, \text{J}$$
Answer: (a) $16.91 \, \mu\text{J}$; (b) $0 \, \text{J}$

P06 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 6.19, p. 214

A capacitor with capacitance $C = 100 \text{ nF}$ has an initial voltage of $20 \text{ V}$ (drop in the reference direction of the current). A rectangular current pulse is applied: $i(t) = 40 \text{ mA}$ for $0 < t < 50 \mu\text{s}$, $i(t) = -20 \text{ mA}$ for $50 \mu\text{s} < t < 150 \mu\text{s}$, and $i(t) = 0$ for $t > 150 \mu\text{s}$. Find the voltage $v(t)$ in each of the three intervals.

The capacitor voltage is determined by the integral of the current:
$$v(t) = v(0) + \frac{1}{C} \int_0^t i(\tau) \, d\tau$$
Given $C = 100 \times 10^{-9} \, \text{F}$, the reciprocal is $\frac{1}{C} = 10^7 \, \text{F}^{-1}$.

For the interval $0 < t < 50 \, \mu\text{s}$, the current is $i = 40 \times 10^{-3} \, \text{A}$. The voltage is:
$$v(t) = 20 + 10^7 \cdot 40 \times 10^{-3} \cdot t = 20 + 400{,}000 \, t$$
At $t = 50 \, \mu\text{s} = 50 \times 10^{-6} \, \text{s}$:
$$v = 20 + 400{,}000(50 \times 10^{-6}) = 20 + 20 = 40 \, \text{V}$$

For the interval $50 \, \mu\text{s} < t < 150 \, \mu\text{s}$, the current is $i = -20 \times 10^{-3} \, \text{A}$. The voltage is:
$$v(t) = v(50 \, \mu\text{s}) + 10^7 \cdot (-20 \times 10^{-3}) \cdot (t - 50 \, \mu\text{s}) = 40 - 200{,}000 (t - 50 \, \mu\text{s})$$
At $t = 150 \, \mu\text{s}$, the time difference is $t - 50 \, \mu\text{s} = 100 \, \mu\text{s} = 100 \times 10^{-6} \, \text{s}$. Thus:
$$v = 40 - 200{,}000(100 \times 10^{-6}) = 40 - 20 = 20 \, \text{V}$$

For the interval $t > 150 \, \mu\text{s}$, the current is $i = 0$. The voltage remains constant:
$$v(t) = v(150 \, \mu\text{s}) = 20 \, \text{V}$$
Answer: $v(t) = 20 + 400{,}000t \text{ V}$ for $0 < t < 50 \text{ }\mu\text{s}$;
$v(t) = 40 - 200{,}000(t - 50 \text{ }\mu\text{s}) \text{ V}$ for $50 \text{ }\mu\text{s} < t < 150 \text{ }\mu\text{s}$;
$v(t) = 20 \text{ V}$ for $t > 150 \text{ }\mu\text{s}$
P07 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 6.21, p. 215

A capacitor with capacitance $C = 1 \mu F$ is subjected to a voltage pulse defined as: $v(t) = 20$ V for $0 \leq t \leq 1$ s; $v(t) = 20(2-t)$ V for $1 < t \leq 2$ s; $v(t) = 0$ elsewhere. Derive the expression for the capacitor current $i(t)$ during the interval $0 \leq t \leq 2$ s.

$i(t) = C \frac{dv}{dt}$. $C = 1 \times 10^{-6} \text{ F}$. For $0 < t < 1 \text{ s}$, $v(t) = 20 \text{ V}$ (constant). $\frac{dv}{dt} = 0$. $i(t) = 0$. At $t = 0$, there is a step from $0$ to $20 \text{ V}$, which implies an impulse current of charge $Q = C \Delta V = 1 \times 10^{-6} \times 20 = 20 \text{ \mu C}$. For $1 < t < 2 \text{ s}$, $v(t) = 20(2 - t) = 40 - 20t$. $\frac{dv}{dt} = -20 \text{ V/s}$. $i(t) = 1 \times 10^{-6} \times (-20) = -20 \text{ \mu A}$. P08 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 6.25, p. 215

Two inductors, $L_1 = 5 \text{ H}$ and $L_2 = 15 \text{ H}$, are connected in parallel. The combination is connected to a load at $t=0$. The voltage across the parallel combination is $v(t) = 900 e^{-10t} \text{ V}$ for $t > 0$. The initial currents are $i_1(0) = 2 \text{ A}$ and $i_2(0) = -8 \text{ A}$. (a) Find the equivalent inductance and the total current $i(t)$. (b) Find $i_1(t)$. (c) Find $i_2(t)$. (d) Calculate the energy delivered to the load from $t=0$ to $t=\infty$. (e) Calculate the initial stored energy. (f) Calculate the energy trapped in the inductors at $t=\infty$.

(a) The equivalent inductance is given by:
$$L_{eq} = \frac{L_1 L_2}{L_1 + L_2} = \frac{5 \cdot 15}{5 + 15} = \frac{75}{20} = 3.75 \text{ H}$$
The total current is the sum of the individual currents:
$$i(t) = i_1(t) + i_2(t)$$
The voltage across the equivalent inductance is related to the total current by:
$$v = L_{eq} \frac{di}{dt}$$
Solving for the derivative of the current:
$$\frac{di}{dt} = \frac{v}{L_{eq}} = \frac{900 e^{-10t}}{3.75} = 240 e^{-10t} \text{ A/s}$$
Integrating to find the total current:
$$i(t) = \int 240 e^{-10t} \, dt = -24 e^{-10t} + K$$
The initial total current is:
$$i(0) = i_1(0) + i_2(0) = 2 - 8 = -6 \text{ A}$$
Substituting $t=0$ into the expression for $i(t)$:
$$-24 + K = -6 \implies K = 18$$
Thus, the total current is:
$$i(t) = 18 - 24 e^{-10t} \text{ A}$$

(b) The current through the first inductor is:
$$i_1(t) = i_1(0) + \frac{1}{L_1} \int_0^t v(\tau) \, d\tau$$
$$i_1(t) = 2 + \frac{1}{5} \int_0^t 900 e^{-10\tau} \, d\tau$$
$$i_1(t) = 2 + 180 \left[ -0.1 e^{-10\tau} \right]_0^t$$
$$i_1(t) = 2 + 180 \left( -0.1 e^{-10t} + 0.1 \right)$$
$$i_1(t) = 2 + 18 (1 - e^{-10t})$$
$$i_1(t) = 20 - 18 e^{-10t} \text{ A}$$

(c) The current through the second inductor is:
$$i_2(t) = i_2(0) + \frac{1}{L_2} \int_0^t v(\tau) \, d\tau$$
$$i_2(t) = -8 + \frac{1}{15} \int_0^t 900 e^{-10\tau} \, d\tau$$
$$i_2(t) = -8 + 60 \left( -0.1 e^{-10t} + 0.1 \right)$$
$$i_2(t) = -8 + 6 (1 - e^{-10t})$$
$$i_2(t) = -2 - 6 e^{-10t} \text{ A}$$
Verification:
$$i_1(t) + i_2(t) = (20 - 18 e^{-10t}) + (-2 - 6 e^{-10t}) = 18 - 24 e^{-10t} \text{ A}$$
This matches the total current derived in part (a).

(d) The energy delivered to the load is determined by the power integral. The power delivered to the load is defined as $p_{load} = v \cdot i_{load}$. Based on the passive sign convention for the inductors, the total current $i(t)$ flows into the positive terminal of the inductor combination. Therefore, the current flowing out of the inductors into the load is $-i(t)$.
$$p_{load} = v(t) \cdot (-i(t)) = -v(t) i(t)$$
$$p_{load} = -900 e^{-10t} (18 - 24 e^{-10t})$$
$$p_{load} = -16200 e^{-10t} + 21600 e^{-20t} \text{ W}$$
The energy delivered to the load is the integral of power from $t=0$ to $t=\infty$:
$$W_{load} = \int_0^\infty p_{load} \, dt = \int_0^\infty (-16200 e^{-10t} + 21600 e^{-20t}) \, dt$$
$$W_{load} = \left[ \frac{-16200}{-10} e^{-10t} + \frac{21600}{-20} e^{-20t} \right]_0^\infty$$
$$W_{load} = \left[ 1620 e^{-10t} - 1080 e^{-20t} \right]_0^\infty$$
$$W_{load} = (0 - 0) - (1620 - 1080)$$
$$W_{load} = -540 \text{ J}$$

(e) The initial energy stored in the inductors is:
$$W_{initial} = \frac{1}{2} L_1 i_1(0)^2 + \frac{1}{2} L_2 i_2(0)^2$$
$$W_{initial} = \frac{1}{2}(5)(2)^2 + \frac{1}{2}(15)(-8)^2$$
$$W_{initial} = \frac{1}{2}(5)(4) + \frac{1}{2}(15)(64)$$
$$W_{initial} = 10 + 480 = 490 \text{ J}$$

(f) At $t=\infty$, $e^{-10t} = 0$. The final currents are:
$$i_1(\infty) = 20 \text{ A}$$
$$i_2(\infty) = -2 \text{ A}$$
The final energy stored in the inductors is:
$$W_{final} = \frac{1}{2} L_1 i_1(\infty)^2 + \frac{1}{2} L_2 i_2(\infty)^2$$
$$W_{final} = \frac{1}{2}(5)(20)^2 + \frac{1}{2}(15)(-2)^2$$
$$W_{final} = \frac{1}{2}(5)(400) + \frac{1}{2}(15)(4)$$
$$W_{final} = 1000 + 30 = 1030 \text{ J}$$
The change in energy stored in the inductors is:
$$\Delta W_L = W_{final} - W_{initial} = 1030 - 490 = 540 \text{ J}$$
By conservation of energy, the energy delivered to the load is equal to the negative of the change in stored energy (assuming no other sources):
$$W_{load} = -\Delta W_L = -540 \text{ J}$$
The negative sign indicates that the load delivers $540 \text{ J}$ to the inductors.
Answer: (a) $L_{eq} = 3.75 \text{ H}$, $i(t) = 18 - 24 e^{-10t} \text{ A}$; (b) $i_1(t) = 20 - 18 e^{-10t} \text{ A}$; (c) $i_2(t) = -2 - 6 e^{-10t} \text{ A}$; (d) $-540 \text{ J}$; (e) $490 \text{ J}$; (f) $1030 \text{ J}$
P09 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 6.32, p. 216

Two capacitors, $C_1 = 10 \text{ nF}$ and $C_2 = 30 \text{ nF}$, are connected in series. The combination is connected to a load at $t=0$. The initial voltages are $v_1(0) = 30 \text{ V}$ and $v_2(0) = 10 \text{ V}$, with polarities additive (positive terminals facing each other is NOT the case, they are in series aiding the total voltage drop). Specifically, the total voltage $v_o = v_1 + v_2$. The current flowing through the series combination is $i(t) = -1000 e^{-500t} \mu \text{ A}$ for $t > 0$. (a) Find the equivalent capacitance and the total voltage $v_o(t)$. (b) Find $v_1(t)$. (c) Find $v_2(t)$. (d) Calculate the energy delivered to the load. (e) Calculate the initial stored energy. (f) Calculate the energy trapped in the capacitors.

Calculate the energy stored in a 2 nH inductor for the following current values: (a) 0 mA, (b) 2 mA, (c) 10 A, and (d) 4 sin(100t) mA for t > 0.

The energy stored in an inductor is given by $w = \frac{1}{2} L i^2$. (a) For $i = 0$ mA, $w = \frac{1}{2}(2 \times 10^{-9})(0)^2 = 0$ J. (b) For $i = 2$ mA $= 2 \times 10^{-3}$ A, $w = \frac{1}{2}(2 \times 10^{-9})(2 \times 10^{-3})^2 = 10^{-9} \times 4 \times 10^{-6} = 4 \times 10^{-15}$ J $= 4$ fJ. (c) For $i = 10$ A, $w = \frac{1}{2}(2 \times 10^{-9})(10)^2 = 10^{-9} \times 100 = 10^{-7}$ J $= 0.1$ $\mu$J. (d) For $i(t) = 4 \sin(100t)$ mA, $w(t) = \frac{1}{2}(2 \times 10^{-9})(4 \times 10^{-3} \sin(100t))^2 = 10^{-9} \times 16 \times 10^{-6} \sin^2(100t) = 16 \times 10^{-15} \sin^2(100t)$ J $= 16 \sin^2(100t)$ fJ.
Answer: (a) $0\ \text{J}$; (b) $4\ \text{fJ}$; (c) $0.1\ \mu\text{J}$; (d) $16 \sin^2(100t)\ \text{fJ}$

Consider a DC circuit with a 12 V source connected to a series combination of a 4 kΩ resistor and a 2 mH inductor. The circuit has been connected for a very long time. Find the current $i_x$ through the inductor and the energy stored in it.

In DC steady state, an inductor behaves as a short circuit (zero voltage drop). The circuit reduces to the 12 V source in series with the 4 kΩ resistor. 📖 Adapted from Hayt 8e, Prob 7.4 #47, p. 252

Three inductors with values $L_1 = 2$ H, $L_2 = 4$ H, and $L_3 = 4$ H are connected in parallel. The total current entering the parallel combination is $i_s(t) = 10 e^{-100t}$ A for $t \ge 0$. The initial current in $L_1$ is $i_1(0) = 5$ A, and the initial currents in $L_2$ and $L_3$ are 0 A. (a) Find the equivalent inductance. (b) Find the voltage $v(t)$ across the inductors for $t \ge 0$. (c) Find the current $i_1(t)$ for $t \ge 0$.

(a) For parallel inductors, $\frac{1}{L_{eq}} = \frac{1}{L_1} + \frac{1}{L_2} + \frac{1}{L_3} = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{4} = 1$ S. Thus, $L_{eq} = 1$ H. (b) The voltage across the parallel combination is $v(t) = L_{eq} \frac{di_s}{dt}$. A series circuit consists of a voltage source $v_s(t) = 50 e^{-50t}$ V, a 2 µF capacitor, and a 2 µF capacitor. The initial voltage across the first capacitor is $v_1(0) = 10$ V, and the initial voltage across the second is $v_2(0) = 0$ V. (a) Find the equivalent capacitance. (b) Find the current $i(t)$ for $t \ge 0$. (c) Find the voltage $v_1(t)$ for $t \ge 0$.

P27 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Hayt 8e, Prob 7.5 #52, p. 254

An ideal op-amp integrator circuit has an input resistor $R_1 = 50$ kΩ and a feedback capacitor $C_f = 100$ µF. The input voltage is $v_s(t) = 10 \sin(100t)$ mV. Assuming the initial output voltage is zero, find the output voltage $v_{out}(t)$.

For an ideal inverting integrator, the output voltage is given by $v_{out}(t) = -\frac{1}{R_1 C_f} \int_0^t v_s(\tau) d\tau + v_{out}(0)$. With the initial condition $v_{out}(0) = 0$, the gain coefficient is calculated as:
$$ \frac{1}{R_1 C_f} = \frac{1}{(50 \times 10^3)(100 \times 10^{-6})} = \frac{1}{5} = 0.2 \text{ s}^{-1} $$
The input signal is $v_s(t) = 10 \sin(100t)$ mV. The definite integral of the input from 0 to $t$ is:
$$ \int_0^t 10 \sin(100\tau) d\tau = \left[ -\frac{10}{100} \cos(100\tau) \right]_0^t = -0.1 (\cos(100t) - \cos(0)) = 0.1 - 0.1 \cos(100t) \text{ mV} $$
Substituting this result into the integrator equation yields:
$$ v_{out}(t) = -0.2 (0.1 - 0.1 \cos(100t)) = -0.02 + 0.02 \cos(100t) \text{ mV} $$
This expression can be written as:
$$ v_{out}(t) = 0.02(\cos(100t) - 1) \text{ mV} $$
Answer: $v_{out}(t) = 0.02(\cos(100t) - 1) \text{ mV}$
P28 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Hayt 8e, Prob 7.5 #53, p. 254

An ideal op-amp differentiator circuit has an input inductor $L_1 = 10$ mH and a feedback resistor $R_f = 5$ kΩ. The input voltage is $v_s(t)$. Derive the expression for the output voltage $v_{out}(t)$ in terms of $v_s(t)$.

For an ideal inverting differentiator with a capacitor $C_1$ in the input path and a resistor $R_f$ in the feedback path, the current through the capacitor is $i_C = C_1 \frac{dv_s}{dt}$. Since the op-amp input is a virtual ground, this current flows through the feedback resistor. The output voltage is $v_{out}(t) = -i_C R_f$. Substituting the expression for $i_C$, we get $v_{out}(t) = -R_f C_1 \frac{dv_s}{dt}$. Given $R_f = 5$ kΩ $= 5000$ Ω and $C_1 = 10$ µF $= 10 \times 10^{-6}$ F, the gain coefficient is $R_f C_1 = 5000 \times 10 \times 10^{-6} = 0.05$ s. Thus, $v_{out}(t) = -0.05 \frac{dv_s}{dt}$.
Answer: $$v_{out}(t) = -0.05 \frac{dv_s}{dt}$$
P29 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Hayt 8e, Probs 7.5 #55, #58, p. 258

Design an op-amp differentiator circuit to measure the rate of change of a temperature sensor signal. The sensor outputs 10 mV for every 1 °C. The desired output is 1 V for every 1 °C/s. Determine the values of the input capacitor $C_1$ and feedback resistor $R_f$ if $C_1 = 1$ µF.

The sensor output voltage is $v_s(t) = k_T T(t)$, where $k_T = 10$ mV/°C $= 0.01$ V/°C. The desired output is $v_{out}(t) = k_{out} \frac{dT}{dt}$, where $k_{out} = 1$ V/(°C/s). The differentiator circuit produces $v_{out}(t) = -R_f C_1 \frac{dv_s}{dt}$. Substituting $v_s(t)$, we get $v_{out}(t) = -R_f C_1 k_T \frac{dT}{dt}$. We want the magnitude of the gain to match $k_{out}$. So, $R_f C_1 k_T = k_{out}$. Solving for $R_f$: $R_f = \frac{k_{out}}{C_1 k_T}$. Given $C_1 = 1$ µF $= 10^{-6}$ F, $k_T = 0.01$ V/°C, and $k_{out} = 1$ V/(°C/s). $R_f = \frac{1}{10^{-6} \times 0.01} = \frac{1}{10^{-8}} = 10^8$ Ω $= 100$ MΩ. This is a very high resistance, but theoretically correct for the specified calibration.
Answer: $R_f = 100 \text{ M}\Omega$

A DC circuit consists of a 10 V source in series with a 100 Ω resistor and a 5 mH inductor. There is also a 50 Ω resistor in parallel with the inductor. The circuit has been connected for a long time. (a) Find the current through the inductor. (b) Find the power dissipated by the 50 Ω resistor.

In DC steady state, the inductor acts as a short circuit. The 50 Ω resistor is in parallel with the shorted inductor, so the voltage across the 50 Ω resistor is 0 V. Consequently, the current through the 50 Ω resistor is 0 A, and the power dissipated by it is 0 W. The entire circuit current flows through the inductor. The total resistance seen by the source is the 100 Ω series resistor (since the parallel branch is shorted). The current $i_L$ is $i_L = \frac{V}{R_{series}} = \frac{10 \text{ V}}{100 \text{ Ω}} = 0.1$ A $= 100$ mA. (a) The current through the inductor is 100 mA. (b) The power dissipated by the 50 Ω resistor is $P = I^2 R = 0^2 \times 50 = 0$ W.
Answer: (a) $100\ \text{mA}$; (b) $0\ \text{W}$
