P01 · Circuit Problem

A 100 µF capacitor has a voltage across it given by $v(t) = 5t$ V for $t \ge 0$. Find the current $i(t)$ flowing through the capacitor.

Given $C = 100 \, \mu\text{F} = 10^{-4} \, \text{F}$ and $v(t) = 5t \, \text{V}$.
Using the capacitor V-I relation:
$i(t) = C \frac{dv(t)}{dt}$
$i(t) = 10^{-4} \cdot \frac{d}{dt}(5t)$
$i(t) = 10^{-4} \cdot 5 = 5 \times 10^{-4} \, \text{A}$
$i(t) = 0.5 \, \text{mA}$
Answer: 0.5 mA
P02 · Circuit Problem

A 2 H inductor has a current through it given by $i(t) = 3t^2$ A for $t \ge 0$. Find the voltage $v(t)$ across the inductor.

Given $L = 2 \, \text{H}$ and $i(t) = 3t^2 \, \text{A}$.
Using the inductor V-I relation:
$v(t) = L \frac{di(t)}{dt}$
$v(t) = 2 \cdot \frac{d}{dt}(3t^2)$
$v(t) = 2 \cdot (6t) = 12t \, \text{V}$
Answer: 12t V
P03 · Circuit Problem

Calculate the energy stored in a 4 µF capacitor charged to 10 V.

Given $C = 4 \, \mu\text{F} = 4 \times 10^{-6} \, \text{F}$ and $v = 10 \, \text{V}$.
Using the energy formula:
$w = \frac{1}{2} C v^2$
$w = \frac{1}{2} (4 \times 10^{-6}) (10)^2$
$w = \frac{1}{2} (4 \times 10^{-6}) (100)$
$w = 2 \times 10^{-4} \, \text{J}$
$w = 200 \, \mu\text{J}$
Answer: 200 µJ
P04 · Circuit Problem

Calculate the energy stored in a 5 mH inductor carrying a current of 2 A.

Given $L = 5 \, \text{mH} = 5 \times 10^{-3} \, \text{H}$ and $i = 2 \, \text{A}$.
Using the energy formula:
$w = \frac{1}{2} L i^2$
$w = \frac{1}{2} (5 \times 10^{-3}) (2)^2$
$w = \frac{1}{2} (5 \times 10^{-3}) (4)$
$w = 10 \times 10^{-3} \, \text{J}$
$w = 10 \, \text{mJ}$
Answer: 10 mJ
P05 · Circuit Problem

Find the equivalent capacitance of three capacitors connected in parallel: $C_1 = 10 \, \mu\text{F}$, $C_2 = 20 \, \mu\text{F}$, and $C_3 = 30 \, \mu\text{F}$.

For capacitors in parallel, the equivalent capacitance is the sum of individual capacitances:
$C_{eq} = C_1 + C_2 + C_3$
$C_{eq} = 10 \, \mu\text{F} + 20 \, \mu\text{F} + 30 \, \mu\text{F}$
$C_{eq} = 60 \, \mu\text{F}$
Answer: 60 µF
P06 · Circuit Problem

Find the equivalent inductance of two inductors connected in series: $L_1 = 4 \, \text{H}$ and $L_2 = 6 \, \text{H}$ (assume no mutual coupling).

For inductors in series (uncoupled), the equivalent inductance is the sum of individual inductances:
$L_{eq} = L_1 + L_2$
$L_{eq} = 4 \, \text{H} + 6 \, \text{H}$
$L_{eq} = 10 \, \text{H}$
Answer: 10 H
P07 · Circuit Problem

Find the equivalent capacitance of two capacitors connected in series: $C_1 = 10 \, \mu\text{F}$ and $C_2 = 20 \, \mu\text{F}$.

For capacitors in series, the reciprocal of the equivalent capacitance is the sum of the reciprocals:
$\frac{1}{C_{eq}} = \frac{1}{C_1} + \frac{1}{C_2}$
$\frac{1}{C_{eq}} = \frac{1}{10} + \frac{1}{20} = \frac{2}{20} + \frac{1}{20} = \frac{3}{20}$
$C_{eq} = \frac{20}{3} \, \mu\text{F} \approx 6.67 \, \mu\text{F}$
Answer: 6.67 µF
P08 · Circuit Problem

Find the equivalent inductance of two inductors connected in parallel: $L_1 = 4 \, \text{H}$ and $L_2 = 6 \, \text{H}$ (assume no mutual coupling).

For inductors in parallel (uncoupled), the reciprocal of the equivalent inductance is the sum of the reciprocals:
$\frac{1}{L_{eq}} = \frac{1}{L_1} + \frac{1}{L_2}$
$\frac{1}{L_{eq}} = \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{6} = \frac{3}{12} + \frac{2}{12} = \frac{5}{12}$
$L_{eq} = \frac{12}{5} \, \text{H} = 2.4 \, \text{H}$
Answer: 2.4 H
P09 · Circuit Problem

A 100 µF capacitor is connected in a DC circuit. After a long time, the voltage across it is found to be 12 V. Calculate the energy stored in the capacitor.

In DC steady state, the capacitor acts as an open circuit, but it holds a constant voltage.
Given $C = 100 \, \mu\text{F} = 10^{-4} \, \text{F}$ and $v = 12 \, \text{V}$.
$w = \frac{1}{2} C v^2$
$w = \frac{1}{2} (10^{-4}) (12)^2$
$w = \frac{1}{2} (10^{-4}) (144)$
$w = 72 \times 10^{-4} \, \text{J}$
$w = 7.2 \, \text{mJ}$
Answer: 7.2 mJ
P10 · Circuit Problem

An inductor with $L = 2 \, \text{H}$ has a current $i(t) = 2 \sin(100t)$ A. Find the instantaneous power $p(t)$ absorbed by the inductor at $t = \frac{\pi}{200}$ s.

First, find the voltage $v(t)$:
$v(t) = L \frac{di}{dt} = 2 \cdot \frac{d}{dt}(2 \sin(100t)) = 2 \cdot (200 \cos(100t)) = 400 \cos(100t) \, \text{V}$.
Now, calculate power $p(t) = v(t) i(t)$:
$p(t) = [400 \cos(100t)] \cdot [2 \sin(100t)] = 800 \sin(100t) \cos(100t)$.
Using the identity $\sin(2\theta) = 2 \sin\theta \cos\theta$, we get $p(t) = 400 \sin(200t) \, \text{W}$.
At $t = \frac{\pi}{200}$:
$p(\frac{\pi}{200}) = 400 \sin(200 \cdot \frac{\pi}{200}) = 400 \sin(\pi) = 0 \, \text{W}$.
Answer: 0 W
P11 · Circuit Problem

Two capacitors $C_1 = 10 \, \mu\text{F}$ and $C_2 = 20 \, \mu\text{F}$ are connected in series. If the total voltage across the combination is 30 V, find the voltage across each capacitor.

First, find $C_{eq}$:
$\frac{1}{C_{eq}} = \frac{1}{10} + \frac{1}{20} = \frac{3}{20} \implies C_{eq} = \frac{20}{3} \, \mu\text{F}$.
Total charge $Q = C_{eq} V_{total} = \frac{20}{3} \, \mu\text{F} \cdot 30 \, \text{V} = 200 \, \mu\text{C}$.
In series, charge is the same on all capacitors.
$V_1 = \frac{Q}{C_1} = \frac{200 \, \mu\text{C}}{10 \, \mu\text{F}} = 20 \, \text{V}$.
$V_2 = \frac{Q}{C_2} = \frac{200 \, \mu\text{C}}{20 \, \mu\text{F}} = 10 \, \text{V}$.
Check: $V_1 + V_2 = 20 + 10 = 30 \, \text{V}$. Correct.
Answer: V1 = 20 V, V2 = 10 V
P12 · Circuit Problem

Two inductors $L_1 = 2 \, \text{H}$ and $L_2 = 4 \, \text{H}$ are connected in parallel. If the total current entering the combination is 6 A, find the current through each inductor.

First, find $L_{eq}$:
$\frac{1}{L_{eq}} = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{4} \implies L_{eq} = \frac{4}{3} \, \text{H}$.
Voltage across parallel combination $V = L_{eq} \frac{di_{total}}{dt}$.
Since $V_1 = V_2$, we have $L_1 \frac{di_1}{dt} = L_2 \frac{di_2}{dt}$.
Thus, $\frac{di_1}{dt} = \frac{L_2}{L_1} \frac{di_2}{dt}$.
Also $i_{total} = i_1 + i_2 \implies \frac{di_{total}}{dt} = \frac{di_1}{dt} + \frac{di_2}{dt}$.
Substituting: $\frac{di_{total}}{dt} = \frac{L_2}{L_1} \frac{di_2}{dt} + \frac{di_2}{dt} = \frac{di_2}{dt} (\frac{L_2 + L_1}{L_1})$.
So, $\frac{di_2}{dt} = \frac{L_1}{L_1 + L_2} \frac{di_{total}}{dt}$.
Integrating (assuming zero initial current): $i_2 = \frac{L_1}{L_1 + L_2} i_{total}$.
$i_2 = \frac{2}{2 + 4} (6) = \frac{2}{6} (6) = 2 \, \text{A}$.
$i_1 = i_{total} - i_2 = 6 - 2 = 4 \, \text{A}$.
Note: Current divides inversely proportional to inductance.
Answer: I1 = 4 A, I2 = 2 A
P13 · Circuit Problem

A circuit consists of a 12 V DC source connected to a series combination of a 4 kΩ resistor and a 100 µF capacitor. Find the energy stored in the capacitor at $t = \infty$.

At $t = \infty$, the capacitor is fully charged and acts as an open circuit.
No current flows through the resistor, so there is no voltage drop across the resistor.
Therefore, the entire source voltage appears across the capacitor: $v_C(\infty) = 12 \, \text{V}$.
Energy stored:
$w = \frac{1}{2} C v^2 = \frac{1}{2} (100 \times 10^{-6}) (12)^2$
$w = \frac{1}{2} (10^{-4}) (144) = 72 \times 10^{-4} \, \text{J} = 7.2 \, \text{mJ}$.
Answer: 7.2 mJ
P14 · Circuit Problem

A circuit consists of a 10 A DC source connected to a parallel combination of a 5 Ω resistor and a 2 H inductor. Find the energy stored in the inductor at $t = \infty$.

At $t = \infty$, the inductor acts as a short circuit.
All the current from the source will flow through the short circuit (inductor) because it offers zero resistance, while the resistor is in parallel.
Thus, $i_L(\infty) = 10 \, \text{A}$.
Energy stored:
$w = \frac{1}{2} L i^2 = \frac{1}{2} (2) (10)^2$
$w = 1 \cdot 100 = 100 \, \text{J}$.
Answer: 100 J
P15 · Circuit Problem

Find the equivalent capacitance of a network where $C_1 = 6 \, \mu\text{F}$ and $C_2 = 3 \, \mu\text{F}$ are in series, and this combination is in parallel with $C_3 = 2 \, \mu\text{F}$.

Step 1: Find equivalent capacitance of the series branch ($C_{12}$).
$\frac{1}{C_{12}} = \frac{1}{C_1} + \frac{1}{C_2} = \frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{6} + \frac{2}{6} = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2}$.
$C_{12} = 2 \, \mu\text{F}$.
Step 2: Find total equivalent capacitance ($C_{eq}$) of $C_{12}$ in parallel with $C_3$.
$C_{eq} = C_{12} + C_3 = 2 \, \mu\text{F} + 2 \, \mu\text{F} = 4 \, \mu\text{F}$.
Answer: 4 µF
