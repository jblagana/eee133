P01 · Circuit Problem

Consider a single inductor with L = 2 mH. The current through the inductor is given by i(t) = 10(e^{-200t} - e^{-800t}) A for t >= 0, with i(0-) = 0. Determine: (a) the initial voltage across the inductor at t = 0+ in the direction of the current; (b) the time t > 0 when the voltage is zero; (c) the expression for the power p(t) delivered to the inductor; (d) the time at which the power is maximum; (e) the maximum power value; (f) the time at which the stored energy is maximum; (g) the maximum stored energy.

(a) The voltage is $v(t) = L \frac{di}{dt}$. Differentiating $i(t)$: $\frac{di}{dt} = 10(-200e^{-200t} + 800e^{-800t}) = -2000e^{-200t} + 8000e^{-800t} \text{ A/s}$. At $t=0$, $\frac{di}{dt} = 6000 \text{ A/s}$. Thus $v(0^+) = 2 \times 10^{-3} \cdot 6000 = 12 \text{ V}$.

(b) Set $v(t) = 0$:
$$ -2000e^{-200t} + 8000e^{-800t} = 0 $$
$$ 4e^{-800t} = e^{-200t} $$
$$ 4 = e^{600t} $$
$$ t = \frac{\ln(4)}{600} \approx 2.31 \text{ ms} $$

(c) $p(t) = v(t)i(t) = 2 \times 10^{-3}(-2000e^{-200t} + 8000e^{-800t}) \cdot 10(e^{-200t} - e^{-800t}) = 20(-2e^{-400t} + 5e^{-600t} - 2e^{-1000t}) \text{ W}$.

(d) To find max $p$, differentiate $p(t)$ with respect to $t$ and set to $0$. Let $u = e^{-200t}$.
$$ p(u) = 20(-2u^2 + 5u^3 - 2u^5) $$
$$ \frac{dp}{du} = 20(-4u + 15u^2 - 10u^4) $$
Setting $\frac{dp}{du}=0$ gives $10u^4 - 15u^2 + 4u = 0 \Rightarrow 5u(2u^3 - 3u + 0.8) = 0$. The relevant root for $u$ in $(0,1)$ is $u \approx 0.5$.
$$ e^{-200t} = 0.5 \Rightarrow t = \frac{\ln(2)}{200} \approx 3.47 \text{ ms} $$

(e) Substitute $t = \frac{\ln(2)}{200}$ into $p(t)$. $u=0.5$.
$$ p = 20(-2(0.25) + 5(0.125) - 2(0.03125)) = 20(-0.5 + 0.625 - 0.0625) = 20(0.0625) = 1.25 \text{ W} $$

(f) Energy $w(t) = 0.5 L i^2$. Max energy occurs when $i(t)$ is max. $\frac{di}{dt} = 0$ at $t = \frac{\ln(4)}{600} \approx 2.31 \text{ ms}$.

(g) At $t = \frac{\ln(4)}{600}$, $e^{-200t} = e^{-\ln(4)/3} = 4^{-1/3}$. $e^{-800t} = 4^{-4/3}$.
$$ i_{\text{max}} = 10(4^{-1/3} - 4^{-4/3}) = 10(4^{-1/3})(1 - \frac{1}{4}) = 10(4^{-1/3})(0.75) $$
$$ 4^{-1/3} \approx 0.63 $$
$$ i_{\text{max}} \approx 10 \cdot 0.63 \cdot 0.75 \approx 4.72 \text{ A} $$
$$ w_{\text{max}} = 0.5 \cdot 2 \times 10^{-3} \cdot (4.72)^2 \approx 0.001 \cdot 22.28 \approx 22.28 \text{ mJ} $$
Answer: (a) $12\text{ V}$; (b) $2.31\text{ ms}$; (c) $p(t) = 20(-2e^{-400t} + 5e^{-600t} - 2e^{-1000t})\text{ W}$; (d) $3.47\text{ ms}$; (e) $1.25\text{ W}$; (f) $2.31\text{ ms}$; (g) $22.28\text{ mJ}$
P02 · Circuit Problem

An inductor with L = 100 mH is connected to a voltage source v(t) = 100 sin(500t) V for t >= 0. The initial current is i(0) = -2 A. (a) Find the expression for the current i(t) for t >= 0. (b) Determine the subintervals within the first cycle (0 <= t <= 4 ms) where the inductor absorbs power and where it delivers power.

(a) The relationship is $v(t) = L \frac{di}{dt}$. Thus $\frac{di}{dt} = \frac{v(t)}{L} = \frac{100 \sin(500t)}{0.1} = 1000 \sin(500t) \text{ A/s}$. Integrating:
$$ i(t) = \int 1000 \sin(500t) \, dt = -2 \cos(500t) + K $$
Using $i(0) = -2$:
$$ -2 = -2 \cos(0) + K \implies -2 = -2 + K \implies K = 0 $$
So $i(t) = -2 \cos(500t) \text{ A}$.

(b) Power $p(t) = v(t)i(t) = 100 \sin(500t) \cdot (-2 \cos(500t)) = -200 \sin(500t)\cos(500t) = -100 \sin(1000t) \text{ W}$. The inductor absorbs power when $p(t) > 0$, i.e., $-100 \sin(1000t) > 0 \implies \sin(1000t) < 0$. The inductor delivers power when $p(t) < 0$, i.e., $\sin(1000t) > 0$. The period of $\sin(1000t)$ is $T = \frac{2\pi}{1000} = 2 \text{ ms}$. One cycle of the original signal is $4 \text{ ms}$. In the interval $0 \le t < 1 \text{ ms}$, $1000t$ is in $[0, \pi)$, so $\sin(1000t) > 0$, thus $p(t) < 0$ (delivers power). In the interval $1 \text{ ms} \le t < 2 \text{ ms}$, $1000t$ is in $[\pi, 2\pi)$, so $\sin(1000t) < 0$, thus $p(t) > 0$ (absorbs power). This pattern repeats for $2 \text{ ms} \le t < 3 \text{ ms}$ (delivers) and $3 \text{ ms} \le t < 4 \text{ ms}$ (absorbs).
Answer: (a) $i(t) = -2 \cos(500t) \text{ A}$; (b) Delivers power: $0$-$1 \text{ ms}$, $2$-$3 \text{ ms}$; Absorbs power: $1$-$2 \text{ ms}$, $3$-$4 \text{ ms}$
P03 · Circuit Problem

A capacitor with C = 1 \mu F has a voltage v(t) = 0 for t < 0 and v(t) = 20 e^{-5000t} \sin(10000t) V for t >= 0. (a) Find the initial capacitor current i(0+) in the direction of the voltage drop. (b) Calculate the power delivered to the capacitor at t = \pi/40 ms. (c) Calculate the energy stored in the capacitor at t = \pi/40 ms.

(a) The current is given by $i(t) = C \frac{dv}{dt}$. The voltage is $v(t) = 20 e^{-5000t} \sin(10000t)$. Differentiating with respect to time yields:
$$ \frac{dv}{dt} = 20 \left[ -5000 e^{-5000t} \sin(10000t) + 10000 e^{-5000t} \cos(10000t) \right] $$
$$ \frac{dv}{dt} = 20 e^{-5000t} \left[ -5000 \sin(10000t) + 10000 \cos(10000t) \right] $$
At $t = 0$:
$$ \frac{dv}{dt} = 20 [ 0 + 10000 ] = 200,000 \text{ V/s} $$
The initial current is:
$$ i(0^+) = 1 \times 10^{-6} \cdot 200,000 = 0.2 \text{ A} = 200 \text{ mA} $$

(b) The time is $t = \frac{\pi}{40} \text{ ms} = \frac{\pi}{40000} \text{ s}$.
The arguments of the trigonometric functions are:
$$ 10000t = 10000 \left( \frac{\pi}{40000} \right) = \frac{\pi}{4} $$
$$ 5000t = \frac{\pi}{8} $$
The voltage at this time is:
$$ v(t) = 20 e^{-\pi/8} \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right) = 20 e^{-\pi/8} \left( \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right) = 10\sqrt{2} e^{-\pi/8} \text{ V} $$
The current at this time is:
$$ i(t) = 1 \times 10^{-6} \cdot 20 e^{-\pi/8} \left[ -5000 \left( \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right) + 10000 \left( \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right) \right] $$
$$ i(t) = 20 \times 10^{-6} e^{-\pi/8} \left[ \frac{5000\sqrt{2}}{2} \right] = 20 \times 10^{-6} e^{-\pi/8} \cdot 2500\sqrt{2} $$
$$ i(t) = 0.05\sqrt{2} e^{-\pi/8} \text{ A} $$
The instantaneous power is:
$$ p(t) = v(t)i(t) = \left( 10\sqrt{2} e^{-\pi/8} \right) \left( 0.05\sqrt{2} e^{-\pi/8} \right) $$
$$ p(t) = 10 \cdot 0.05 \cdot 2 \cdot e^{-\pi/4} = 1 \cdot e^{-\pi/4} \text{ W} $$
Since $e^{-\pi/4} \approx e^{-0.785} \approx 0.456$:
$$ p \approx 0.456 \text{ W} $$

(c) The energy stored in the capacitor is $w(t) = 0.5 C v^2$.
Using $v(t) = 10\sqrt{2} e^{-\pi/8}$:
$$ v^2 = 200 e^{-\pi/4} $$
The energy is:
$$ w = 0.5 \cdot 1 \times 10^{-6} \cdot 200 e^{-\pi/4} = 100 \times 10^{-6} e^{-\pi/4} $$
$$ w = 100 e^{-\pi/4} \text{ }\mu\text{J} \approx 45.6 \text{ }\mu\text{J} $$
Answer: (a) $200\ \text{mA}$; (b) $0.456\ \text{W}$; (c) $45.6\ \mu\text{J}$
P04 · Circuit Problem

A capacitor with C = 2 \mu F has zero current and voltage for t < 0. For t >= 0, the current is i(t) = 4 \cos(20000t) A. (a) Find the voltage drop v(t) in the direction of the current. (b) Determine the maximum instantaneous power delivered to the capacitor. (c) Determine the maximum stored energy.

(a) The voltage is given by $v(t) = \frac{1}{C} \int i(t) \, dt$. Substituting the given values:
$$ v(t) = \frac{1}{2 \times 10^{-6}} \int 4 \cos(20000t) \, dt $$
$$ v(t) = 500,000 \left[ \frac{4}{20000} \sin(20000t) \right] + K $$
$$ v(t) = 500,000 \left[ 0.0002 \sin(20000t) \right] + K $$
$$ v(t) = 100 \sin(20000t) + K $$
Since $v(0) = 0$, $K = 0$. Therefore:
$$ v(t) = 100 \sin(20000t) \, \text{V} $$

(b) The instantaneous power is given by $p(t) = v(t)i(t)$.
$$ p(t) = 100 \sin(20000t) \cdot 4 \cos(20000t) $$
$$ p(t) = 400 \sin(20000t) \cos(20000t) $$
Using the trigonometric identity $\sin(2\theta) = 2 \sin(\theta) \cos(\theta)$:
$$ p(t) = 200 \sin(40000t) \, \text{W} $$
The maximum value of $\sin(40000t)$ is $1$. Thus:
$$ p_{\text{max}} = 200 \, \text{W} $$

(c) The energy stored in the capacitor is given by $w(t) = 0.5 C v^2$.
$$ w(t) = 0.5 \cdot 2 \times 10^{-6} \cdot (100 \sin(20000t))^2 $$
$$ w(t) = 1 \times 10^{-6} \cdot 10000 \sin^2(20000t) $$
$$ w(t) = 0.01 \sin^2(20000t) \, \text{J} $$
The maximum value of $\sin^2(20000t)$ is $1$. Therefore:
$$ w_{\text{max}} = 0.01 \, \text{J} = 10 \, \text{mJ} $$
Answer: (a) $v(t) = 100 \sin(20000t) \text{ V}$; (b) $200 \text{ W}$; (c) $10 \text{ mJ}$
P05 · Circuit Problem

A capacitor with C = 100 nF has an initial voltage v(0) = -40 V. For t >= 0, the current is i(t) = 50 e^{-10000t} mA. (a) Find the expression for the voltage v(t). (b) Calculate the energy stored in the capacitor at t = 100 \mu s. (c) Calculate the energy stored in the capacitor as t approaches infinity.

(a) The voltage is given by $v(t) = v(0) + \frac{1}{C} \int_0^t i(\tau) \, d\tau$. The current is $i(t) = 50 \times 10^{-3} e^{-10000t} \, \text{A}$. Evaluating the integral:
$$ \int_0^t 50 \times 10^{-3} e^{-10000\tau} \, d\tau = 50 \times 10^{-3} \left[ \frac{e^{-10000\tau}}{-10000} \right]_0^t $$
$$ = 50 \times 10^{-3} \left[ \frac{1}{10000} (1 - e^{-10000t}) \right] = 5 \times 10^{-6} (1 - e^{-10000t}) \, \text{C} $$
The reciprocal of the capacitance is:
$$ \frac{1}{C} = \frac{1}{100 \times 10^{-9}} = 10^7 \, \text{F}^{-1} $$
The change in voltage is:
$$ \Delta v = 10^7 \cdot 5 \times 10^{-6} (1 - e^{-10000t}) = 50 (1 - e^{-10000t}) \, \text{V} $$
Thus, the voltage expression is:
$$ v(t) = -40 + 50(1 - e^{-10000t}) = 10 - 50 e^{-10000t} \, \text{V} $$

(b) At $t = 100 \, \mu\text{s} = 100 \times 10^{-6} \, \text{s}$:
$$ 10000t = 1 $$
$$ e^{-1} \approx 0.3679 $$
The voltage at this time is:
$$ v(100 \, \mu\text{s}) = 10 - 50(0.3679) = 10 - 18.395 = -8.395 \, \text{V} $$
The energy stored is:
$$ w = 0.5 C v^2 = 0.5 \cdot 100 \times 10^{-9} \cdot (-8.395)^2 = 50 \times 10^{-9} \cdot 70.48 = 3524 \times 10^{-9} \, \text{J} = 3.52 \, \mu\text{J} $$

(c) As $t \to \infty$, $e^{-10000t} \to 0$. Therefore:
$$ v(\infty) = 10 \, \text{V} $$
The energy at infinity is:
$$ w(\infty) = 0.5 \cdot 100 \times 10^{-9} \cdot 10^2 = 50 \times 10^{-9} \cdot 100 = 5000 \times 10^{-9} \, \text{J} = 5 \, \mu\text{J} $$
Answer: (a) $v(t) = 10 - 50 e^{-10000t} \text{ V}$; (b) $3.52 \ \mu\text{J}$; (c) $5 \ \mu\text{J}$
P06 · Circuit Problem

A capacitor with C = 50 nF has an initial voltage of 20 V drop in the reference direction of the current. A rectangular current pulse is applied: i = 40 mA for 0 < t < 50 \mu s; i = -20 mA for 50 \mu s < t < 150 \mu s; i = 0 for t > 150 \mu s. Find the expression for v(t) in each of the three intervals.

Interval 1: $0 < t < 50 \, \mu\text{s}$.

$i = 40 \times 10^{-3} \, \text{A}$.

$$ \frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{i}{C} = \frac{40 \times 10^{-3}}{50 \times 10^{-9}} = 800,000 \, \text{V/s} $$

$$ v(t) = v(0) + \int_0^t 800,000 \, d\tau = 20 + 800,000 t $$

At $t = 50 \, \mu\text{s} = 50 \times 10^{-6} \, \text{s}$:

$$ v = 20 + 800,000(50 \times 10^{-6}) = 20 + 40 = 60 \, \text{V} $$

Interval 2: $50 \, \mu\text{s} < t < 150 \, \mu\text{s}$.

$i = -20 \times 10^{-3} \, \text{A}$.

$$ \frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{-20 \times 10^{-3}}{50 \times 10^{-9}} = -400,000 \, \text{V/s} $$

$$ v(t) = v(50 \, \mu\text{s}) + \int_{50 \, \mu\text{s}}^t -400,000 \, d\tau = 60 - 400,000(t - 50 \times 10^{-6}) $$

At $t = 150 \, \mu\text{s}$:

$$ v = 60 - 400,000(100 \times 10^{-6}) = 60 - 40 = 20 \, \text{V} $$

Interval 3: $t > 150 \, \mu\text{s}$.

$i = 0$.

$$ \frac{dv}{dt} = 0 $$

$$ v(t) = v(150 \, \mu\text{s}) = 20 \, \text{V} $$
Answer: $v(t) = 20 + 800{,}000t \text{ V}$ ($0 \le t \le 50 \ \mu\text{s}$); $v(t) = 60 - 400{,}000(t - 50 \ \mu\text{s}) \text{ V}$ ($50 \ \mu\text{s} \le t \le 150 \ \mu\text{s}$); $v(t) = 20 \text{ V}$ ($t > 150 \ \mu\text{s}$)
P07 · Circuit Problem

A capacitor with C = 1 \mu F is subjected to a voltage pulse defined as: v(t) = 20 V for 0 <= t <= 1 s; v(t) = 20(2-t) V for 1 < t <= 2 s; v(t) = 0 elsewhere. Derive the expression for the capacitor current i(t) during the interval 0 <= t <= 2 s, noting any impulses at the boundaries.

$i(t) = C \frac{dv}{dt}$. For $0 < t < 1 \text{ s}$, $v(t) = 20 \text{ V}$, so $\frac{dv}{dt} = 0$, thus $i(t) = 0$. At $t = 0$, $v$ jumps from $0$ to $20 \text{ V}$. This is a step change $\Delta v = 20 \text{ V}$. The current is an impulse $i(t) = C \Delta v \delta(t) = 1 \times 10^{-6} \cdot 20 \delta(t) = 20 \ \mu\text{C} \ \delta(t)$. For $1 < t < 2 \text{ s}$, $v(t) = 20(2-t) = 40 - 20t$. $\frac{dv}{dt} = -20 \text{ V/s}$. $i(t) = 1 \times 10^{-6} \cdot (-20) = -20 \ \mu\text{A}$. At $t = 1 \text{ s}$, $v$ is continuous ($20 \text{ V}$ from left, $20(2-1)=20 \text{ V}$ from right), so no impulse. At $t = 2 \text{ s}$, $v$ jumps from $0$ to $0$ (since $v(2)=0$ and $v(t>2)=0$), so no impulse. Thus, $i(t)$ consists of an impulse of $20 \ \mu\text{C}$ at $t=0$, $0$ for $0<t<1$, $-20 \ \mu\text{A}$ for $1<t<2$, and $0$ for $t>2$.
Answer: $i(t) = 20\,\mu\text{C}\,\delta(t) + 0\,(0<t<1) - 20\,\mu\text{A}\,(1<t<2) + 0\,(t>2)$
P08 · Circuit Problem

Two inductors, L1 = 5 H and L2 = 15 H, are connected in parallel. At t=0, they are connected to a load (black box). The voltage across the parallel combination is v(t) = 900 e^{-10t} V for t > 0. The initial currents are i1(0) = 2 A and i2(0) = -8 A. (a) Find the equivalent inductance and the total current i(t) entering the load. (b) Find the individual currents i1(t) and i2(t). (c) Calculate the total energy delivered to the load from t=0 to t=\infty. (d) Calculate the initial stored energy in the inductors. (e) Calculate the energy trapped in the inductors at t=\infty.

(a) The equivalent inductance $L_{eq}$ is calculated as:
$$ L_{eq} = \frac{L_1 L_2}{L_1 + L_2} = \frac{5 \cdot 15}{5 + 15} = \frac{75}{20} = 3.75 \text{ H} $$
The total current $i(t)$ is the sum of the branch currents. The relationship between voltage and current is given by $v(t) = L_{eq} \frac{di}{dt}$. Solving for the derivative:
$$ \frac{di}{dt} = \frac{v(t)}{L_{eq}} = \frac{900 e^{-10t}}{3.75} = 240 e^{-10t} \text{ A/s} $$
Integrating to find $i(t)$:
$$ i(t) = \int 240 e^{-10t} \, dt = -24 e^{-10t} + K $$
The initial total current is the sum of the initial branch currents:
$$ i(0) = i_1(0) + i_2(0) = 2 - 8 = -6 \text{ A} $$
Substituting $t=0$ into the expression for $i(t)$:
$$ -6 = -24(1) + K \implies K = 18 $$
Thus, the total current is:
$$ i(t) = 18 - 24 e^{-10t} \text{ A} $$

(b) For inductor $L_1$:
$$ v(t) = L_1 \frac{di_1}{dt} \implies \frac{di_1}{dt} = \frac{900 e^{-10t}}{5} = 180 e^{-10t} $$
Integrating:
$$ i_1(t) = \int 180 e^{-10t} \, dt = -18 e^{-10t} + K_1 $$
Using the initial condition $i_1(0) = 2 \text{ A}$:
$$ 2 = -18 + K_1 \implies K_1 = 20 $$
Thus:
$$ i_1(t) = 20 - 18 e^{-10t} \text{ A} $$

For inductor $L_2$:
$$ v(t) = L_2 \frac{di_2}{dt} \implies \frac{di_2}{dt} = \frac{900 e^{-10t}}{15} = 60 e^{-10t} $$
Integrating:
$$ i_2(t) = \int 60 e^{-10t} \, dt = -6 e^{-10t} + K_2 $$
Using the initial condition $i_2(0) = -8 \text{ A}$:
$$ -8 = -6 + K_2 \implies K_2 = -2 $$
Thus:
$$ i_2(t) = -2 - 6 e^{-10t} \text{ A} $$

Verification:
$$ i_1(t) + i_2(t) = (20 - 18 e^{-10t}) + (-2 - 6 e^{-10t}) = 18 - 24 e^{-10t} \text{ A} $$
This matches the total current $i(t)$ derived in part (a).

(c) The energy delivered to the load is determined by integrating the power over time.

(d) The initial energy stored in the inductors is:
$$ W_0 = \frac{1}{2} L_1 i_1(0)^2 + \frac{1}{2} L_2 i_2(0)^2 $$
$$ W_0 = \frac{1}{2}(5)(2^2) + \frac{1}{2}(15)(-8^2) = 10 + \frac{1}{2}(15)(64) = 10 + 480 = 490 \text{ J} $$

(e) The final energy stored in the inductors as $t \to \infty$ is:
$$ W_{\infty} = \frac{1}{2} L_1 i_1(\infty)^2 + \frac{1}{2} L_2 i_2(\infty)^2 $$
As $t \to \infty$, $e^{-10t} \to 0$, so $i_1(\infty) = 20 \text{ A}$ and $i_2(\infty) = -2 \text{ A}$.
$$ W_{\infty} = \frac{1}{2}(5)(20^2) + \frac{1}{2}(15)(-2^2) = \frac{1}{2}(5)(400) + \frac{1}{2}(15)(4) = 1000 + 30 = 1030 \text{ J} $$

The energy delivered to the load is calculated by integrating the power $p_{load} = v(t) i(t)$ from $t=0$ to $t=\infty$:
$$ W_{load} = \int_0^\infty 900 e^{-10t} (18 - 24 e^{-10t}) \, dt $$
$$ W_{load} = \int_0^\infty (16200 e^{-10t} - 21600 e^{-20t}) \, dt $$
$$ W_{load} = \left[ -1620 e^{-10t} + 1080 e^{-20t} \right]_0^\infty $$
$$ W_{load} = (0) - (-1620 + 1080) = 540 \text{ J} $$

The energy delivered to the load is $540 \text{ J}$. The energy trapped in the inductors at $t=\infty$ is $1030 \text{ J}$.
Answer: (a) $L_{eq} = 3.75 \text{ H}$, $i(t) = 18 - 24 e^{-10t} \text{ A}$; (b) $i_1(t) = 20 - 18 e^{-10t} \text{ A}$, $i_2(t) = -2 - 6 e^{-10t} \text{ A}$; (c) $540 \text{ J}$; (d) $490 \text{ J}$; (e) $1030 \text{ J}$
P09 · Circuit Problem

Two capacitors, C1 = 10 nF and C2 = 20 nF, are connected in series. The positive terminal of C1 is at the top, and the positive terminal of C2 is at the bottom, such that their voltages add algebraically around the series string. At t = 0, the initial voltages are v1(0) = 30 V and v2(0) = 10 V. For t > 0, the current flowing through the series combination is i(t) = -1000 e^{-500t} \mu A. Determine: (a) the equivalent capacitance and the total voltage v_o(t) across the series combination; (b) the voltage v1(t) across C1; (c) the voltage v2(t) across C2; (d) the energy delivered to the load connected across the series combination; (e) the initial energy stored in the capacitors; (f) the final energy trapped in the capacitors.

(a) The equivalent capacitance of two series capacitors is $C_{eq} = \frac{C_1 C_2}{C_1 + C_2} = \frac{10 \cdot 20}{10 + 20} = \frac{200}{30} = \frac{20}{3} \text{ nF}$. The total initial voltage is $v_o(0) = v_1(0) + v_2(0) = 30 + 10 = 40 \text{ V}$. The relationship between current and voltage for a capacitor is $i(t) = C_{eq} \frac{dv_o}{dt}$. Thus, $\frac{dv_o}{dt} = \frac{i(t)}{C_{eq}} = \frac{-1000 \times 10^{-6} e^{-500t}}{\frac{20}{3} \times 10^{-9}} = \frac{-10^{-3}}{\frac{20}{3} \times 10^{-9}} e^{-500t} = -\frac{3}{20} \times 10^6 e^{-500t} = -150,000 e^{-500t} \text{ V/s}$. Integrating from 0 to $t$:
$$v_o(t) = v_o(0) + \int_0^t -150,000 e^{-500\tau} d\tau = 40 - 150,000 \left[ \frac{e^{-500\tau}}{-500} \right]_0^t = 40 + 300 (e^{-500t} - 1) = 300 e^{-500t} - 260 \text{ V}$$

(b) For $C_1$, $i(t) = C_1 \frac{dv_1}{dt}$.
$$\frac{dv_1}{dt} = \frac{i(t)}{C_1} = \frac{-1000 \times 10^{-6}}{10 \times 10^{-9}} e^{-500t} = -100,000 e^{-500t} \text{ V/s}$$
Integrating:
$$v_1(t) = v_1(0) + \int_0^t -100,000 e^{-500\tau} d\tau = 30 - 100,000 \left[ \frac{e^{-500\tau}}{-500} \right]_0^t = 30 + 200 (e^{-500t} - 1) = 200 e^{-500t} - 170 \text{ V}$$

(c) For $C_2$, $i(t) = C_2 \frac{dv_2}{dt}$.
$$\frac{dv_2}{dt} = \frac{i(t)}{C_2} = \frac{-1000 \times 10^{-6}}{20 \times 10^{-9}} e^{-500t} = -50,000 e^{-500t} \text{ V/s}$$
Integrating:
$$v_2(t) = v_2(0) + \int_0^t -50,000 e^{-500\tau} d\tau = 10 - 50,000 \left[ \frac{e^{-500\tau}}{-500} \right]_0^t = 10 + 100 (e^{-500t} - 1) = 100 e^{-500t} - 90 \text{ V}$$
Verification: $v_1(t) + v_2(t) = (200 e^{-500t} - 170) + (100 e^{-500t} - 90) = 300 e^{-500t} - 260 = v_o(t)$.

(d) The energy delivered to the load is determined by the power absorbed by the external circuit. The instantaneous power delivered to the load is $p(t) = v_o(t) i(t)$.

(e) The initial energy stored in the capacitors is:
$$W_{initial} = \frac{1}{2} C_1 v_1(0)^2 + \frac{1}{2} C_2 v_2(0)^2 = \frac{1}{2}(10 \times 10^{-9})(30)^2 + \frac{1}{2}(20 \times 10^{-9})(10)^2 = 5 \times 10^{-9}(900) + 10 \times 10^{-9}(100) = 4500 \times 10^{-9} + 1000 \times 10^{-9} = 5500 \text{ nJ} = 5.5 \mu \text{J}$$

(f) The final energy stored in the capacitors as $t \to \infty$ is calculated using $v_1(\infty) = -170 \text{ V}$ and $v_2(\infty) = -90 \text{ V}$:
$$W_{final} = \frac{1}{2} C_1 v_1(\infty)^2 + \frac{1}{2} C_2 v_2(\infty)^2 = \frac{1}{2}(10 \times 10^{-9})(-170)^2 + \frac{1}{2}(20 \times 10^{-9})(-90)^2 = 5 \times 10^{-9}(28900) + 10 \times 10^{-9}(8100) = 144500 \times 10^{-9} + 81000 \times 10^{-9} = 225500 \times 10^{-9} \text{ J} = 225.5 \mu \text{J}$$

The energy transferred between the capacitor network and the external circuit is the integral of the power $p(t) = v_o(t) i(t)$ over time:
$$W_{load} = \int_0^\infty v_o(t) i(t) dt = \int_0^\infty (300 e^{-500t} - 260)(-1000 e^{-500t}) \mu dt$$
$$W_{load} = \int_0^\infty (-300,000 e^{-1000t} + 260,000 e^{-500t}) \mu dt$$
$$W_{load} = -300,000 \left[ \frac{e^{-1000t}}{-1000} \right]_0^\infty + 260,000 \left[ \frac{e^{-500t}}{-500} \right]_0^\infty$$
$$W_{load} = -300,000 (0 - (-1/1000)) + 260,000 (0 - (-1/500)) = -300 + 520 = 220 \mu \text{J}$$
The positive result indicates that $220 \mu \text{J}$ of energy is delivered to the load. This is consistent with the change in stored energy in the capacitors, which increases by $225.5 \mu \text{J} - 5.5 \mu \text{J} = 220 \mu \text{J}$, implying the capacitors absorb this energy from the external circuit.
Answer: (a) $C_{eq} = 6.67 \text{ nF}$, $v_o(t) = 300 e^{-500t} - 260 \text{ V}$; (b) $v_1(t) = 200 e^{-500t} - 170 \text{ V}$; (c) $v_2(t) = 100 e^{-500t} - 90 \text{ V}$; (d) $220 \text{ }\mu\text{J}$ (absorbed by capacitors); (e) $5.5 \text{ }\mu\text{J}$; (f) $225.5 \text{ }\mu\text{J}$
P10 · Circuit Problem

A series RLC circuit consists of a 240 V dc source, an inductor L = 10 mH, and a capacitor C = 250 nF. The switch has been open for a long time, so the initial current is zero and the initial capacitor voltage is zero. At t = 0, the switch closes. The current in the circuit for t >= 0+ is given by $i_o(t) = 40 e^{-4000t} (\cos 3000t + 2 \sin 3000t) \text{ mA}$. Determine the voltage across the inductor $v_1(0+)$ and the voltage across the capacitor $v_2(0+)$ immediately after the switch closes.

The current is given by $i_o(t) = 40 e^{-4000t} (\cos 3000t + 2 \sin 3000t) \text{ mA}$ for $t \ge 0+$. The voltage across the inductor is defined as $v_1(t) = L \frac{di_o}{dt}$.

First, the derivative of the current with respect to time is calculated. Expressing the current in amperes:
$$ i_o(t) = 40 \times 10^{-3} e^{-4000t} (\cos 3000t + 2 \sin 3000t) $$

Applying the product rule:
$$ \frac{di_o}{dt} = 40 \times 10^{-3} \left[ -4000 e^{-4000t} (\cos 3000t + 2 \sin 3000t) + e^{-4000t} (-3000 \sin 3000t + 6000 \cos 3000t) \right] $$

At $t = 0+$, the trigonometric and exponential terms evaluate to $e^0 = 1$, $\cos 0 = 1$, and $\sin 0 = 0$. Substituting these values:
$$ \frac{di_o}{dt}\bigg|_{0+} = 40 \times 10^{-3} \left[ -4000 (1) (1 + 0) + 1 (-0 + 6000 (1)) \right] $$
$$ \frac{di_o}{dt}\bigg|_{0+} = 40 \times 10^{-3} [ -4000 + 6000 ] $$
$$ \frac{di_o}{dt}\bigg|_{0+} = 40 \times 10^{-3} (2000) = 80 \text{ A/s} $$

The inductor voltage at $t = 0+$ is:
$$ v_1(0+) = L \frac{di_o}{dt}\bigg|_{0+} = 10 \times 10^{-3} \text{ H} \times 80 \text{ A/s} = 0.8 \text{ V} $$

For the capacitor voltage $v_2(0+)$, the initial condition is determined by the continuity of capacitor voltage. Since the switch was open for a long time prior to $t=0$, the initial capacitor voltage is $v_2(0-) = 0$. Therefore:
$$ v_2(0+) = v_2(0-) = 0 \text{ V} $$

Final Answers:
$$ v_1(0+) = 0.8 \text{ V} $$
$$ v_2(0+) = 0 \text{ V} $$
Answer: $v_1(0^+) = 0.8\ \text{V}$; $v_2(0^+) = 0\ \text{V}$
P11 · Circuit Problem

A capacitor with capacitance C = 200 \mu F is connected to a voltage source. The voltage across the capacitor is a triangular pulse defined as follows: $v(t) = 0$ for $t < 0$; $v(t) = 2t$ V for $0 \le t < 5$ s; $v(t) = 10$ V for $5 \le t < 15$ s; $v(t) = -2t + 40$ V for $15 \le t < 20$ s; $v(t) = 0$ for $t \ge 20$ s. Determine the expressions for the current $i(t)$, power $p(t)$, and energy $w(t)$ stored in the capacitor for all time intervals.

The current through a capacitor is given by $i(t) = C \frac{dv}{dt}$. The power is $p(t) = v(t) i(t)$. The energy stored is $w(t) = \frac{1}{2} C v(t)^2$. The capacitance is $C = 200 \ \mu\text{F} = 2 \times 10^{-4} \ \text{F}$.

Interval 1: $0 \le t < 5 \ \text{s}$.
The voltage is $v(t) = 2t$.
The derivative is $\frac{dv}{dt} = 2 \ \text{V/s}$.
The current is $i(t) = 200 \times 10^{-6} \times 2 = 400 \ \mu\text{A} = 0.4 \ \text{mA}$.
The power is $p(t) = (2t)(0.4 \times 10^{-3}) = 0.8t \ \text{mW}$.
The energy is $w(t) = \frac{1}{2} (200 \times 10^{-6}) (2t)^2 = 100 \times 10^{-6} (4t^2) = 400 t^2 \ \mu\text{J} = 4 \times 10^{-4} t^2 \ \text{J}$.

Interval 2: $5 \le t < 15 \ \text{s}$.
The voltage is $v(t) = 10$.
The derivative is $\frac{dv}{dt} = 0$.
The current is $i(t) = 0$.
The power is $p(t) = 0$.
The energy is $w(t) = \frac{1}{2} (200 \times 10^{-6}) (10)^2 = 100 \times 10^{-6} (100) = 10,000 \ \mu\text{J} = 10 \ \text{mJ}$.

Interval 3: $15 \le t < 20 \ \text{s}$.
The voltage is $v(t) = -2t + 40$.
The derivative is $\frac{dv}{dt} = -2 \ \text{V/s}$.
The current is $i(t) = 200 \times 10^{-6} \times (-2) = -400 \ \mu\text{A} = -0.4 \ \text{mA}$.
The power is $p(t) = (-2t + 40)(-0.4 \times 10^{-3}) = (0.8t - 16) \times 10^{-3} \ \text{W} = (0.8t - 16) \ \text{mW}$.
The energy is $w(t) = \frac{1}{2} (200 \times 10^{-6}) (-2t + 40)^2 = 100 \times 10^{-6} (4t^2 - 160t + 1600) = (400t^2 - 16000t + 160000) \ \mu\text{J}$.

Interval 4: $t \ge 20 \ \text{s}$.
The voltage is $v(t) = 0$.
The current is $i(t) = 0$.
The power is $p(t) = 0$.
The energy is $w(t) = 0$.

For $t < 0$, all quantities are $0$.
Answer: $i(t)$: $0.4\ \text{mA}$ ($0 \le t < 5\ \text{s}$), $0$ ($5\ \text{s} \le t < 15\ \text{s}$), $-0.4\ \text{mA}$ ($15\ \text{s} \le t < 20\ \text{s}$), $0$ ($t > 20\ \text{s}$).
$p(t)$: $0.8t\ \text{mW}$ ($0 \le t < 5\ \text{s}$), $0$ ($5\ \text{s} \le t < 15\ \text{s}$), $(0.8t - 16)\ \text{mW}$ ($15\ \text{s} \le t < 20\ \text{s}$), $0$ ($t > 20\ \text{s}$).
$w(t)$: $4 \times 10^{-4} t^2\ \text{J}$ ($0 \le t < 5\ \text{s}$), $10\ \text{mJ}$ ($5\ \text{s} \le t < 15\ \text{s}$), $1 \times 10^{-4} (-2t + 40)^2\ \text{J}$ ($15\ \text{s} \le t < 20\ \text{s}$), $0$ ($t > 20\ \text{s}$).
P12 · Circuit Problem

A capacitor with capacitance $C = 100$ nF has an initial voltage of $0$ V. The current flowing into the positive terminal of the capacitor is a piecewise linear waveform defined as: $i(t) = 0$ for $t < 10$ ms; $i(t) = 2.5t - 25$ mA for $10 \le t < 30$ ms; $i(t) = -2.5t + 125$ mA for $30 \le t < 50$ ms; and $i(t) = 0$ for $t \ge 50$ ms. Determine: (a) the charge on the capacitor at $t = 30$ ms; (b) the voltage across the capacitor at $t = 50$ ms; and (c) the total energy stored in the capacitor at $t = 50$ ms.

The current waveform is a triangle. From 10 ms to 30 ms, it rises from 0 to 50 mA. The slope is $\frac{50 - 0}{30 - 10} = \frac{50}{20} = 2.5$ mA/ms. Equation: $i(t) = 2.5(t - 10)$ mA for $10 \le t < 30$. From 30 ms to 50 ms, it falls from 50 mA to 0 mA. The slope is $\frac{0 - 50}{50 - 30} = \frac{-50}{20} = -2.5$ mA/ms. Equation: $i(t) = -2.5(t - 50) = 2.5(50 - t)$ mA for $30 \le t < 50$. (a) Charge at $t = 30$ ms. $q(30) = q(0) + \int_0^{30} i(t) dt$. $q(0) = C v(0) = 0$. The integral is the area under the current curve from 0 to 30 ms. The area is a triangle with base 20 ms and height 50 mA. Area $= \frac{1}{2} \times 20 \times 10^{-3} \text{ s} \times 50 \times 10^{-3} \text{ A} = 500 \times 10^{-6} \text{ C} = 500 \mu C$. So $q(30) = 500 \mu C$. (b) Voltage at $t = 50$ ms. $v(50) = \frac{q(50)}{C}$. $q(50) = q(30) + \int_{30}^{50} i(t) dt$. The integral from 30 to 50 is the area of the second triangle, base 20 ms, height 50 mA. Area $= \frac{1}{2} \times 20 \times 10^{-3} \times 50 \times 10^{-3} = 500 \mu C$. Total charge $q(50) = 500 \mu C + 500 \mu C = 1000 \mu C = 1$ mC. $v(50) = \frac{1 \times 10^{-3} \text{ C}}{100 \times 10^{-9} \text{ F}} = \frac{10^{-3}}{10^{-7}} = 10,000 \text{ V} = 10$ kV. (c) Energy stored at $t = 50$ ms. $w(50) = \frac{1}{2} C v(50)^2 = \frac{1}{2} (100 \times 10^{-9}) (10,000)^2 = 50 \times 10^{-9} \times 10^8 = 50 \times 10^{-1} = 5$ J.
Answer: (a) $500\ \mu\text{C}$; (b) $10\ \text{kV}$; (c) $5\ \text{J}$
