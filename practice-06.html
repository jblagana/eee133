P01 · Circuit Problem

An undriven LC circuit has $L = 10 \text{ mH}$ and $C = 10 \text{ \mu F}$. Calculate the natural frequency $\omega_0$ in rad/s.

The natural frequency is given by $\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}$.
Substituting the values: $L = 10 \times 10^{-3} \text{ H}$, $C = 10 \times 10^{-6} \text{ F}$.
$LC = 10^{-3} \times 10^{-5} = 10^{-8} \text{ s}^2$.
$\sqrt{LC} = \sqrt{10^{-8}} = 10^{-4} \text{ s}$.
$\omega_0 = \frac{1}{10^{-4}} = 10,000 \text{ rad/s}$.
Answer: 10,000 rad/s
P02 · Circuit Problem

A series RLC circuit has $R = 10 \, \Omega$, $L = 1 \text{ H}$, and $C = 0.01 \text{ F}$. Determine the damping factor $\alpha$ and the natural frequency $\omega_0$.

For a series RLC circuit:
$\alpha = \frac{R}{2L} = \frac{10}{2(1)} = 5 \text{ Np/s}$.
$\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 \times 0.01}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{0.01}} = \frac{1}{0.1} = 10 \text{ rad/s}$.
Answer: α = 5 Np/s, ω₀ = 10 rad/s
P03 · Circuit Problem

A parallel RLC circuit has $R = 50 \, \Omega$, $L = 2 \text{ H}$, and $C = 0.5 \text{ F}$. Calculate $\alpha$ and $\omega_0$.

For a parallel RLC circuit:
$\alpha = \frac{1}{2RC} = \frac{1}{2(50)(0.5)} = \frac{1}{50} = 0.02 \text{ Np/s}$.
$\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2 \times 0.5}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1}} = 1 \text{ rad/s}$.
Answer: α = 0.02 Np/s, ω₀ = 1 rad/s
P04 · Circuit Problem

Classify the response of the series RLC circuit in P02 ($\alpha = 5$, $\omega_0 = 10$).

$\alpha = 5$, $\omega_0 = 10$.
Since $\alpha < \omega_0$ ($5 < 10$), the circuit is underdamped.
Answer: Underdamped

P06 · Circuit Problem

A series RLC circuit has $L = 1 \text{ H}$ and $C = 0.25 \text{ F}$. Find the value of R required for critical damping.

For critical damping, $\alpha = \omega_0$.
$\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 \times 0.25}} = \frac{1}{0.5} = 2 \text{ rad/s}$.
$\alpha = \frac{R}{2L} = \frac{R}{2(1)} = \frac{R}{2}$.
Set $\alpha = \omega_0$: $\frac{R}{2} = 2 \implies R = 4 \, \Omega$.
Answer: 4 Ω
P07 · Circuit Problem

A parallel RLC circuit has $L = 4 \text{ H}$ and $C = 1 \text{ F}$. Find the value of R required for critical damping.

For critical damping, $\alpha = \omega_0$.
$\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4 \times 1}} = \frac{1}{2} = 0.5 \text{ rad/s}$.
$\alpha = \frac{1}{2RC} = \frac{1}{2(R)(1)} = \frac{1}{2R}$.
Set $\alpha = \omega_0$: $\frac{1}{2R} = 0.5 \implies 2R = 2 \implies R = 1 \, \Omega$.
Answer: 1 Ω
P08 · Circuit Problem

For the underdamped series RLC circuit in P02 ($\alpha = 5$, $\omega_0 = 10$), calculate the damped natural frequency $\omega_d$.

The damped natural frequency is $\omega_d = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \alpha^2}$.
$\omega_0 = 10$, $\alpha = 5$.
$\omega_d = \sqrt{10^2 - 5^2} = \sqrt{100 - 25} = \sqrt{75} = 5\sqrt{3} \approx 8.66 \text{ rad/s}$.
Answer: 8.66 rad/s
P09 · Circuit Problem

An undriven series RLC circuit has $v_C(0) = 10 \text{ V}$ and $i_L(0) = 0 \text{ A}$. Given $\alpha = 0$ and $\omega_0 = 100 \text{ rad/s}$, find the expression for $v_C(t)$ for $t \ge 0$.

Since $\alpha = 0$, the circuit is undamped (pure LC). The response is $v_C(t) = K_1 \cos \omega_0 t + K_2 \sin \omega_0 t$.
1. Apply $v_C(0) = 10$:
$v_C(0) = K_1 \cos(0) + K_2 \sin(0) = K_1 = 10$.
2. Apply $i_L(0) = 0$:
For a series circuit, $i_L = i_C = C \frac{dv_C}{dt}$.
$\frac{dv_C}{dt} = -K_1 \omega_0 \sin \omega_0 t + K_2 \omega_0 \cos \omega_0 t$.
At $t=0$, $\frac{dv_C(0)}{dt} = K_2 \omega_0$.
Since $i_L(0) = C \frac{dv_C(0)}{dt} = 0$, we have $\frac{dv_C(0)}{dt} = 0 \implies K_2 \omega_0 = 0 \implies K_2 = 0$.
Thus, $v_C(t) = 10 \cos(100t) \text{ V}$.
Answer: v_C(t) = 10 cos(100t) V
P10 · Circuit Problem

An undriven series RLC circuit has $v_C(0) = 0 \text{ V}$ and $i_L(0) = 5 \text{ A}$. Given $\alpha = 0$ and $\omega_0 = 100 \text{ rad/s}$, find the expression for $v_C(t)$ for $t \ge 0$.

Response form: $v_C(t) = K_1 \cos \omega_0 t + K_2 \sin \omega_0 t$.
1. Apply $v_C(0) = 0$:
$v_C(0) = K_1 = 0$.
2. Apply $i_L(0) = 5$:
$\frac{dv_C(0)}{dt} = K_2 \omega_0$.
$i_L(0) = C \frac{dv_C(0)}{dt} = C (K_2 \omega_0) = 5$.
We need C. From $\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}$, we don't have L or C individually, but we know $K_2 = \frac{5}{C \omega_0}$.
Wait, the standard formula derived in the lecture for undriven LC is:
$v(t) = v_C(0^+) \cos \omega_0 t - \sqrt{\frac{L}{C}} i_L(0^+) \sin \omega_0 t$.
Here $v_C(0) = 0$, so the first term is 0.
$v_C(t) = -\sqrt{\frac{L}{C}} (5) \sin(100t)$.
We need $\sqrt{L/C}$. We know $\omega_0 = 100$. This problem is underspecified without L or C values to determine the impedance $\sqrt{L/C}$.
Let's assume standard values for a solvable problem context or re-evaluate. Usually, such problems provide L and C. Let's assume $L=1H, C=0.0001F$ (since $1/\sqrt{1*10^{-4}}=100$). Then $\sqrt{L/C} = \sqrt{1/10^{-4}} = 100 \Omega$.
$v_C(t) = -100(5) \sin(100t) = -500 \sin(100t) \text{ V}$.
Note: In a real exam, L and C would be given. Assuming $\sqrt{L/C}=100 \Omega$ based on typical textbook scaling for $\omega_0=100$.
Answer: v_C(t) = -500 sin(100t) V (assuming √(L/C)=100 Ω)
P11 · Circuit Problem

A series RLC circuit is overdamped with roots $s_1 = -1$ and $s_2 = -4$. If $i(0) = 0$ and $\frac{di(0)}{dt} = 10 \text{ A/s}$, find $i(t)$.

The general solution for overdamped response is $i(t) = A_1 e^{s_1 t} + A_2 e^{s_2 t} = A_1 e^{-t} + A_2 e^{-4t}$.
1. Apply $i(0) = 0$:
$A_1 + A_2 = 0 \implies A_2 = -A_1$.
2. Apply $\frac{di(0)}{dt} = 10$:
$\frac{di}{dt} = -A_1 e^{-t} - 4A_2 e^{-4t}$.
At $t=0$: $-A_1 - 4A_2 = 10$.
Substitute $A_2 = -A_1$:
$-A_1 - 4(-A_1) = 10 \implies -A_1 + 4A_1 = 10 \implies 3A_1 = 10 \implies A_1 = \frac{10}{3}$.
$A_2 = -\frac{10}{3}$.
Thus, $i(t) = \frac{10}{3} e^{-t} - \frac{10}{3} e^{-4t} \text{ A}$.
Answer: i(t) = (10/3)(e⁻ᵗ - e⁻⁴ᵗ) A
P12 · Circuit Problem

A parallel RLC circuit is critically damped with $\alpha = \omega_0 = 2 \text{ rad/s}$. If $v(0) = 5 \text{ V}$ and $\frac{dv(0)}{dt} = 0 \text{ V/s}$, find $v(t)$.

For critical damping, the roots are repeated: $s_1 = s_2 = -\alpha = -2$.
The general solution is $v(t) = (A_1 + A_2 t) e^{-2t}$.
1. Apply $v(0) = 5$:
$v(0) = (A_1 + 0) e^0 = A_1 = 5$.
2. Apply $\frac{dv(0)}{dt} = 0$:
$\frac{dv}{dt} = A_2 e^{-2t} + (A_1 + A_2 t)(-2)e^{-2t} = e^{-2t} [A_2 - 2A_1 - 2A_2 t]$.
At $t=0$: $\frac{dv(0)}{dt} = A_2 - 2A_1 = 0$.
Substitute $A_1 = 5$: $A_2 - 10 = 0 \implies A_2 = 10$.
Thus, $v(t) = (5 + 10t) e^{-2t} \text{ V}$.
Answer: v(t) = (5 + 10t)e⁻²ᵗ V
P13 · Circuit Problem

An underdamped series RLC circuit has $\alpha = 1 \text{ Np/s}$ and $\omega_0 = 5 \text{ rad/s}$. If $i(0) = 0$ and $\frac{di(0)}{dt} = 5 \text{ A/s}$, find $i(t)$.

First, find $\omega_d = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \alpha^2} = \sqrt{25 - 1} = \sqrt{24} = 2\sqrt{6} \approx 4.899 \text{ rad/s}$.
The general solution is $i(t) = e^{-\alpha t} (A_1 \cos \omega_d t + A_2 \sin \omega_d t) = e^{-t} (A_1 \cos(2\sqrt{6}t) + A_2 \sin(2\sqrt{6}t))$.
1. Apply $i(0) = 0$:
$i(0) = e^0 (A_1(1) + A_2(0)) = A_1 = 0$.
So, $i(t) = A_2 e^{-t} \sin(2\sqrt{6}t)$.
2. Apply $\frac{di(0)}{dt} = 5$:
$\frac{di}{dt} = A_2 [ -e^{-t} \sin(2\sqrt{6}t) + e^{-t} (2\sqrt{6}) \cos(2\sqrt{6}t) ]$.
At $t=0$: $\frac{di(0)}{dt} = A_2 [ 0 + 1(2\sqrt{6})(1) ] = 2\sqrt{6} A_2$.
Set equal to 5: $2\sqrt{6} A_2 = 5 \implies A_2 = \frac{5}{2\sqrt{6}} = \frac{5\sqrt{6}}{12} \approx 1.02$.
Thus, $i(t) = \frac{5}{2\sqrt{6}} e^{-t} \sin(2\sqrt{6}t) \text{ A}$.
Answer: i(t) = (5/(2√6))e⁻ᵗ sin(2√6 t) A
P14 · Circuit Problem

A series RLC circuit has $R = 4 \, \Omega$, $L = 1 \text{ H}$, $C = 0.04 \text{ F}$. It is initially at rest ($i(0)=0, v_C(0)=0$) but is suddenly excited by an impulse current source of $1 \text{ A}\cdot\text{s}$ at $t=0$. Find $i(t)$ for $t>0$.

First, determine the damping.
$\alpha = \frac{R}{2L} = \frac{4}{2(1)} = 2 \text{ Np/s}$.
$\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 \times 0.04}} = \frac{1}{0.2} = 5 \text{ rad/s}$.
Since $\alpha < \omega_0$ ($2 < 5$), it is underdamped.
$\omega_d = \sqrt{25 - 4} = \sqrt{21} \approx 4.58 \text{ rad/s}$.
An impulse current source of strength $Q=1$ in series with L injects an initial current $i(0^+) = \frac{1}{L} = \frac{1}{1} = 1 \text{ A}$.
The capacitor voltage remains continuous, so $v_C(0^+) = v_C(0^-) = 0 \text{ V}$.
We need $\frac{di(0^+)}{dt}$. From KVL: $L \frac{di}{dt} + Ri + v_C = 0 \implies \frac{di}{dt} = -\frac{Ri + v_C}{L}$.
At $t=0^+$: $\frac{di(0^+)}{dt} = -\frac{4(1) + 0}{1} = -4 \text{ A/s}$.
General solution: $i(t) = e^{-2t} (A_1 \cos \sqrt{21}t + A_2 \sin \sqrt{21}t)$.
1. $i(0) = 1 \implies A_1 = 1$.
2. $\frac{di}{dt} = e^{-2t} [ -2(A_1 \cos \omega_d t + A_2 \sin \omega_d t) + (-A_1 \omega_d \sin \omega_d t + A_2 \omega_d \cos \omega_d t) ]$.
At $t=0$: $\frac{di(0)}{dt} = -2A_1 + A_2 \omega_d = -4$.
Substitute $A_1 = 1, \omega_d = \sqrt{21}$:
$-2(1) + A_2 \sqrt{21} = -4 \implies A_2 \sqrt{21} = -2 \implies A_2 = -\frac{2}{\sqrt{21}}$.
Thus, $i(t) = e^{-2t} \left( \cos(\sqrt{21}t) - \frac{2}{\sqrt{21}} \sin(\sqrt{21}t) \right) \text{ A}$.
Answer: i(t) = e⁻²ᵗ (cos(√21 t) - (2/√21)sin(√21 t)) A
P15 · Circuit Problem

A parallel RLC circuit has $R = 10 \, \Omega$, $L = 0.1 \text{ H}$, $C = 0.01 \text{ F}$. The inductor has an initial current $i_L(0) = 2 \text{ A}$ and the capacitor has an initial voltage $v_C(0) = 0 \text{ V}$. Find $v_C(t)$ for $t \ge 0$.

Calculate parameters:
$\alpha = \frac{1}{2RC} = \frac{1}{2(10)(0.01)} = \frac{1}{0.2} = 5 \text{ Np/s}$.
$\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{0.1 \times 0.01}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{0.001}} = \frac{1}{0.0316} \approx 31.62 \text{ rad/s}$.
Since $\alpha < \omega_0$ ($5 < 31.62$), it is underdamped.
$\omega_d = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \alpha^2} = \sqrt{1000 - 25} = \sqrt{975} \approx 31.22 \text{ rad/s}$.
General solution for voltage: $v(t) = e^{-5t} (A_1 \cos \omega_d t + A_2 \sin \omega_d t)$.
1. Apply $v(0) = 0$:
$v(0) = A_1 = 0$.
So, $v(t) = A_2 e^{-5t} \sin \omega_d t$.
2. Find $\frac{dv(0)}{dt}$.
KCL at the top node: $i_R + i_L + i_C = 0 \implies \frac{v}{R} + i_L + C \frac{dv}{dt} = 0$.
At $t=0$: $\frac{v(0)}{R} + i_L(0) + C \frac{dv(0)}{dt} = 0$.
$0 + 2 + 0.01 \frac{dv(0)}{dt} = 0 \implies \frac{dv(0)}{dt} = -\frac{2}{0.01} = -200 \text{ V/s}$.
Differentiate $v(t)$:
$\frac{dv}{dt} = A_2 [ -5 e^{-5t} \sin \omega_d t + e^{-5t} \omega_d \cos \omega_d t ]$.
At $t=0$: $\frac{dv(0)}{dt} = A_2 \omega_d = -200$.
$A_2 = \frac{-200}{\omega_d} = \frac{-200}{\sqrt{975}} \approx -6.406$.
Thus, $v(t) = -\frac{200}{\sqrt{975}} e^{-5t} \sin(\sqrt{975}t) \text{ V}$.
Answer: v(t) = -(200/√975)e⁻⁵ᵗ sin(√975 t) V
