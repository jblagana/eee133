P01 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 8.1, p. 310

Consider a parallel RLC circuit with R = 50 Ω and L = 10 mH. (a) Determine the value of C required for critical damping. (b) If C is adjusted such that the neper frequency α is 2500 rad/s, find the new value of C and classify the nature of the response.

For a parallel RLC circuit, the neper frequency is $\alpha = \frac{1}{2RC}$ and the resonant frequency is $\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}$. (a) For critical damping, $\alpha = \omega_0$. Thus, $\frac{1}{2RC} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}$. Squaring both sides gives $\frac{1}{4R^2C^2} = \frac{1}{LC}$, which simplifies to $C = \frac{L}{4R^2}$. Substituting $L = 10 \text{ mH} = 0.01 \text{ H}$ and $R = 50 \text{ } \Omega$: $C = \frac{0.01}{4(50)^2} = \frac{0.01}{10000} = 10^{-6} \text{ F} = 1 \text{ } \mu\text{F}$. (b) Given $\alpha = 2500 \text{ rad/s}$, we use $\alpha = \frac{1}{2RC}$ to find C: $2500 = \frac{1}{2(50)C} \Rightarrow 2500 = \frac{1}{100C} \Rightarrow C = \frac{1}{250000} = 4 \times 10^{-6} \text{ F} = 4 \text{ } \mu\text{F}$. To classify the response, calculate $\omega_0$ for this C: $\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{0.01 \times 4 \times 10^{-6}}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4 \times 10^{-8}}} = \frac{1}{2 \times 10^{-4}} = 5000 \text{ rad/s}$. Since $\alpha = 2500 < \omega_0 = 5000$, the response is underdamped.
Answer: (a) $C = 1\ \mu\text{F}$; (b) $C = 4\ \mu\text{F}$, underdamped
P02 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 8.2, p. 310

A parallel RLC circuit has a natural response voltage $v(t) = (10 e^{-100t} + 20 e^{-400t}) \text{ V}$ for $t \ge 0$. The capacitance is $C = 20 \text{ } \mu\text{F}$. Determine the inductor current $i_L(0^+)$ in milliamperes.

Apply KCL at the top node for $t \ge 0$: $i_R(t) + i_L(t) + i_C(t) = 0$. We need $i_L(0^+)$. At $t=0$, $v(0) = 10 + 20 = 30 \text{ V}$. The capacitor current is $i_C(t) = C \frac{dv}{dt}$. First, find $\frac{dv}{dt}$: $\frac{dv}{dt} = 10(-100)e^{-100t} + 20(-400)e^{-400t} = -1000e^{-100t} - 8000e^{-400t}$. At $t=0$, $\frac{dv}{dt}(0) = -1000 - 8000 = -9000 \text{ V/s}$. Thus, $i_C(0) = 20 \times 10^{-6} \times (-9000) = -0.18 \text{ A} = -180 \text{ mA}$. The resistor current is $i_R(t) = \frac{v(t)}{R}$. We need R. From the roots $s_1 = -100, s_2 = -400$, $\alpha = \frac{100+400}{2} = 250 \text{ rad/s}$. For parallel RLC, $\alpha = \frac{1}{2RC}$. So $250 = \frac{1}{2 R (20 \times 10^{-6})} \Rightarrow 250 = \frac{1}{40 \times 10^{-6} R} \Rightarrow R = \frac{1}{250 \times 40 \times 10^{-6}} = \frac{1}{0.01} = 100 \text{ } \Omega$. Now, $i_R(0) = \frac{30}{100} = 0.3 \text{ A} = 300 \text{ mA}$. Using KCL: $i_L(0) = -i_R(0) - i_C(0) = -300 \text{ mA} - (-180 \text{ mA}) = -120 \text{ mA}$.
Answer: $-120\ \text{mA}$
P03 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 8.3, p. 310

A parallel RLC circuit has $C = 100 \text{ nF}$ and a natural response voltage $v(t) = (4 e^{-2000t} + 16 e^{-8000t}) \text{ V}$ for $t \ge 0$. (a) Find $\omega_0$, $\alpha$, $L$, and $R$. (b) Find the expressions for $i_R(t)$, $i_L(t)$, and $i_C(t)$ for $t \ge 0$.

(a) The roots are $s_1 = -2000$ and $s_2 = -8000$. The damping factor is $\alpha = \frac{|s_1| + |s_2|}{2} = \frac{2000 + 8000}{2} = 5000 \text{ rad/s}$. The resonant frequency is determined by $\omega_0^2 = |s_1 s_2| = 2000 \times 8000 = 16 \times 10^6$, which yields $\omega_0 = 4000 \text{ rad/s}$. Using the relation $\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}$, the inductance is calculated as $L = \frac{1}{\omega_0^2 C} = \frac{1}{16 \times 10^6 \times 100 \times 10^{-9}} = \frac{1}{1.6} = 0.625 \text{ H}$. Using the relation $\alpha = \frac{1}{2RC}$, the resistance is calculated as $R = \frac{1}{2 \alpha C} = \frac{1}{2(5000)(100 \times 10^{-9})} = \frac{1}{10^6 \times 10^{-6}} = 1 \text{ } \Omega$.

(b) The resistor current is $i_R(t) = \frac{v(t)}{R} = \frac{4 e^{-2000t} + 16 e^{-8000t}}{1} = 4 e^{-2000t} + 16 e^{-8000t} \text{ A}$. The capacitor current is $i_C(t) = C \frac{dv}{dt}$. Differentiating the voltage gives $\frac{dv}{dt} = 4(-2000)e^{-2000t} + 16(-8000)e^{-8000t} = -8000 e^{-2000t} - 128000 e^{-8000t}$. Substituting this into the current equation yields $i_C(t) = 100 \times 10^{-9} (-8000 e^{-2000t} - 128000 e^{-8000t}) = -8 \times 10^{-4} e^{-2000t} - 12.8 \times 10^{-3} e^{-8000t} \text{ A} = -0.8 e^{-2000t} - 12.8 e^{-8000t} \text{ mA}$. The inductor current is found using Kirchhoff's Current Law: $i_L(t) = -i_R(t) - i_C(t)$. Expressing all currents in Amperes, $i_R(t) = 4 e^{-2000t} + 16 e^{-8000t}$ and $i_C(t) = -0.0008 e^{-2000t} - 0.0128 e^{-8000t}$. Therefore, $i_L(t) = -(4 e^{-2000t} + 16 e^{-8000t}) - (-0.0008 e^{-2000t} - 0.0128 e^{-8000t}) = -(4 - 0.0008) e^{-2000t} - (16 - 0.0128) e^{-8000t} = -3.9992 e^{-2000t} - 15.9872 e^{-8000t} \text{ A}$.
Answer: (a) $\omega_0 = 4000 \text{ rad/s}$, $\alpha = 5000 \text{ rad/s}$, $L = 0.625 \text{ H}$, $R = 1 \ \Omega$; (b) $i_R = 4e^{-2000t} + 16e^{-8000t} \text{ A}$, $i_C = -0.8e^{-2000t} - 12.8e^{-8000t} \text{ mA}$, $i_L = -3.9992e^{-2000t} - 15.9872e^{-8000t} \text{ A}$
P04 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 8.4, p. 310

A parallel RLC circuit consists of a 2 H inductor, a 2 nF capacitor, and a resistor R adjusted for critical damping. The initial voltage across the capacitor is $V_0 = -10 \text{ V}$ and the initial inductor current is $I_0 = -2 \text{ mA}$. Calculate the numerical expression for $v(t)$ for $t \ge 0$.

For critical damping, the condition $\alpha = \omega_0$ applies. The natural frequency is calculated as:
$$ \omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2 \times 2 \times 10^{-9}}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4 \times 10^{-9}}} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{10^{-9}}} = \frac{10^{4.5}}{2} = 5000\sqrt{10} \approx 15811.4 \text{ rad/s} $$
Thus, $\alpha = 5000\sqrt{10} \approx 15811.4 \text{ Np/s}$.

The general form of the critically damped response is:
$$ v(t) = (A_1 + A_2 t) e^{-\alpha t} $$

Applying the initial condition $v(0) = V_0 = -10 \text{ V}$:
$$ v(0) = A_1 = -10 \text{ V} $$

To determine $A_2$, the initial slope $\frac{dv}{dt}(0)$ is required. From the circuit dynamics:
$$ \frac{dv}{dt}(0) = \frac{i_C(0)}{C} $$
Using Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL) at the node, the capacitor current is:
$$ i_C(0) = -i_R(0) - i_L(0) = -\frac{V_0}{R} - I_0 $$
The resistance $R$ is derived from the damping factor $\alpha = \frac{1}{2RC}$:
$$ R = \frac{1}{2 \alpha C} = \frac{1}{2 (5000\sqrt{10}) (2 \times 10^{-9})} = \frac{1}{20000\sqrt{10} \times 10^{-9}} = \frac{10^5}{2\sqrt{10}} = 5000\sqrt{10} \approx 15811.4 \text{ } \Omega $$
Substituting the known values $V_0 = -10 \text{ V}$ and $I_0 = -2 \text{ mA} = -0.002 \text{ A}$:
$$ i_R(0) = \frac{-10}{5000\sqrt{10}} = -\frac{1}{500\sqrt{10}} \text{ A} $$
$$ i_C(0) = -\left( -\frac{1}{500\sqrt{10}} \right) - (-0.002) = \frac{1}{500\sqrt{10}} + 0.002 $$
Rationalizing the term $\frac{1}{500\sqrt{10}}$:
$$ \frac{1}{500\sqrt{10}} = \frac{\sqrt{10}}{5000} $$
Thus:
$$ i_C(0) = \frac{\sqrt{10}}{5000} + 0.002 $$
The initial derivative is:
$$ \frac{dv}{dt}(0) = \frac{i_C(0)}{C} = \frac{1}{2 \times 10^{-9}} \left( \frac{\sqrt{10}}{5000} + 0.002 \right) = 5 \times 10^8 \left( \frac{\sqrt{10}}{5000} + 0.002 \right) $$
$$ \frac{dv}{dt}(0) = 100000\sqrt{10} + 1000000 \text{ V/s} $$

The coefficient $A_2$ is found from the derivative of the response function at $t=0$:
$$ \frac{dv}{dt}(0) = A_2 - \alpha A_1 \implies A_2 = \frac{dv}{dt}(0) + \alpha A_1 $$
Substituting the values:
$$ A_2 = (100000\sqrt{10} + 1000000) + (5000\sqrt{10})(-10) $$
$$ A_2 = 100000\sqrt{10} + 1000000 - 50000\sqrt{10} $$
$$ A_2 = 50000\sqrt{10} + 1000000 $$
Evaluating numerically:
$$ 50000\sqrt{10} \approx 158113.9 $$
$$ A_2 \approx 158113.9 + 1000000 = 1158113.9 $$

The final expression for the voltage is:
$$ v(t) = (-10 + 1158114 t) e^{-15811 t} \text{ V} $$
Answer: $$v(t) = (-10 + 1158114t)e^{-15811t} \text{ V}$$

P07 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 8.7, p. 310

A parallel RLC circuit has $R = 1 \text{ k}\Omega$, $L = 100 \text{ mH}$, and $C = 4 \text{ nF}$. The initial inductor current is $i_L(0^-) = -20 \text{ mA}$ and the initial capacitor voltage is $v_C(0^-) = 40 \text{ V}$. Find $v(t)$ for $t \ge 0$.

$\alpha = \frac{1}{2RC} = \frac{1}{2(1000)(4 \times 10^{-9})} = 125000 \text{ rad/s}$. $\omega_0 = 50000 \text{ rad/s}$. Since $\alpha \neq \omega_0$, the circuit is not critically damped with the initial resistance value. To achieve critical damping, the resistance is adjusted such that $\alpha = \omega_0 = 50000 \text{ rad/s}$. The required resistance is calculated as:
$$ R = \frac{1}{2 \alpha C} = \frac{1}{2(50000)(4 \times 10^{-9})} = \frac{1}{400000 \times 10^{-9}} = \frac{1}{4 \times 10^{-4}} = 2500 \text{ } \Omega = 2.5 \text{ k}\Omega $$
With $R = 2.5 \text{ k}\Omega$, the damping factor is $\alpha = 50000 \text{ rad/s}$. The voltage response for a critically damped circuit is given by:
$$ v(t) = (A_1 + A_2 t) e^{-50000 t} $$
Applying the initial condition $v(0) = 40 \text{ V}$:
$$ v(0) = A_1 = 40 $$
The initial current through the resistor is:
$$ i_R(0) = \frac{40}{2500} = 0.016 \text{ A} $$
Given the initial inductor current $i_L(0) = -0.02 \text{ A}$, the initial capacitor current is determined by Kirchhoff's Current Law:
$$ i_C(0) = -i_R(0) - i_L(0) = -0.016 - (-0.02) = 0.004 \text{ A} $$
The initial rate of change of voltage is:
$$ \frac{dv}{dt}(0) = \frac{i_C(0)}{C} = \frac{0.004}{4 \times 10^{-9}} = 10^6 \text{ V/s} $$
Differentiating the general solution for $v(t)$:
$$ \frac{dv}{dt}(t) = A_2 e^{-50000 t} - 50000(A_1 + A_2 t) e^{-50000 t} $$
Evaluating at $t=0$:
$$ \frac{dv}{dt}(0) = A_2 - 50000 A_1 = A_2 - 50000(40) = A_2 - 2000000 $$
Equating the calculated derivative to the expression derived from the circuit parameters:
$$ 10^6 = A_2 - 2000000 \implies A_2 = 3000000 $$
Thus, the voltage response is:
$$ v(t) = (40 + 3 \times 10^6 t) e^{-50000 t} \text{ V} $$
Answer: $v(t) = (40 + 3000000t)e^{-50000t} \text{ V}$

📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 8.13, p. 311

A parallel RLC circuit has $R = 1 \text{ } \Omega$, $C = 100 \text{ mF}$, and $L = 0.1 \text{ H}$. The initial voltage is $V_0 = 0 \text{ V}$ and the initial inductor current is $I_0 = -5 \text{ A}$. (a) Find $v(t)$ for $t \ge 0$. (b) Find the first time $t_1$ where $\frac{dv}{dt} = 0$.

First, determine the damping parameters. The neper frequency is $\alpha = \frac{1}{2RC} = \frac{1}{2(50)(0.5 \times 10^{-3})} = 20$ Np/s. To find $A_2$, the derivative $\frac{di_C}{dt}(0)$ is required. The relationship between current and voltage is given by $i_C(t) = C \frac{dv}{dt}$. $$v'(0) = \frac{i_C(0)}{C} = \frac{0.01}{0.5 \times 10^{-3}} = 20 \text{ V/s}$$

$$v''(0) = -2\alpha v'(0) - \omega_0^2 v(0) = -2(20)(20) - (1000)(0) = -800 \text{ V/s}^2$$

$$\frac{di_C}{dt}(0) = C v''(0) = (0.5 \times 10^{-3})(-800) = -0.4 \text{ A/s}$$

$$\frac{di_C}{dt} = e^{-20t} [ (-20 A_1 + 24.49 A_2) \cos 24.49t + (-20 A_2 - 24.49 A_1) \sin 24.49t ]$$

$$\frac{di_C}{dt}(0) = -20 A_1 + 24.49 A_2$$

$$-0.4 = -20(0.01) + 24.49 A_2$$
$$-0.4 = -0.2 + 24.49 A_2$$
$$-0.2 = 24.49 A_2$$
$$A_2 = -0.008166 \text{ A}$$
