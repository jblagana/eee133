P01 · Circuit Problem

A high-pass RC circuit has $R = 10 \, \text{k}\Omega$ and $C = 100 \, \text{nF}$. Calculate the 3-dB cutoff frequency $f_c$ in Hz.

The cutoff frequency is given by $f_c = \frac{1}{2\pi RC}$.
Substituting values: $R = 10 \times 10^3 \, \Omega$, $C = 100 \times 10^{-9} \, \text{F}$.
$RC = 10^4 \times 10^{-7} = 10^{-3} \, \text{s}$.
$f_c = \frac{1}{2\pi (10^{-3})} = \frac{1000}{2\pi} \approx 159.15 \, \text{Hz}$.
Answer: 159.15 Hz
P02 · Circuit Problem

A low-pass RC circuit has $R = 1 \, \text{k}\Omega$ and $C = 1 \, \mu\text{F}$. Determine the time constant $\tau$ and the 3-dB cutoff frequency $f_c$.

Time constant $\tau = RC = (1000 \, \Omega)(1 \times 10^{-6} \, \text{F}) = 1 \, \text{ms}$.
Cutoff frequency $f_c = \frac{1}{2\pi \tau} = \frac{1}{2\pi (10^{-3})} \approx 159.15 \, \text{Hz}$.
Answer: τ = 1 ms, f_c ≈ 159.15 Hz
P03 · Circuit Problem

A step voltage $v_{in}(t) = 10 \, u(t) \, \text{V}$ is applied to a high-pass RC circuit with $\tau = 5 \, \text{ms}$. Find the output voltage $v_o(t)$ at $t = 5 \, \text{ms}$.

For a high-pass circuit with step input $V$, $v_o(t) = V e^{-t/\tau}$.
Given $V = 10 \, \text{V}$, $\tau = 5 \, \text{ms}$, and $t = 5 \, \text{ms}$.
$v_o(5\text{ms}) = 10 e^{-5/5} = 10 e^{-1}$.
$e^{-1} \approx 0.3679$.
$v_o(5\text{ms}) = 10 \times 0.3679 = 3.679 \, \text{V}$.
Answer: 3.68 V
P04 · Circuit Problem

A step voltage $v_{in}(t) = 5 \, u(t) \, \text{V}$ is applied to a low-pass RC circuit with $\tau = 2 \, \text{ms}$. Find the output voltage $v_o(t)$ at $t = 2 \, \text{ms}$.

For a low-pass circuit with step input $V$, $v_o(t) = V (1 - e^{-t/\tau})$.
Given $V = 5 \, \text{V}$, $\tau = 2 \, \text{ms}$, and $t = 2 \, \text{ms}$.
$v_o(2\text{ms}) = 5 (1 - e^{-2/2}) = 5 (1 - e^{-1})$.
$e^{-1} \approx 0.3679$.
$v_o(2\text{ms}) = 5 (1 - 0.3679) = 5 (0.6321) = 3.1605 \, \text{V}$.
Answer: 3.16 V
P05 · Circuit Problem

A high-pass RC circuit with $\tau = 1 \, \text{ms}$ receives a pulse input $v_{in}(t) = 10 [u(t) - u(t-2)] \, \text{V}$. Calculate the output voltage $v_o(t)$ just before the falling edge at $t = 2 \, \text{ms}^-$.

During the pulse ($0 < t < 2$), $v_o(t) = V_p e^{-t/\tau}$.
At $t = 2 \, \text{ms}^-$, $t = 2\tau$.
$v_o(2\tau) = 10 e^{-2}$.
$e^{-2} \approx 0.1353$.
$v_o(2\text{ms}^-) = 10 \times 0.1353 = 1.353 \, \text{V}$.
Answer: 1.35 V
P06 · Circuit Problem

Using the same circuit as P05, calculate the output voltage $v_o(t)$ just after the falling edge at $t = 2 \, \text{ms}^+$.

At the falling edge, the input drops by $\Delta V = -10 \, \text{V}$.
The capacitor voltage $v_C$ cannot change instantaneously.
$v_o(t) = v_{in}(t) - v_C(t)$.
Just before the edge, $v_o(2^-) = 1.353 \, \text{V}$ and $v_{in}(2^-) = 10 \, \text{V}$.
So $v_C(2^-) = 10 - 1.353 = 8.647 \, \text{V}$.
Just after the edge, $v_{in}(2^+) = 0 \, \text{V}$ and $v_C(2^+) = 8.647 \, \text{V}$.
$v_o(2^+) = 0 - 8.647 = -8.647 \, \text{V}$.
Alternatively, using the rule: $v_o(2^+) = v_o(2^-) - V_p = 1.353 - 10 = -8.647 \, \text{V}$.
Answer: -8.65 V
P07 · Circuit Problem

A low-pass RC circuit with $\tau = 10 \, \text{ms}$ receives a pulse input $v_{in}(t) = 5 [u(t) - u(t-5)] \, \text{V}$. Calculate the output voltage $v_o(t)$ at the end of the pulse, $t = 5 \, \text{ms}$.

During the pulse ($0 < t < 5$), $v_o(t) = V_p (1 - e^{-t/\tau})$.
At $t = 5 \, \text{ms}$, $t = 0.5 \tau$.
$v_o(5\text{ms}) = 5 (1 - e^{-0.5})$.
$e^{-0.5} \approx 0.6065$.
$v_o(5\text{ms}) = 5 (1 - 0.6065) = 5 (0.3935) = 1.9675 \, \text{V}$.
Answer: 1.97 V
P08 · Circuit Problem

A high-pass RC circuit is designed to act as a differentiator for a square wave with period $T = 10 \, \text{ms}$. What is the maximum allowable time constant $\tau$ to satisfy the condition $\tau \ll T/2$? (Assume 'much less than' means at least 10 times smaller).

Condition for differentiator: $\tau \ll T/2$.
$T/2 = 10 \, \text{ms} / 2 = 5 \, \text{ms}$.
Assuming $\tau \le (T/2) / 10$:
$\tau \le 5 \, \text{ms} / 10 = 0.5 \, \text{ms}$.
Thus, the maximum allowable $\tau$ is $0.5 \, \text{ms}$.
Answer: 0.5 ms
P09 · Circuit Problem

A low-pass RC circuit is designed to act as an integrator for a square wave with period $T = 20 \, \text{ms}$. What is the minimum required time constant $\tau$ to satisfy the condition $\tau \gg T/2$? (Assume 'much greater than' means at least 10 times larger).

Condition for integrator: $\tau \gg T/2$.
$T/2 = 20 \, \text{ms} / 2 = 10 \, \text{ms}$.
Assuming $\tau \ge (T/2) \times 10$:
$\tau \ge 10 \, \text{ms} \times 10 = 100 \, \text{ms}$.
Thus, the minimum required $\tau$ is $100 \, \text{ms}$.
Answer: 100 ms
P10 · Circuit Problem

A low-pass RC circuit processes a 50% duty cycle rectangular wave with amplitude $V = 10 \, \text{V}$ and period $T = 100 \, \text{ms}$. The time constant is $\tau = 10 \, \text{ms}$. Calculate the tilt of the output waveform.

Tilt formula: $\text{Tilt} = \frac{2(1-a)}{1+a}$, where $a = e^{-(T/2)/\tau}$.
$T/2 = 50 \, \text{ms}$.
$a = e^{-50/10} = e^{-5} \approx 0.006738$.
$1 - a = 1 - 0.006738 = 0.993262$.
$1 + a = 1 + 0.006738 = 1.006738$.
$\text{Tilt} = \frac{2(0.993262)}{1.006738} \approx \frac{1.986524}{1.006738} \approx 1.973$.
Tilt is often expressed as a percentage: $197.3\%$. However, tilt is typically defined for small distortions. Let's re-evaluate the formula context. The formula provided in the lecture is $\frac{2(1-a)}{1+a}$.
Wait, if $a$ is small, tilt is large. This indicates significant distortion.
Calculation: $2(0.99326)/1.00674 = 1.973$.
As a percentage: $197.3\%$.
(Note: In some contexts, tilt is defined as $(V_{max}-V_{min})/V_{avg}$. Here $V_{avg} = V/2 = 5$. $V_{max}-V_{min} = 2(1-a)V/(1+a)$. Ratio is $2(1-a)/(1+a)$. Correct.)
Answer: 1.97 (or 197%)
P11 · Circuit Problem

For the circuit in P10, calculate the maximum and minimum output voltages in steady state.

Formulas: $v_{o,max} = \frac{V}{1+a}$ and $v_{o,min} = \frac{aV}{1+a}$.
$V = 10 \, \text{V}$, $a = e^{-5} \approx 0.006738$.
$1+a = 1.006738$.
$v_{o,max} = \frac{10}{1.006738} \approx 9.933 \, \text{V}$.
$v_{o,min} = \frac{0.006738 \times 10}{1.006738} \approx \frac{0.06738}{1.006738} \approx 0.0669 \, \text{V}$.
Answer: V_max ≈ 9.93 V, V_min ≈ 0.067 V
P12 · Circuit Problem

A high-pass RC circuit has $R = 5 \, \text{k}\Omega$. It is required to have a cutoff frequency of $1 \, \text{kHz}$. Calculate the required capacitance $C$.

$f_c = \frac{1}{2\pi RC} \implies C = \frac{1}{2\pi R f_c}$.
$R = 5000 \, \Omega$, $f_c = 1000 \, \text{Hz}$.
$C = \frac{1}{2\pi (5000)(1000)} = \frac{1}{10^7 \pi} \approx \frac{1}{3.1416 \times 10^7}$.
$C \approx 3.183 \times 10^{-8} \, \text{F} = 31.83 \, \text{nF}$.
Answer: 31.83 nF
P13 · Circuit Problem

A low-pass RC circuit has $C = 10 \, \text{nF}$. It is required to have a cutoff frequency of $100 \, \text{kHz}$. Calculate the required resistance $R$.

$f_c = \frac{1}{2\pi RC} \implies R = \frac{1}{2\pi C f_c}$.
$C = 10 \times 10^{-9} \, \text{F}$, $f_c = 100 \times 10^3 \, \text{Hz}$.
$R = \frac{1}{2\pi (10^{-8})(10^5)} = \frac{1}{2\pi (10^{-3})} = \frac{1000}{2\pi}$.
$R \approx 159.15 \, \Omega$.
Answer: 159.15 Ω
P14 · Circuit Problem

A high-pass RC circuit with $\tau = 1 \, \text{ms}$ is driven by a triangular wave with peak-to-peak voltage $20 \, \text{V}$ and period $T = 10 \, \text{ms}$. Estimate the peak output voltage if the circuit acts as a differentiator. (Assume the slope of the triangular wave is constant during the rising/falling edges).

Differentiator output: $v_o(t) \approx RC \frac{dv_{in}}{dt}$.
Triangular wave: Peak-to-peak $20 \, \text{V}$, Period $10 \, \text{ms}$.
Half-period $T/2 = 5 \, \text{ms}$. Voltage change $\Delta V = 20 \, \text{V}$ (from -10 to +10 or vice versa).
Slope $m = \frac{\Delta V}{\Delta t} = \frac{20 \, \text{V}}{5 \, \text{ms}} = 4000 \, \text{V/s}$.
$RC = \tau = 1 \, \text{ms} = 10^{-3} \, \text{s}$.
$v_{o,peak} = \tau \times m = 10^{-3} \times 4000 = 4 \, \text{V}$.
(Note: This is an approximation valid for $\tau \ll T/2$. Here $1 \, \text{ms} \ll 5 \, \text{ms}$, so it is reasonable.)
Answer: 4 V
P15 · Circuit Problem

A low-pass RC circuit with $\tau = 10 \, \text{ms}$ is driven by a triangular wave with peak-to-peak voltage $20 \, \text{V}$ and period $T = 10 \, \text{ms}$. Estimate the peak output voltage if the circuit acts as an integrator. (Assume the input is centered at 0V).

Integrator output: $v_o(t) \approx \frac{1}{RC} \int v_{in}(t) dt$.
Triangular wave: $v_{in}(t)$ goes from $-10$ to $10$ in $5 \, \text{ms}$.
Slope $m = 4000 \, \text{V/s}$.
$v_{in}(t) = m t$ for $0 < t < 5 \, \text{ms}$ (starting from -10V? No, let's define from 0 to T/2).
Let's integrate over half period. The output will be a parabolic shape.
Peak output occurs at the end of the half-cycle (maximum area under the curve).
$v_{o,peak} \approx \frac{1}{\tau} \int_{0}^{T/2} v_{in}(t) dt$.
Assume $v_{in}(t)$ starts at 0 and goes to 10V? No, triangular is symmetric.
Let's assume $v_{in}(t)$ is a ramp from $-10$ to $10$ over $5 \, \text{ms}$.
Average value over this interval is 0. This would result in 0 net change?
Wait, the integrator condition implies $v_o$ is the integral. If the input has zero average, the output is bounded.
Let's look at the area.
$v_{in}(t) = 4000 t - 10$ for $0 \le t \le 5 \, \text{ms}$?
At $t=0, v=-10$. At $t=5, v=10$.
Integral $\int_{0}^{5} (4000 t - 10) dt = [2000 t^2 - 10 t]_0^5 = 2000(25) - 50 = 50000 - 50 = 49950 \, \text{V}\cdot\text{ms}$.
$v_{o,peak} = \frac{1}{\tau} \times \text{Area} = \frac{1}{10 \, \text{ms}} \times 49950 \, \text{V}\cdot\text{ms} = 4995 \, \text{V}$.
This is extremely large, indicating the integrator approximation might be pushing limits or the gain is high.
However, strictly following the formula:
$v_o \approx \frac{1}{RC} \int v_{in} dt$.
$1/RC = 1/\tau = 100 \, \text{s}^{-1}$.
Area $\approx 50 \, \text{V} \times 5 \, \text{ms} = 250 \, \text{V}\cdot\text{ms}$ (approx triangle area).
$v_o \approx 100 \times 0.025 = 2.5 \, \text{V}$.
Let's re-calculate area precisely.
Triangle base $5 \, \text{ms}$, height $20 \, \text{V}$ (from -10 to 10). Area = $0.5 \times 5 \times 20 = 50 \, \text{V}\cdot\text{ms}$.
$v_{o,peak} = \frac{1}{10 \, \text{ms}} \times 50 \, \text{V}\cdot\text{ms} = 5 \, \text{V}$.
Answer: 5 V
