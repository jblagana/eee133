Circuit problems
P01 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.90, p. 268

An ideal inverting integrating amplifier has an input resistor of 20 kΩ and a feedback capacitor of 100 nF. The op-amp is powered by ±12 V supplies. At t = 0, a switch connects a 6 V DC source to the input resistor. Assuming the capacitor is initially uncharged, determine the time in milliseconds after the switch closes when the output voltage saturates at the negative supply rail.

The output voltage of an inverting integrator is given by $v_o(t) = -\frac{1}{RC} \int_0^t v_{in}(\tau) d\tau + v_o(0)$. Given $v_{in} = 6$ V, $R = 20 \times 10^3 \, \Omega$, $C = 100 \times 10^{-9}$ F, and $v_o(0) = 0$ V, the slope of the output ramp is $-\frac{6}{(20 \times 10^3)(100 \times 10^{-9})} = -\frac{6}{2 \times 10^{-3}} = -3000$ V/s. The op-amp saturates when $v_o(t) = -12$ V. Setting $-3000 t = -12$, we solve for $t$: $t = \frac{12}{3000} = 0.004$ s. Converting to milliseconds, $t = 4$ ms.
Answer: $4\ \text{ms}$
P02 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.91, p. 268

Consider an ideal inverting integrating amplifier with an input resistance of 50 kΩ and a feedback capacitance of 20 nF. The op-amp has supply rails at ±10 V. A constant input voltage of 5 V is applied at t = 0. If the capacitor has no initial charge, calculate the time in microseconds required for the output to reach the positive saturation limit.

The governing equation for the inverting integrator is $v_o(t) = -\frac{1}{RC} \int_0^t v_{in}(\tau) d\tau$. With $v_{in} = 5$ V, $R = 50 \times 10^3 \, \Omega$, and $C = 20 \times 10^{-9}$ F, the time constant factor $RC = 1000 \times 10^{-6} = 1$ ms. The slope is $-\frac{5}{1 \times 10^{-3}} = -5000$ V/s. The output starts at 0 V and ramps negative. Saturation occurs at $v_o = -10$ V. Solving $-5000 t = -10$ gives $t = \frac{10}{5000} = 0.002$ s. In microseconds, $t = 2000$ µs.
Answer: 2000 µs
P03 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.92, p. 268

Using the circuit from the previous problem (50 kΩ input resistor, 20 nF feedback capacitor, ±10 V supplies), assume the capacitor initially holds a voltage of 2 V with the positive terminal at the output node. A 5 V DC input is applied at t = 0. Determine the time in microseconds until the output saturates at the negative rail.

The initial output voltage is $v_o(0) = 2$ V. The slope of the ramp remains $-\frac{v_{in}}{RC} = -\frac{5}{1 \times 10^{-3}} = -5000$ V/s. The output voltage expression is $v_o(t) = 2 - 5000 t$. Saturation occurs when $v_o(t) = -10$ V. Setting $2 - 5000 t = -10$, we get $5000 t = 12$, so $t = \frac{12}{5000} = 0.0024$ s. Converting to microseconds, $t = 2400$ µs.
Answer: 2400 µs
P04 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.93, p. 269

An ideal inverting integrating amplifier has an input resistor of 10 kΩ and a feedback capacitor of 10 nF. The input is a triangular wave with a period of 4 ms and an amplitude of 4 V, starting at 0 V and rising linearly to 4 V at t = 2 ms, then falling linearly to 0 V at t = 4 ms. The capacitor is uncharged at t = 0. Find the expression for $v_o(t)$ for $0 \le t \le 2$ ms and determine the peak negative output voltage.

For $0 \le t \le 2$ ms, the input voltage is $v_{in}(t) = \frac{4}{2 \times 10^{-3}} t = 2000 t$ V. The output is $v_o(t) = -\frac{1}{RC} \int_0^t 2000 \tau d\tau$. With $R = 10^4 \, \Omega$ and $C = 10^{-8}$ F, $RC = 10^{-4}$ s. Thus, $v_o(t) = -\frac{1}{10^{-4}} \left[ 1000 \tau^2 \right]_0^t = -10^4 (1000 t^2) = -10^7 t^2$ V. At $t = 2$ ms $= 2 \times 10^{-3}$ s, $v_o(2 \text{ ms}) = -10^7 (4 \times 10^{-6}) = -40$ V. The mathematical peak negative voltage is -40 V.
Answer: $v_o(t) = -10^7 t^2 \text{ V}$; Peak $= -40 \text{ V}$
P05 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.94, p. 269

A voltage pulse of amplitude 10 V and width 1 ms is applied to an ideal inverting integrating amplifier with an input resistor of 20 kΩ and a feedback capacitor of 50 nF. The output voltage is zero at t = 0. Derive the numerical expression for $v_o(t)$ during the pulse ($0 \le t \le 1$ ms) and immediately after the pulse ends ($t > 1$ ms).

During the pulse ($0 \le t \le 1$ ms), $v_{in} = 10$ V. The slope is $-\frac{10}{(20 \times 10^3)(50 \times 10^{-9})} = -\frac{10}{10^{-3}} = -10,000$ V/s. Thus, $v_o(t) = -10,000 t$ V. At $t = 1$ ms $= 10^{-3}$ s, $v_o(1 \text{ ms}) = -10$ V. After the pulse ends ($t > 1$ ms), $v_{in} = 0$ V, so the capacitor holds its charge and the output remains constant at the value reached at the end of the pulse. Therefore, $v_o(t) = -10$ V for $t > 1$ ms.
Answer: $v_o(t) = -10{,}000t \text{ V}$ for $0 \le t \le 1 \text{ ms}$; $v_o(t) = -10 \text{ V}$ for $t > 1 \text{ ms}$

P06 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.95, p. 269

Repeat the previous problem, but add a 1 MΩ resistor in parallel with the 50 nF feedback capacitor. The input is a 10 V pulse of width 1 ms. The output is zero at t = 0. Find the expression for $v_o(t)$ during the pulse and the steady-state output voltage after the pulse if the pulse were infinitely long (or simply the DC gain limit).

This is a practical integrator. The transfer function is $H(s) = -\frac{R_f}{R_{in}} \frac{1}{1 + s R_f C}$. The time constant is $\tau = R_f C = (10^6)(50 \times 10^{-9}) = 0.05$ s. The DC gain is $-\frac{R_f}{R_{in}} = -\frac{10^6}{20 \times 10^3} = -50$. For a step input $V_{in}$, the output response is $v_o(t) = -V_{in} \frac{R_f}{R_{in}} (1 - e^{-t/\tau})$. With $V_{in} = 10$ V, $v_o(t) = -500 (1 - e^{-t/0.05})$ V. The steady-state value (as $t \to \infty$) is $-500$ V, which would saturate, but mathematically the limit is $-500$ V. The question asks for the expression during the pulse (1 ms is much less than 0.05 s, so it approximates the ideal integrator) and the steady-state limit. The expression is $v_o(t) = -500(1 - e^{-20t})$ V. The steady-state limit is -500 V.
Answer: $v_o(t) = -500(1 - e^{-20t}) \text{ V}$; Limit = $-500 \text{ V}$
P07 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.96, p. 269

A circuit consists of two cascaded ideal inverting integrating amplifiers. The first stage has an input resistor $R_1 = 10$ kΩ and feedback capacitor $C_1 = 10$ nF. The second stage has an input resistor $R_2 = 10$ kΩ and feedback capacitor $C_2 = 10$ nF. The input to the first stage is a constant voltage $V_{in} = 5$ V applied at t = 0. All capacitors are initially uncharged. Find the expression for the final output voltage $v_{o2}(t)$.

The first integrator output is $v_{o1}(t) = -\frac{1}{R_1 C_1} \int_0^t V_{in} d\tau = -\frac{5}{(10^4)(10^{-8})} t = -5000 t$ V. This voltage is the input to the second integrator. The second integrator output is $v_{o2}(t) = -\frac{1}{R_2 C_2} \int_0^t v_{o1}(\tau) d\tau = -\frac{1}{10^{-4}} \int_0^t (-5000 \tau) d\tau = -10^4 \left[ -2500 \tau^2 \right]_0^t = 25,000,000 t^2$ V. Thus, $v_{o2}(t) = 2.5 \times 10^7 t^2$ V.
Answer: $$v_{o2}(t) = 2.5 \times 10^7 t^2 \text{ V}$$
P08 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.97, p. 270

An astable multivibrator circuit uses two transistors with collector resistors $R_C = 10$ kΩ and coupling capacitors $C = 1$ nF. The base resistors are $R_B = 100$ kΩ. The supply voltage is $V_{CC} = 10$ V. Assuming the transistors switch between saturation and cutoff, and the capacitor charges towards $V_{CC}$ through $R_B$ and discharges towards $-V_{CC}$ (or ground, depending on configuration, assume standard symmetric astable with $V_{CC}$ and ground), derive the expression for the time constant of the charging phase. Note: For a standard symmetric astable multivibrator with $R_1=R_2=R$ and $C_1=C_2=C$, the on-time is $t_{on} = R C \ln(2)$. Calculate $t_{on}$.

In a standard symmetric astable multivibrator, the capacitor charges from 0 V towards $V_{CC}$ through the base resistor $R_B$. The switching occurs when the base-emitter voltage reaches the turn-on threshold, typically approximated as 0.7 V, but in idealized textbook problems for timing, the threshold is often taken as half the supply or derived from the voltage divider. However, the standard formula for the time a transistor remains ON in a symmetric astable multivibrator (where the capacitor charges from 0 to $V_{CC}/2$ effectively due to the other transistor's state) is $t_{on} = R_B C \ln(2)$. Using $R_B = 100$ kΩ and $C = 1$ nF, $t_{on} = (100 \times 10^3)(1 \times 10^{-9}) \ln(2) = 10^{-4} \times 0.693 = 69.3 \times 10^{-6}$ s. Thus, $t_{on} = 69.3$ µs.
Answer: 69.3 µs
P09 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.98, p. 270

Consider an astable multivibrator with $V_{CC} = 9$ V, $R_1 = R_2 = 18$ kΩ, and $C_1 = C_2 = 2$ nF. Assuming the standard symmetric operation where the on-time is given by $t_{on} = R C \ln(2)$ and the off-time is equal to the on-time, calculate: (a) the time transistor T2 is in the off state, (b) the time transistor T2 is in the on state, and (c) the frequency of oscillation.

P11 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.100, p. 270

Design an astable multivibrator circuit using two NPN transistors with a supply voltage $V_{CC} = 15$ V and load resistors $R_L = 4$ kΩ. The circuit uses two coupling capacitors $C_1 = C_2 = 0.01$ µF. Determine the values of the base resistors $R_1$ and $R_2$ such that each transistor is ON for $40$ µs and OFF for $60$ µs per cycle. Assume the base-emitter voltage drop is $0.7$ V and the capacitors charge/discharge between $0.7$ V and $-0.7$ V.

The OFF time of the transistor is determined by the charging of the coupling capacitor from $-0.7$ V to $+0.7$ V through the base resistor. The voltage across the capacitor is described by $v_C(t) = V_{final} - (V_{final} - V_{initial})e^{-t/RC}$. In this configuration, $V_{initial} = -0.7$ V and $V_{final} = 0.7$ V. The standard formula for the OFF time $t_{off}$ in an astable multivibrator, where the capacitor charges from $-V_{BE}$ towards $V_{CC}$, is:

$$t_{off} = R C \ln\left(\frac{V_{CC} + V_{BE}}{V_{CC} - V_{BE}}\right)$$

Given $t_{off} = 60$ µs, $V_{CC} = 15$ V, $V_{BE} = 0.7$ V, and $C = 0.01$ µF, the argument of the logarithm is calculated as:

$$\frac{V_{CC} + V_{BE}}{V_{CC} - V_{BE}} = \frac{15 + 0.7}{15 - 0.7} = \frac{15.7}{14.3} \approx 1.0979$$

The natural logarithm of this ratio is:

$$\ln(1.0979) \approx 0.0934$$

Substituting the known values into the time equation:

$$60 \times 10^{-6} = R (0.01 \times 10^{-6}) (0.0934)$$

Solving for $R$:

$$R = \frac{60 \times 10^{-6}}{0.01 \times 10^{-6} \times 0.0934} = \frac{60}{0.000934} \approx 64240 \text{ }\Omega$$

Rounding to a standard value, $R_1 = R_2 = 64$ kΩ.
Answer: $R_1 = R_2 = 64\ \text{k}\Omega$
P12 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.104, p. 271

A cardiac pacemaker circuit uses a DC source $V_s = 9$ V, a resistor $R = 100$ kΩ, and a capacitor $C = 10$ µF. The circuit triggers a pulse when the capacitor voltage reaches a threshold $V_{max} = 6$ V. Assuming the capacitor starts discharging to 0 V immediately after a pulse, calculate the time interval between pulses (the charging time to $V_{max}$).

The voltage across the charging capacitor in an RC circuit is given by $v_C(t) = V_s (1 - e^{-t/RC})$. To determine the time $t$ when $v_C(t) = V_{max}$, set $V_{max} = V_s (1 - e^{-t/RC})$ and rearrange to solve for $t$:

$$ \frac{V_{max}}{V_s} = 1 - e^{-t/RC} \implies e^{-t/RC} = 1 - \frac{V_{max}}{V_s} $$

Taking the natural logarithm of both sides:

$$ -\frac{t}{RC} = \ln\left(1 - \frac{V_{max}}{V_s}\right) \implies t = -RC \ln\left(1 - \frac{V_{max}}{V_s}\right) = RC \ln\left(\frac{V_s}{V_s - V_{max}}\right) $$

Substituting the given values $R = 100$ kΩ $= 10^5$ Ω, $C = 10$ µF $= 10^{-5}$ F, $V_s = 9$ V, and $V_{max} = 6$ V:

The time constant is $RC = 10^5 \times 10^{-5} = 1$ s.

The ratio is $\frac{V_s}{V_s - V_{max}} = \frac{9}{9 - 6} = \frac{9}{3} = 3$.

Therefore, $t = 1 \times \ln(3) \approx 1.0986$ s.
Answer: $t = 1.099 \text{ s}$
P13 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.105, p. 271

Consider a pacemaker circuit with a supply voltage $V_s = 12$ V and a capacitor $C = 5$ µF. The trigger threshold is set to $V_{max} = 9$ V. If the desired time between pulses is 1.5 seconds, determine the required resistance $R$ in the charging branch.

Using the charging equation derived previously, $t = RC \ln\left(\frac{V_s}{V_s - V_{max}}\right)$. We are given $t = 1.5$ s, $V_s = 12$ V, $V_{max} = 9$ V, and $C = 5$ µF $= 5 \times 10^{-6}$ F. Solving for $R$: $R = \frac{1.5}{5 \times 10^{-6} \times 1.3863} = \frac{1.5}{6.9315 \times 10^{-6}} \approx 216403$ Ω. Rounding to three significant figures, $R \approx 216$ kΩ.

P14 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.106, p. 271

Derive a general expression for the resistance $R$ in a pacemaker circuit in terms of the supply voltage $V_s$, the threshold voltage $V_{max}$, the capacitance $C$, and the desired heart rate $f$ in beats per minute. Assume the capacitor discharges instantaneously to 0 V after each trigger.

The period $T$ of the pacemaker is the inverse of the frequency in Hz. If the heart rate is $f$ beats per minute, the frequency in Hz is $f_{Hz} = \frac{f}{60}$. Thus, the period is $T = \frac{60}{f}$ seconds. The charging time to reach $V_{max}$ is given by $T = RC \ln\left(\frac{V_s}{V_s - V_{max}}\right)$. We can rearrange this equation to solve for $R$: $R = \frac{T}{C \ln\left(\frac{V_s}{V_s - V_{max}}\right)}$. Substituting $T = \frac{60}{f}$, we get $R = \frac{60}{f C \ln\left(\frac{V_s}{V_s - V_{max}}\right)}$. This is the required general expression.
Answer: $$R = \frac{60}{f C \ln\left(\frac{V_s}{V_s - V_{max}}\right)}$$

P16 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Hayt 8e, Prob 7.5 #51, p. 254

An op-amp circuit is configured as a differentiator by placing a capacitor $C = 0.1$ µF in the input branch and a resistor $R_f = 10$ kΩ in the feedback branch. The input voltage is $v_s(t) = 5 \cos(1000t)$ V. (a) Determine the output voltage $v_o(t)$ assuming an ideal op-amp. (b) If the op-amp has a finite open-loop gain $A = 10^4$, input resistance $R_i = \infty$, and output resistance $R_o = 0$, derive the expression for $v_o(t)$ in terms of $v_s(t)$ and calculate the amplitude of the output.

(a) For an ideal op-amp differentiator, the output voltage is given by $v_o(t) = -R_f C \frac{dv_s(t)}{dt}$. Given $v_s(t) = 5 \cos(1000t)$, the derivative is $\frac{dv_s}{dt} = -5000 \sin(1000t)$. Substituting $R_f = 10^4$ Ω and $C = 0.1 \times 10^{-6}$ F: $v_o(t) = -(10^4)(0.1 \times 10^{-6})(-5000 \sin(1000t)) = (10^{-3})(5000) \sin(1000t) = 5 \sin(1000t)$ V. (b) For a finite gain $A$, the relationship is $v_o = -A v_d$, where $v_d = v_- - v_+$. Since $v_+ = 0$, $v_d = v_-$. The current through the capacitor is $i_C = C \frac{dv_s}{dt}$. This current flows through the feedback resistor $R_f$ and the op-amp input (infinite impedance). The voltage at the inverting node is $v_- = -i_C R_f = -R_f C \frac{dv_s}{dt}$. However, this assumes ideal virtual ground. With finite gain, $v_- = -\frac{v_o}{A}$. The KCL at the inverting node is $\frac{v_s - v_-}{1/j\omega C} + \frac{v_o - v_-}{R_f} = 0$. In the time domain, $C \frac{d}{dt}(v_s - v_-) + \frac{1}{R_f}(v_o - v_-) = 0$. Since $v_- = -v_o/A$, we have $C \frac{d}{dt}(v_s + \frac{v_o}{A}) + \frac{1}{R_f}(v_o + \frac{v_o}{A}) = 0$. For sinusoidal steady state, $v_s = V_s e^{j\omega t}$, $v_o = V_o e^{j\omega t}$. $j\omega C (V_s + \frac{V_o}{A}) + \frac{1}{R_f} V_o (1 + \frac{1}{A}) = 0$. $j\omega C V_s + V_o [ \frac{j\omega C}{A} + \frac{1}{R_f}(1 + \frac{1}{A}) ] = 0$. $V_o = \frac{-j\omega C V_s}{\frac{j\omega C}{A} + \frac{1}{R_f}(1 + \frac{1}{A})}$. For large $A$, $\frac{1}{A}$ is small. $V_o \approx \frac{-j\omega C V_s}{\frac{1}{R_f}} = -j\omega R_f C V_s$. The magnitude is $|V_o| = \omega R_f C |V_s|$. $\omega = 1000$, $R_f = 10^4$, $C = 10^{-7}$, $|V_s| = 5$. $|V_o| = 1000 \times 10^4 \times 10^{-7} \times 5 = 5$ V. The finite gain correction is negligible for $A=10^4$ at this frequency.
Answer: (a) $v_o(t) = 5 \sin(1000t) \text{ V}$; (b) Amplitude $= 5 \text{ V}$

The input voltage is $v_s(t)$. (a) Find $v_o(t)$ if $v_s(t) = 4 u(t)$ V. (b) Find $v_o(t)$ if $v_s(t) = 4 e^{-130000 t} u(t)$ V.

The transfer function is $H(s) = -\frac{Z_f}{Z_{in}} = -\frac{R_2 || \frac{1}{sC}}{R_1} = -\frac{\frac{R_2}{1 + s R_2 C}}{R_1} = -\frac{R_2}{R_1} \frac{1}{1 + s \tau}$, where $\tau = R_2 C = 10^{-3}$ s. $H(s) = -1 \frac{1}{1 + 1000 s}$. (a) $v_s(t) = 4 u(t) \implies V_s(s) = \frac{4}{s}$. $V_o(s) = -\frac{4}{s(1 + 1000 s)} = -\frac{4}{1000} \frac{1}{s(s + 0.001)} = -4 \frac{1}{s(s + 1000)}$. Partial fractions: $\frac{1}{s(s+1000)} = \frac{A}{s} + \frac{B}{s+1000}$. $A = \frac{1}{1000}$, $B = -\frac{1}{1000}$. $V_o(s) = -4 (\frac{1}{1000 s} - \frac{1}{1000 (s+1000)}) = -\frac{4}{1000} (\frac{1}{s} - \frac{1}{s+1000})$. $v_o(t) = -4 (1 - e^{-1000 t}) u(t)$ V. (b) $v_s(t) = 4 e^{-130000 t} u(t) \implies V_s(s) = \frac{4}{s + 130000}$. $V_o(s) = -\frac{1}{1 + 1000 s} \frac{4}{s + 130000} = -\frac{4}{(1 + 1000 s)(s + 130000)} = -\frac{4}{1000 (s + 0.001)(s + 130000)}$. This is a convolution of two exponentials.
Answer: (a) $v_o(t) = -4(1 - e^{-1000t})u(t) \text{ V}$. (b) $v_o(t) = -4 \frac{e^{-0.001t} - e^{-130000t}}{130000 - 0.001} u(t) \text{ V}$

An inverting op-amp differentiator circuit has an input capacitor $C = 10$ nF and a feedback resistor $R = 1$ kΩ. Calculate the output voltage $v_{out}(t)$ if the input voltage $v_s(t)$ is (a) $2 \cos(100 t)$ mV; (b) $2 \sin(4 t + 19^\circ)$ V.

The output of an inverting differentiator is $v_{out}(t) = -RC \frac{dv_s}{dt}$. Here $RC = 10^{-3}$ s. (a) $v_s(t) = 2 \cos(100 t)$ mV. $\frac{dv_s}{dt} = -200 \sin(100 t)$ mV/s. $v_{out}(t) = -10^{-3} (-200 \sin(100 t)) = 0.2 \sin(100 t)$ mV. (b) $v_s(t) = 2 \sin(4 t + 19^\circ)$ V. $\frac{dv_s}{dt} = 8 \cos(4 t + 19^\circ)$ V/s. $v_{out}(t) = -10^{-3} (8 \cos(4 t + 19^\circ)) = -8 \times 10^{-3} \cos(4 t + 19^\circ)$ V = $-8 \cos(4 t + 19^\circ)$ mV.
Answer: (a) $0.2 \sin(100t) \text{ mV}$; (b) $-8 \cos(4t + 19^\circ) \text{ mV}$
