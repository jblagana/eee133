First-Order Circuits · Part 1

Where does the famous exponential come from? This lecture derives it from a first-order differential equation, defines the time constant, and works the two source-free cases — RC and RL.

🎯 Initial conditions & τ of RL / RC ✏️ Solve 1st-order linear DEs 📈 Separate transient & steady-state

1 What is a first-order circuit?

Any circuit with exactly one independent energy-storage element (a single L or single C, after all series/parallel reductions), an arbitrary number of sources, and an arbitrary number of resistors. Any voltage or current in such a circuit is the solution of a first-order differential equation.

📦 “Independent” energy-storage elements

Elements are independent if they cannot be reduced to a single equivalent element via series or parallel combinations. Two series capacitors that collapse to one equivalent capacitor? Not independent — the circuit is still first-order. A C and an L that can't be merged? Second-order (beyond this lecture).

RL networks and RC networks are the two flavors of first-order circuit. Everything in this lecture is a statement about those two families.

3 Example 1 · An intuitive transient (switched RC)

“The switch has been in position 1 for a very, very long time. At t = 0 it moves to position 2. Sketch v(t) and i(t) for all time.”

For t < 0 the switch charges the capacitor from E. At t = 0 it swings to position 2 and the capacitor is left with R alone.

“Very, very long time” ⇒ DC steady state ⇒ the capacitor is an open circuit. No current flows, so the full source appears across C:

\[ v(0^-) = E, \qquad i(0^-) = 0 \]

Capacitor voltage cannot jump, so the discharge starts exactly where the charging left off:

\[ v(0^+) = v(0^-) = E \]

(The current will jump — see step 4. Only v is continuous.)

Position 2 is a capacitor and a resistor in a loop — the source-free RC circuit of §7. Its solution is the decaying exponential:

\[ v(t) = E\,e^{-t/RC}, \qquad t \ge 0 \]

With the passive reference direction shown, \( i = C\,dv/dt \):

\[ i(t) = C\,\frac{d}{dt}\left(E e^{-t/RC}\right) = -\frac{E}{R}\,e^{-t/RC} \]

At t = 0⁺ the capacitor (still at E) sees the whole of R: \( i(0^+) = -E/R \). The current jumps from 0 to −E/R at the switching instant while v stays continuous — the sketch on the slide follows immediately.

4 The first-order differential equation

Take the simplest excited case — an inductor, a resistor and a DC source (the circuit of §9). KVL gives

(3.1) \[ L\,\frac{di(t)}{dt} + R\,i(t) = V \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \frac{di}{dt} + \frac{R}{L}\,i = \frac{V}{L} \] First-order, linear, constant-coefficient. No matter how complicated the rest of the network is, its response always looks like this.

The general solution is the sum of two pieces, which the rest of the lecture separates:

\[ i(t) = \underbrace{i_{\text{natural}}(t)}_{\text{solution of the homogeneous DE}} \;+\; \underbrace{i_{\text{forced}}(t)}_{\text{solution of the full DE}} \]

5 Homogeneous DE ⇒ natural response

Set the source to zero: \( \dfrac{di}{dt} + \dfrac{R}{L} i = 0 \). Try a solution of the form \( i(t) = A\,e^{st} \); substituting gives the characteristic equation:

\[ s + \frac{R}{L} = 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad s = -\frac{R}{L} \]

So every natural response in a first-order circuit has the shape

(3.2) \[ i_{\text{natural}}(t) = A\,e^{-t/\tau}, \qquad \tau = \frac{L}{R}\ \ (\text{RL}) \;\;\text{or}\;\; \tau = RC\ \ (\text{RC}) \] An exponential decay. The constant A is fixed by the initial condition; τ is fixed by the circuit.
🌿 Properties of the natural (transient) response

(1) It is an exponential whose rate is set by R, L, C. (2) It is called the natural response of the network. (3) It is independent of the sources — kill the sources and it’s still there. (4) It is the bridge between the initial state and the final steady state.

6 The time constant τ

The time constant \( \tau \) is the single number that sets the speed of any first-order transient:

\( \tau_{RC} = R\,C \)RC network [s]
\( \tau_{RL} = \dfrac{L}{R} \)RL network [s]

After \( t = n\tau \), a decay \( e^{-n} \) of its initial swing has disappeared:

tτ2τ3τ4τ5τ
remaining fraction 36.8%13.5%5.0%1.8%0.7%
“progress” toward final value 63.2%86.5%95.0%98.2%99.3%
⏱️ The 5τ rule

By \( t = 5\tau \) the transient is down to under 1% — engineers call that steady state. “How long is the transient?” is answered by τ alone.

7 The source-free RC circuit

A charged capacitor (\( v(0) = V_0 \)) in a loop with a resistor. KCL/KVL around the loop: \( R\,i + v = 0 \) with \( i = C\,dv/dt \):

\[ RC\,\frac{dv}{dt} + v = 0 \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; \boxed{\,v(t) = V_0\,e^{-t/\tau}\,},\qquad \tau = RC \] Voltage decays from V₀ with time constant τ = RC. The current is \( i(t) = -\dfrac{V_0}{R}e^{-t/\tau} \) — an opposite-sign exponential that jumps at t = 0.
🔁 Energy viewpoint

All energy the capacitor started with, \( \tfrac12 C V_0^2 \), ends up as heat in R: \( \int_0^\infty R i^2 dt = \tfrac12 C V_0^2 \). The exponential is just the bookkeeping of that hand-off.

8 The source-free RL circuit

An inductor carrying \( i(0) = I_0 \) in a loop with a resistor. KVL: \( L\,di/dt + R\,i = 0 \):

\[ \boxed{\,i(t) = I_0\,e^{-t/\tau}\,},\qquad \tau = \frac{L}{R} \] Current decays from I₀ with time constant τ = L/R. The inductor voltage is \( v(t) = -R I_0 e^{-t/\tau} \).

Same shape, different element: where RC decays its voltage, RL decays its current — the continuous quantity of each element.

9 The step response: forced + natural

Now energize the RL loop: a DC source V steps in at t = 0 (switch closes), with \( i(0) = 0 \). The answer has two parts:

Forced: \( i_f = \dfrac{V}{R} \) the constant that makes the DE true forever (DC: inductor = short, so i = V/R)
Natural: \( i_n = A\,e^{-Rt/L} \) the homogeneous solution from §5
(3.3) \[ i(t) = \frac{V}{R} + A\,e^{-Rt/L} \quad\xrightarrow{\;i(0)=0\;}\quad \boxed{\,i(t) = \frac{V}{R}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right)\,},\quad \tau = \frac{L}{R} \] Rises from 0 toward V/R. At t = τ the current has covered 63.2% of its total swing; at 5τ it is in steady state.

10 Solving the non-homogeneous DE

The formal recipe behind (3.3), matching the worked slides: (1) write the DE, (2) solve the homogeneous part, (3) guess the forced part, (4) fix the constant from the initial condition.

\[ \frac{di}{dt} + \frac{R}{L}\,i = \frac{V}{L} \qquad (t \ge 0) \]
\[ i_h(t) = A\,e^{-(R/L)t} \]

The source is constant ⇒ guess a constant \( i_f = K \). Substituting: \( 0 + (R/L)K = V/L \Rightarrow K = V/R \).

\[ i(t) = \frac{V}{R} + A\,e^{-(R/L)t} \]

The switch was open before t = 0, so \( i(0^-) = 0 \); inductor current is continuous: \( i(0^+) = 0 \):

\[ 0 = \frac{V}{R} + A \;\Rightarrow\; A = -\frac{V}{R} \quad\Rightarrow\quad i(t) = \frac{V}{R}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right) \]

The slide then plots exactly this: an exponential climb from 0 to V/R.

🧮 General first-order response — the formula to keep

For any first-order circuit, any state variable x(t) (capacitor voltage, inductor current, any branch quantity that is first-order):

\[ x(t) = x(\infty) + \big[x(0^+) - x(\infty)\big]\,e^{-t/\tau} \]

Three ingredients — final value, initial value, time constant — and you never touch a differential equation again. This is the engine of Lecture 4.

11 Transient vs steady-state response

Transient response

  • Solution of the homogeneous DE: \( A e^{-t/\tau} \).
  • Its rate is set by the R, L, C values — independent of the sources.
  • Also called the natural response.
  • Serves as the transition from the initial steady state to the final one.

Steady-state (forced) response

  • Solution of the original (non-homogeneous) DE.
  • Called forced: its form is forced on the network by the applied source.
  • Same form as the source (plus unique derivatives) — for a DC source, a constant.
  • Independent of the initial conditions; exists as long as the source is applied.

13 Summary

📌

• A first-order circuit has one independent energy-storage element; every response satisfies a first-order linear DE.

• The response = natural (homogeneous, \( Ae^{-t/\tau} \), source-independent) + forced/steady-state (full DE, source-shaped, initial-condition-independent).

• Source-free RC: \( v = V_0 e^{-t/RC} \). Source-free RL: \( i = I_0 e^{-Rt/L} \).

• \( \tau_{RC} = RC \), \( \tau_{RL} = L/R \); the transient is “done” at \( 5\tau \).

• Universal form: \( x(t) = x(\infty) + [x(0^+) - x(\infty)]e^{-t/\tau} \).
