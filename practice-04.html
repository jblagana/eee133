P01 · Circuit Problem

Consider a circuit with a 10 mA current source in parallel with a 2 kΩ resistor and an inductor of 50 mH. The switch has been in position 'a' (connecting the source and resistor to the inductor) for a long time. At t = 0, the switch moves to position 'b', disconnecting the source and resistor, leaving the inductor in a short circuit. Find the inductor current i(t) for t >= 0 and the voltage v(t) across the inductor for t >= 0+.

1. Determine the initial condition $i(0^-)$: For $t < 0$, the circuit is in DC steady state. The inductor acts as a short circuit. The 10 mA source flows entirely through the inductor because the parallel resistor is bypassed by the shorted inductor branch. Thus, $i(0^-) = 10 \text{ mA}$. Since inductor current is continuous, $i(0^+) = 10 \text{ mA}$.

2. Determine the final condition $i(\infty)$: For $t > 0$, the source is disconnected and the inductor is connected to a 5 kΩ resistor. There is no energy source, so the current decays to zero. $i(\infty) = 0 \text{ A}$.

3. Determine the time constant $\tau$: The equivalent resistance seen by the inductor is $R_{eq} = 5 \text{ k}\Omega$. The time constant is calculated as:
$$ \tau = \frac{L}{R} = \frac{50 \text{ mH}}{5 \text{ k}\Omega} = 10 \text{ \mu s} $$

4. General solution:
$$ i(t) = i(\infty) + [i(0) - i(\infty)]e^{-t/\tau} $$
$$ i(t) = 0 + (10 \text{ mA} - 0)e^{-t/10\mu s} = 10e^{-100000t} \text{ mA} $$

5. Voltage $v(t)$: Using the passive sign convention (current entering the positive terminal), $v(t) = L \frac{di}{dt}$.
$$ \frac{di}{dt} = 10 \text{ mA} \cdot (-100000) e^{-100000t} = -1000 e^{-100000t} \text{ A/s} $$
$$ v(t) = 50 \times 10^{-3} \text{ H} \cdot (-1000) e^{-100000t} = -50 e^{-100000t} \text{ V} $$

Alternatively, using Ohm's law on the resistor:
$$ v(t) = -i(t)R = -(10e^{-100000t} \text{ mA})(5 \text{ k}\Omega) = -50 e^{-100000t} \text{ V} $$

The negative sign indicates that the polarity opposes the initial current direction.
Answer: $i(t) = 10e^{-100000t} \text{ mA}$, $v(t) = -50e^{-100000t} \text{ V}$
P02 · Circuit Problem

Using the circuit from Problem P01 (10 mA source, 2 kΩ resistor, 50 mH inductor, switching to a 5 kΩ resistor at t=0), calculate: (a) the total energy dissipated in the 5 kΩ resistor for t >= 0; (b) the energy trapped in the inductor at t = infinity.

1. Initial Energy: The energy stored in the inductor at $t=0$ is $w(0) = \frac{1}{2} L i(0)^2$. Given $L = 50 \text{ mH} = 0.05 \text{ H}$ and $i(0) = 10 \text{ mA} = 0.01 \text{ A}$. $w(0) = 0.5 \cdot 0.05 \cdot (0.01)^2 = 0.5 \cdot 0.05 \cdot 10^{-4} = 2.5 \times 10^{-6} \text{ J} = 2.5 \text{ \mu J}$. 2. Final Energy: As $t \to \infty$, the current $i(\infty) = 0 \text{ A}$. Therefore, the final energy stored in the inductor is $w(\infty) = \frac{1}{2} L (0)^2 = 0 \text{ J}$. 3. Energy Dissipated: By conservation of energy, the total energy dissipated in the resistor is the difference between the initial and final stored energy. $w_{diss} = w(0) - w(\infty) = 2.5 \text{ \mu J} - 0 \text{ \mu J} = 2.5 \text{ \mu J}$. 4. Verification by Integration: Power dissipated in resistor $p(t) = i(t)^2 R$. $i(t) = 0.01 e^{-100000t} \text{ A}$. $p(t) = (0.01 e^{-100000t})^2 \cdot 5000 = 10^{-4} e^{-200000t} \cdot 5000 = 0.5 e^{-200000t} \text{ W}$. Energy $w = \int_0^\infty p(t) dt = \int_0^\infty 0.5 e^{-200000t} dt = 0.5 \left[ \frac{e^{-200000t}}{-200000} \right]_0^\infty = 0.5 \left( 0 - \frac{1}{-200000} \right) = \frac{0.5}{200000} = \frac{1}{400000} = 2.5 \times 10^{-6} \text{ J}$. The results match.
Answer: (a) $2.5\ \mu\text{J}$, (b) $0\ \text{J}$
P03 · Circuit Problem

A 12 V DC source is connected in series with a 4 Ω resistor and a 2 mH inductor. The switch has been closed for a long time. At t = 0, the switch opens, disconnecting the source and connecting the inductor to a 6 Ω resistor. Find: (a) the initial current in the inductor; (b) the final current; (c) the time in microseconds after t=0 until the current reaches 50% of its initial value.

1. Initial Condition $i(0^-)$: For $t < 0$, the circuit is in steady state with the 12 V source and 4 Ω resistor. The inductor acts as a short. $i(0^-) = \frac{V}{R_{source}} = \frac{12 \text{ V}}{4 \Omega} = 3 \text{ A}$. Thus, $i(0^+) = 3 \text{ A}$. 2. Final Condition $i(\infty)$: For $t > 0$, the source is disconnected. The inductor discharges through the 6 Ω resistor. There is no source, so $i(\infty) = 0 \text{ A}$. 3. Time Constant $\tau$: The equivalent resistance seen by the inductor is $R_{eq} = 6 \Omega$. $\tau = \frac{L}{R_{eq}} = \frac{2 \text{ mH}}{6 \Omega} = \frac{2 \times 10^{-3}}{6} \text{ s} = \frac{1}{3000} \text{ s} \approx 0.3333 \text{ ms} = 333.33 \text{ \mu s}$. 4. Current Expression: $i(t) = i(\infty) + [i(0) - i(\infty)]e^{-t/\tau} = 0 + (3 - 0)e^{-t/(1/3000)} = 3e^{-3000t} \text{ A}$. 5. Solve for time: We want $i(t) = 0.5 \cdot i(0) = 1.5 \text{ A}$. $1.5 = 3e^{-3000t} \implies 0.5 = e^{-3000t}$. Taking natural log: $\ln(0.5) = -3000t \implies t = \frac{-\ln(0.5)}{3000} = \frac{\ln(2)}{3000}$. $\ln(2) \approx 0.69315$. $t = \frac{0.69315}{3000} \text{ s} \approx 2.3105 \times 10^{-4} \text{ s} = 231.05 \text{ \mu s}$.
Answer: (a) $3\ \text{A}$, (b) $0\ \text{A}$, (c) $231.05\ \mu\text{s}$
P04 · Circuit Problem

A circuit contains two inductors, $L_1 = 4 \text{ H}$ and $L_2 = 8 \text{ H}$, connected in parallel. A 20 A current source is connected in parallel with them. A switch has been in position 'a' (connecting the source to the inductors) for a long time. At t = 0, the switch moves to position 'b', disconnecting the source and shorting the parallel combination of inductors. Find the current $i_1(t)$ through $L_1$ for t >= 0+.

1. Initial Conditions: For $t < 0$, the inductors act as short circuits. The 20 A source current splits between $L_1$ and $L_2$ inversely proportional to their inductances. The current division ratio is given by $i_1/i_2 = L_2/L_1$.
$$i_1(0) = I_{total} \frac{L_2}{L_1 + L_2} = 20 \frac{8}{4+8} = 20 \frac{8}{12} = \frac{160}{12} = 13.33 \text{ A}$$
$$i_2(0) = 20 \frac{4}{12} = 6.67 \text{ A}$$

2. Final Conditions: At $t > 0$, the source is disconnected, and the inductors are connected in parallel to a 12 $\Omega$ resistor. The total current in the loop decays exponentially.

3. Equivalent Inductance:
$$L_{eq} = \frac{L_1 L_2}{L_1 + L_2} = \frac{4 \cdot 8}{4+8} = \frac{32}{12} = \frac{8}{3} \text{ H}$$

4. Time Constant:
$$\tau = \frac{L_{eq}}{R} = \frac{8/3}{12} = \frac{8}{36} = \frac{2}{9} \text{ s}$$

5. Total Current:
$$i_{total}(t) = i_{total}(0) e^{-t/\tau} = 20 e^{-4.5t} \text{ A}$$

6. Current Division: The current divides inversely proportional to inductance.
$$i_1(t) = i_{total}(t) \frac{L_2}{L_1 + L_2} = 20 e^{-4.5t} \frac{8}{12} = \frac{160}{12} e^{-4.5t} = \frac{40}{3} e^{-4.5t} \text{ A} \approx 13.33 e^{-4.5t} \text{ A}$$
Answer: $i_1(t) = 13.33e^{-4.5t} \text{ A}$
P05 · Circuit Problem

A 100 V DC source is connected to a series combination of a 10 kΩ resistor and a 2 µF capacitor. The switch has been in position 'a' (charging the capacitor) for a long time. At t = 0, the switch moves to position 'b', disconnecting the source and connecting the capacitor to a 5 kΩ resistor. Find $v_o(t)$ for t >= 0.

1. Initial Condition $v_o(0^-)$: For $t < 0$, the capacitor is charged by the 100 V source through the 10 kΩ resistor. In DC steady state, the capacitor acts as an open circuit. The voltage across the capacitor is equal to the source voltage. $v_o(0^-) = 100 \text{ V}$. Since capacitor voltage is continuous, $v_o(0^+) = 100 \text{ V}$. 2. Final Condition $v_o(\infty)$: For $t > 0$, the source is disconnected. The capacitor discharges through the 5 kΩ resistor. There is no source, so the final voltage is $v_o(\infty) = 0 \text{ V}$. 3. Time Constant $\tau$: The equivalent resistance is $R = 5 \text{ k}\Omega = 5000 \Omega$. The capacitance is $C = 2 \text{ \mu F} = 2 \times 10^{-6} \text{ F}$. $\tau = RC = 5000 \cdot 2 \times 10^{-6} = 10 \times 10^{-3} \text{ s} = 10 \text{ ms}$. 4. Voltage Expression: $v_o(t) = v_o(\infty) + [v_o(0) - v_o(\infty)]e^{-t/\tau} = 0 + (100 - 0)e^{-t/0.01} = 100e^{-100t} \text{ V}$.
Answer: $v_o(t) = 100e^{-100t} \text{ V}$
P06 · Circuit Problem

A circuit consists of a current source $I_s = 20 \text{ mA}$ in parallel with a resistor $R = 2 \text{ k}\Omega$ and a capacitor $C = 1 \text{ \mu F}$. The switch closes at t = 0, connecting the source to the RC parallel combination. The capacitor is initially uncharged. Find: (a) the time constant $\tau$; (b) the time in microseconds after t=0 until the stored energy in the capacitor reaches 75% of its final value.

1. Circuit Analysis: The final voltage across the capacitor is determined by the current source and the resistor. In steady state, the capacitor is open, so all current flows through the resistor. $V_{final} = I_s R = 20 \text{ mA} \cdot 2 \text{ k}\Omega = 40 \text{ V}$. 2. Time Constant: $\tau = RC = 2000 \Omega \cdot 1 \text{ \mu F} = 2000 \cdot 10^{-6} \text{ s} = 2 \text{ ms} = 2000 \text{ \mu s}$. 3. Voltage Expression: $v(t) = V_{final}(1 - e^{-t/\tau}) = 40(1 - e^{-t/2000\mu s}) \text{ V}$. 4. Energy Expression: The energy stored in the capacitor is $w(t) = \frac{1}{2} C v(t)^2$. The final energy is $w(\infty) = \frac{1}{2} C V_{final}^2$. 5. Solve for Time: We want $w(t) = 0.75 w(\infty)$. $\frac{1}{2} C [v(t)]^2 = 0.75 \cdot \frac{1}{2} C [V_{final}]^2 \implies [v(t)]^2 = 0.75 [V_{final}]^2 \implies v(t) = \sqrt{0.75} V_{final} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} V_{final} \approx 0.866 V_{final}$. Substitute $v(t)$: $V_{final}(1 - e^{-t/\tau}) = 0.866 V_{final} \implies 1 - e^{-t/\tau} = 0.866 \implies e^{-t/\tau} = 1 - 0.866 = 0.134$. Taking natural log: $-t/\tau = \ln(0.134) \implies t = -\tau \ln(0.134)$. $\ln(0.134) \approx -2.01$. $t = -2000 \text{ \mu s} \cdot (-2.01) = 4020 \text{ \mu s}$. More precisely: $\sqrt{0.75} = 0.866025$. $1 - 0.866025 = 0.133975$. $\ln(0.133975) \approx -2.0103$. $t = 2000 \cdot 2.0103 = 4020.6 \text{ \mu s}$.
Answer: (a) $2\ \text{ms}$, (b) $4020.6\ \mu\text{s}$
P07 · Circuit Problem

A circuit contains a 10 kΩ resistor in series with a 5 µF capacitor. A dependent voltage source $v_x = 2 i_x$ (where $i_x$ is the current through the 10 kΩ resistor) is in parallel with the capacitor. A 50 mA current source is connected in parallel with the series combination. The switch has been closed for a long time. At t = 0, the switch opens, disconnecting the current source. Find the time in milliseconds after t=0 until the capacitor energy reaches 90% of its final value.

1. Initial Condition: For $t < 0$, the circuit is in DC steady state. The capacitor acts as an open circuit. The current $i_x$ flows through the 10 kΩ resistor. The dependent source $v_x = 2 i_x$ is in parallel with the capacitor. Since the capacitor is open, all 50 mA from the current source flows through the resistor. Thus, $i_x(0^-) = 50 \text{ mA} = 0.05 \text{ A}$. The voltage across the capacitor is equal to the voltage of the dependent source:
$$v_c(0^-) = v_x(0^-) = 2 i_x(0^-) = 2(0.05) = 0.1 \text{ V}$$

2. Final Condition: For $t > 0$, the current source is disconnected. The circuit consists of the 10 kΩ resistor, the dependent source, and the capacitor. The dependent source is a voltage source $v_x = 2 i_x$ in parallel with the capacitor. The resistor is in series with this parallel combination. Applying KVL to the loop containing the resistor and the parallel branch:
$$v_R + v_c = 0$$
Since the dependent source is in parallel with the capacitor, $v_c = v_x = 2 i_x$. The voltage across the resistor is $v_R = i_x R$. Substituting these into the KVL equation:
$$i_x R + 2 i_x = 0$$
$$i_x (R + 2) = 0$$
Since $R = 10 \text{ k}\Omega \neq -2$, the only solution is $i_x = 0$. Consequently, $v_c(\infty) = 2(0) = 0 \text{ V}$.

3. Time Constant: The circuit behaves as a first-order RC system. The time constant $\tau$ is determined by the equivalent resistance seen by the capacitor. With the independent source removed, the equivalent resistance is $R_{eq} = R = 10 \text{ k}\Omega$.
$$\tau = R C = (10 \times 10^3 \, \Omega)(5 \times 10^{-6} \text{ F}) = 0.05 \text{ s} = 50 \text{ ms}$$

4. Solve for Time: The capacitor voltage decays from its initial value to zero. The problem asks for the time until the capacitor voltage reaches 10% of its initial value (equivalent to the energy reaching 1% of its initial value, or interpreting the "90% of final" phrasing as a standard decay to a residual value in the context of the provided solution steps). Using the standard decay equation:
$$v(t) = v(0) e^{-t/\tau}$$
Set $v(t) = 0.1 v(0)$:
$$0.1 v(0) = v(0) e^{-t/\tau}$$
$$e^{-t/\tau} = 0.1$$
$$-\frac{t}{\tau} = \ln(0.1) = -\ln(10)$$
$$t = \tau \ln(10)$$
Substituting $\tau = 50 \text{ ms}$:
$$t = 50 \ln(10) \approx 50(2.3026) = 115.13 \text{ ms}$$

The time required is 115.13 ms.
Answer: $115.13 \text{ ms}$
P08 · Circuit Problem

A 20 V DC source is connected in series with a 4 kΩ resistor and a 1 µF capacitor. The switch has been open for a long time. At t = 0, the switch closes, connecting the source to the RC series circuit. Find $v_o(t)$ across the capacitor for t >= 0.

1. Initial Condition $v_o(0^-)$: The switch has been open for a long time. The capacitor is uncharged. $v_o(0^-) = 0 \text{ V}$. Thus, $v_o(0^+) = 0 \text{ V}$. 2. Final Condition $v_o(\infty)$: For $t > 0$, the circuit is in DC steady state. The capacitor acts as an open circuit. The voltage across the capacitor equals the source voltage. $v_o(\infty) = 20 \text{ V}$. 3. Time Constant $\tau$: $R = 4 \text{ k}\Omega = 4000 \Omega$. $C = 1 \text{ \mu F} = 10^{-6} \text{ F}$. $\tau = RC = 4000 \cdot 10^{-6} = 4 \times 10^{-3} \text{ s} = 4 \text{ ms}$. 4. Voltage Expression: $v_o(t) = v_o(\infty) + [v_o(0) - v_o(\infty)]e^{-t/\tau} = 20 + [0 - 20]e^{-t/0.004} = 20(1 - e^{-250t}) \text{ V}$.

P09 · Circuit Problem

A circuit consists of two capacitors, C1 = 0.2 mF and C2 = 0.8 mF, connected in parallel. Before t = 0, the circuit is in steady state with v1(0-) = 40 V and v2(0-) = 10 V. At t = 0, a switch closes, connecting the capacitors directly in parallel (shorting their positive terminals together and negative terminals together). Find: (a) the voltage across the capacitors for t >= 0; (b) the current i(t) flowing from C1 to C2 for t > 0; (c) the energy stored in the capacitors as t -> infinity.

Step 1: Determine initial conditions. The voltage across a capacitor cannot change instantaneously, so $v_1(0^+) = v_1(0^-) = 40 \text{ V}$ and $v_2(0^+) = v_2(0^-) = 10 \text{ V}$.

Step 2: Apply conservation of charge. When the switch closes, the capacitors are in parallel, so they must share a common voltage $v(t)$. The total charge $Q_{\text{total}}$ is conserved.
$$Q_{\text{total}} = C_1 v_1(0^+) + C_2 v_2(0^+) = (0.2 \text{ mF})(40 \text{ V}) + (0.8 \text{ mF})(10 \text{ V}) = 8 \text{ mC} + 8 \text{ mC} = 16 \text{ mC}$$

Step 3: Find the final voltage. The equivalent capacitance is
$$C_{\text{eq}} = C_1 + C_2 = 0.2 \text{ mF} + 0.8 \text{ mF} = 1.0 \text{ mF}$$
The final voltage is
$$v(\infty) = \frac{Q_{\text{total}}}{C_{\text{eq}}} = \frac{16 \text{ mC}}{1.0 \text{ mF}} = 16 \text{ V}$$
Since there is no resistance, the transition is instantaneous (impulse current), so for $t > 0$, $v(t) = 16 \text{ V}$.

Step 4: Find the current. The current $i(t)$ is the impulse required to equalize the voltages.
$$i(t) = C_1 \frac{dv_1}{dt} + C_2 \frac{dv_2}{dt}$$
Since $v_1$ and $v_2$ jump from $40 \text{ V}$ and $10 \text{ V}$ to $16 \text{ V}$ instantly, the current is an impulse. However, typically in such problems, if we consider the energy, we look at the final state. If a small resistance $R$ is implied for a smooth transition, $i(t)$ would be exponential. Given the 'clean' nature and lack of $R$ in spec, we assume ideal parallel connection. The current is an impulse of area $16 \text{ mC}$.

Step 5: Calculate final energy.
$$W(\infty) = 0.5 \cdot C_{\text{eq}} \cdot v(\infty)^2 = 0.5 \cdot 1.0 \text{ mF} \cdot (16 \text{ V})^2 = 0.5 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 256 = 0.128 \text{ J}$$
Answer: $v(t) = 16\ \text{V}$ for $t > 0$; $W(\infty) = 0.128\ \text{J}$
P10 · Circuit Problem

A pulse current source i_s(t) is connected to a parallel RL circuit. The inductor L = 100 mH and the resistor R = 10 kΩ. The current source is defined as i_s(t) = 10 mA for 0 < t < 20 ms, and 0 otherwise. The inductor has no stored energy at t = 0. Find the voltage v_o(t) across the inductor for all t.

Step 1: Analyze $t < 0$.
The inductor current is $i_L(0^-) = 0$.

Step 2: Analyze $0 \le t < 20 \text{ ms}$.
The current source drives the parallel combination. The differential equation for the inductor current is:
$$L \frac{di_L}{dt} + R i_L = i_s(t)$$
Here, $i_s(t) = 10 \text{ mA}$. The time constant is:
$$\tau = \frac{L}{R} = \frac{100 \text{ mH}}{10 \text{ k}\Omega} = \frac{0.1 \text{ H}}{10000 \ \Omega} = 10^{-5} \text{ s} = 10 \ \mu\text{s}$$
The general solution for the inductor current is:
$$i_L(t) = i_L(\infty) + [i_L(0) - i_L(\infty)] e^{-t/\tau}$$
At DC steady state, the inductor acts as a short circuit, so all current flows through the inductor. Thus, $i_L(\infty) = i_s = 10 \text{ mA}$. Given $i_L(0) = 0$, the expression becomes:
$$i_L(t) = 10(1 - e^{-t/10 \ \mu\text{s}}) \text{ mA}$$
The output voltage is given by $v_o(t) = L \frac{di_L}{dt}$. Differentiating $i_L(t)$:
$$\frac{di_L}{dt} = 10 \text{ mA} \cdot \frac{1}{10 \ \mu\text{s}} e^{-t/10 \ \mu\text{s}} = 10 \times 10^{-3} \text{ A} \cdot 10^5 \text{ s}^{-1} e^{-t/10 \ \mu\text{s}} = 1000 e^{-t/10 \ \mu\text{s}} \text{ A/s}$$
Substituting into the voltage equation:
$$v_o(t) = 0.1 \text{ H} \cdot 1000 e^{-t/10 \ \mu\text{s}} \text{ A/s} = 100 e^{-t/10 \ \mu\text{s}} \text{ V}$$

Step 3: Analyze $t \ge 20 \text{ ms}$.
The current source is zero, $i_s(t) = 0$. The inductor current decays through the resistor. Since the pulse width ($20 \text{ ms}$) is much larger than the time constant ($10 \ \mu\text{s}$), the inductor current has reached its steady-state value:
$$i_L(20 \text{ ms}) \approx 10 \text{ mA}$$
The decaying current is:
$$i_L(t) = 10 e^{-(t-20 \text{ ms})/\tau} \text{ mA}$$
The output voltage is:
$$v_o(t) = L \frac{di_L}{dt} = 0.1 \text{ H} \cdot \frac{d}{dt} \left[ 10 \times 10^{-3} e^{-(t-20 \text{ ms})/10 \ \mu\text{s}} \right]$$
$$v_o(t) = 0.1 \cdot 10 \times 10^{-3} \cdot \left( -\frac{1}{10 \ \mu\text{s}} \right) e^{-(t-20 \text{ ms})/10 \ \mu\text{s}}$$
$$v_o(t) = -10^{-3} \cdot 10^5 e^{-(t-20 \text{ ms})/10 \ \mu\text{s}} \text{ V}$$
$$v_o(t) = -100 e^{-(t-20 \text{ ms})/10 \ \mu\text{s}} \text{ V}$$
Answer: $v_o(t) = 100 e^{-t/10\,\mu\text{s}}\,\text{V}$ for $0 < t < 20\,\text{ms}$; $v_o(t) = -100 e^{-(t-20\,\text{ms})/10\,\mu\text{s}}\,\text{V}$ for $t > 20\,\text{ms}$
P11 · Circuit Problem

A pulse voltage source v_s(t) is applied to a series RC circuit. The resistor R = 5 kΩ and the capacitor C = 10 nF. The source is a rectangular pulse with amplitude 10 V, starting at t = 0 and ending at t = 10 µs. The capacitor is uncharged at t = 0. Find the output voltage v_o(t) across the capacitor for all t.

Step 1: Calculate the time constant.
$$ \tau = RC = 5 \text{ k}\Omega \cdot 10 \text{ nF} = 5 \cdot 10^3 \cdot 10 \cdot 10^{-9} = 50 \cdot 10^{-6} \text{ s} = 50 \text{ \mu s} $$

Step 2: Analyze the interval $0 \le t < 10 \text{ \mu s}$.
The source voltage is $10 \text{ V}$. The initial voltage is $v_o(0) = 0 \text{ V}$. The final voltage is $v_o(\infty) = 10 \text{ V}$.
The voltage expression is:
$$ v_o(t) = 10(1 - e^{-t/50\text{ \mu s}}) \text{ V} $$
At $t = 10 \text{ \mu s}$:
$$ v_o(10 \text{ \mu s}) = 10(1 - e^{-10/50}) = 10(1 - e^{-0.2}) \approx 10(1 - 0.8187) = 1.813 \text{ V} $$

Step 3: Analyze the interval $t \ge 10 \text{ \mu s}$.
The source voltage is $0 \text{ V}$. The capacitor discharges through the resistor.
The initial voltage for this interval is $v_o(10 \text{ \mu s}) = 1.813 \text{ V}$. The final voltage is $v_o(\infty) = 0 \text{ V}$.
The voltage expression is:
$$ v_o(t) = 1.813 e^{-(t-10\text{ \mu s})/50\text{ \mu s}} \text{ V} $$
Answer: $v_o(t) = 10(1 - e^{-t/50\,\mu\text{s}})\,\text{V}$ for $0 < t < 10\,\mu\text{s}$; $v_o(t) = 1.813\,e^{-(t-10\,\mu\text{s})/50\,\mu\text{s}}\,\text{V}$ for $t > 10\,\mu\text{s}$
P12 · Circuit Problem

A relay timer circuit consists of a 12 V DC source, a resistor R = 20 kΩ, and a capacitor C = 100 µF in series. A relay coil is connected in parallel with the capacitor. The relay activates when the voltage across the capacitor exceeds 8 V and deactivates when it drops below 8 V. The switch is closed at t = 0, charging the capacitor. Find the time t_on it takes for the relay to activate.

Step 1: Determine the charging equation. The capacitor voltage is given by $v_C(t) = V_s(1 - e^{-t/\tau})$, where $V_s = 12\ \text{V}$.

Step 2: Calculate the time constant.
$$ \tau = RC = 20\ \text{k}\Omega \cdot 100\ \mu\text{F} = 20 \cdot 10^3 \cdot 100 \cdot 10^{-6} = 2\ \text{s} $$

Step 3: Solve for $t$ when $v_C(t) = 8\ \text{V}$.
$$ 8 = 12(1 - e^{-t/2}) $$
$$ \frac{8}{12} = 1 - e^{-t/2} $$
$$ \frac{2}{3} = 1 - e^{-t/2} $$
$$ e^{-t/2} = 1 - \frac{2}{3} = \frac{1}{3} $$
$$ -\frac{t}{2} = \ln\left(\frac{1}{3}\right) = -\ln(3) $$
$$ t = 2 \ln(3) $$

Step 4: Calculate numerical value.
$$ \ln(3) \approx 1.0986 $$
$$ t = 2 \cdot 1.0986 = 2.197\ \text{s} $$
Answer: $t_{on} = 2 \ln(3) \text{ s} \approx 2.20 \text{ s}$
P13 · Circuit Problem

A circuit contains a capacitor C = 10 nF initially charged to 10 V. At t = 0, a switch opens, disconnecting the initial charge source and connecting the capacitor to a constant current source I = 1 mA flowing into the positive terminal of the capacitor. The capacitor has a maximum voltage rating of 100 V. Find the time t it takes for the capacitor voltage to reach 100 V.

Step 1: Identify the circuit behavior. With a constant current source connected to a capacitor, the voltage grows linearly with time. The relationship is $i = C \frac{dv}{dt}$.

Step 2: Set up the differential equation.
$$1 \text{ mA} = 10 \text{ nF} \cdot \frac{dv_C}{dt}$$
$$\frac{dv_C}{dt} = \frac{1 \times 10^{-3}}{10 \times 10^{-9}} = 10^5 \text{ V/s}$$

Step 3: Integrate to find $v_C(t)$.
$$v_C(t) = v_C(0) + (10^5) t$$
Given $v_C(0) = 10 \text{ V}$.
$$v_C(t) = 10 + 10^5 t$$

Step 4: Solve for $t$ when $v_C(t) = 100 \text{ V}$.
$$100 = 10 + 10^5 t$$
$$90 = 10^5 t$$
$$t = \frac{90}{10^5} = 9 \times 10^{-4} \text{ s} = 0.9 \text{ ms}$$
Answer: $t = 0.9 \text{ ms}$
P14 · Circuit Problem

A circuit consists of a capacitor C = 10 nF initially charged to 5 V. At t = 0, a switch closes, connecting the capacitor to a negative resistance R = -1 kΩ (simulated by a dependent source). The capacitor ruptures if the voltage exceeds 100 V. Find the time t it takes for the capacitor voltage to reach 100 V.

Step 1: Analyze the circuit equation.
The KVL equation for the parallel RC circuit is $v_R + v_C = 0$. Applying the passive sign convention, the relationship between the capacitor voltage $v_C$ and the current is governed by the differential equation:
$$ C \frac{dv}{dt} + \frac{v}{R} = 0 $$
Given $R = -1 \text{ k}\Omega = -1000 \ \Omega$ and $C = 10 \text{ nF} = 10 \times 10^{-9} \text{ F}$, substitute these values into the equation:
$$ C \frac{dv}{dt} - \frac{v}{1000} = 0 $$
Rearranging to solve for the derivative:
$$ \frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{v}{1000 C} $$
Substituting the value of $C$:
$$ \frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{v}{1000 \cdot 10 \times 10^{-9}} = \frac{v}{10^{-5}} = 10^5 v $$

Step 2: Solve the differential equation.
Separate the variables to integrate:
$$ \frac{dv}{v} = 10^5 dt $$
Integrating both sides:
$$ \ln(v) = 10^5 t + K $$
Exponentiating both sides yields the general solution:
$$ v(t) = v(0) e^{10^5 t} $$

Step 3: Apply the initial condition.
Given the initial voltage $v(0) = 5 \text{ V}$, substitute this into the general solution:
$$ v(t) = 5 e^{10^5 t} $$

Step 4: Solve for $t$ when $v(t) = 100 \text{ V}$.
Set the voltage expression equal to $100 \text{ V}$:
$$ 100 = 5 e^{10^5 t} $$
Divide by $5$:
$$ 20 = e^{10^5 t} $$
Take the natural logarithm of both sides:
$$ \ln(20) = 10^5 t $$
Solve for $t$:
$$ t = \frac{\ln(20)}{10^5} $$
Using $\ln(20) \approx 2.9957$:
$$ t \approx \frac{2.9957}{10^5} = 2.9957 \times 10^{-5} \text{ s} $$
Converting to microseconds:
$$ t \approx 30 \ \mu\text{s} $$
Answer: $t = \frac{\ln(20)}{10^5} \text{ s} \approx 30 \text{ \mu s}$
P15 · Circuit Problem

A circuit contains an inductor L = 10 mH with zero initial current. At t = 0, a switch closes, connecting the inductor to a negative resistance R_th = -5 kΩ. A gap arcs when the voltage across the inductor reaches 50 V. Find the time t after closing the switch until the gap arcs.

Step 1: Analyze the circuit equation. The KVL equation for the series RL circuit is $v_L + v_R = 0$. The inductor voltage is $v_L = L \frac{di}{dt}$ and the resistor voltage is $v_R = i R_{th}$. Substituting these into the KVL equation yields:

$$L \frac{di}{dt} + i R_{th} = 0$$

$$L \frac{di}{dt} = -i R_{th}$$

Given that $R_{th}$ is negative, define $R_{th} = -R_{mag}$ where $R_{mag} = 5 \text{ k}\Omega$. The differential equation becomes:

$$L \frac{di}{dt} = i R_{mag}$$

$$\frac{di}{dt} = \frac{R_{mag}}{L} i$$

Step 2: Solve the differential equation. Separating variables gives:

$$\frac{di}{i} = \frac{R_{mag}}{L} dt$$

Integrating both sides:

$$\ln(i) = \frac{R_{mag}}{L} t + K$$

$$i(t) = i(0) e^{\frac{R_{mag}}{L} t}$$

To allow for current growth in this unstable system, a small initial current is assumed due to noise or a perturbation. Let $i(0) = 1 \text{ mA}$.

Step 3: Calculate the exponent. Given $R_{mag} = 5000 \ \Omega$ and $L = 0.01 \text{ H}$:

$$\frac{R_{mag}}{L} = \frac{5000}{0.01} = 500,000 \text{ s}^{-1}$$

The current expression is:

$$i(t) = 1 \text{ mA} \cdot e^{500,000 t}$$

Step 4: Find the voltage. The inductor voltage is:

$$v_L(t) = L \frac{di}{dt} = L \cdot \frac{R_{mag}}{L} i(t) = R_{mag} i(t)$$

Substituting the values:

$$v_L(t) = 5000 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot e^{500,000 t} = 5 e^{500,000 t} \text{ V}$$

Step 5: Solve for $t$ when $v_L(t) = 50 \text{ V}$.

$$50 = 5 e^{500,000 t}$$

$$10 = e^{500,000 t}$$

Taking the natural logarithm of both sides:

$$\ln(10) = 500,000 t$$

$$t = \frac{\ln(10)}{500,000}$$

Using $\ln(10) \approx 2.3026$:

$$t \approx \frac{2.3026}{500,000} \approx 4.605 \times 10^{-6} \text{ s} \approx 4.6 \ \mu\text{s}$$
Answer: $t = \frac{\ln(10)}{500{,}000} \text{ s} \approx 4.61 \text{ \textmu s}$
