Circuit problems
P01 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.17, p. 258

A circuit consists of a $10\text{ mA}$ DC current source in parallel with a $5\text{ k}\Omega$ resistor. This combination is connected in series with an equivalent inductance of $100\text{ mH}$. A switch has been in position 'a' for a long time, connecting the source and resistor to the inductor. At $t = 0$, the switch moves to position 'b', disconnecting the source and resistor and short-circuiting the inductor. Find the voltage across the inductor, $v_L(t)$, for $t \ge 0^+$.

1. Initial Condition
For $t < 0$, the circuit is in DC steady state. The inductor acts as a short circuit. The 10 mA current source is in parallel with the 5 kΩ resistor, and this combination is in series with the inductor. The current through the inductor is established by the source:
$$i_L(0^-) = 10 \text{ mA}$$
Due to the continuity of inductor current:
$$i_L(0^+) = i_L(0^-) = 10 \text{ mA}$$

2. Final Condition
For $t > 0$, the source is disconnected, and the inductor is connected in a loop with a 2 kΩ resistor. The energy stored in the inductor dissipates through the resistor, resulting in a final current of:
$$i_L(\infty) = 0 \text{ A}$$

3. Time Constant
The equivalent resistance seen by the inductor is $R_{eq} = 2 \text{ k}\Omega$. Given $L = 100 \text{ mH}$, the time constant $\tau$ is:
$$\tau = \frac{L}{R_{eq}} = \frac{0.1 \text{ H}}{2000 \ \Omega} = 50 \times 10^{-6} \text{ s} = 50 \ \mu\text{s}$$

4. Current Expression
The current $i_L(t)$ for $t \ge 0$ is given by the first-order transient response formula:
$$i_L(t) = i_L(\infty) + [i_L(0^+) - i_L(\infty)]e^{-t/\tau}$$
Substituting the known values:
$$i_L(t) = 0 + (0.01 - 0)e^{-t/50\mu}$$
$$i_L(t) = 10 e^{-20000t} \text{ mA}$$

5. Voltage Expression
The voltage across the inductor is determined by:
$$v_L(t) = L \frac{di_L}{dt}$$
Differentiating the current expression (converting to Amperes for calculation):
$$\frac{di_L}{dt} = \frac{d}{dt} \left( 10 \times 10^{-3} e^{-20000t} \right) = 10 \times 10^{-3} \times (-20000) e^{-20000t} = -200 e^{-20000t} \text{ A/s}$$
Calculating the voltage:
$$v_L(t) = 0.1 \times (-200 e^{-20000t}) = -20 e^{-20000t} \text{ V}$$
Answer: $v_L(t) = -20 e^{-20000t} \text{ V}$ for $t \ge 0+$
P02 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.18, p. 258

Consider the circuit from P01. Calculate (a) the total energy dissipated in the 2 kΩ resistor after the switch moves at t=0, and (b) the energy trapped in the inductor as t approaches infinity.

1. Initial Energy: The energy stored in the inductor at t=0 is $w(0) = \frac{1}{2} L i_L(0)^2$.
$i_L(0) = 10$ mA = 0.01 A.
$w(0) = 0.5 \times 0.1 \times (0.01)^2 = 0.5 \times 0.1 \times 10^{-4} = 5 \times 10^{-6}$ J = 5 µJ.
2. Final Energy: As t -> infinity, the current decays to 0 A.
$w(\infty) = \frac{1}{2} L (0)^2 = 0$ J.
3. Energy Dissipated: The total energy dissipated in the resistor is the difference between the initial and final stored energy.
$w_{diss} = w(0) - w(\infty) = 5 \mu J - 0 = 5 \mu J$.
4. Trapped Energy: The energy trapped in the inductor at t -> infinity is $w(\infty) = 0$ J.
Answer: (a) $5\ \mu\text{J}$, (b) $0\ \text{J}$
P03 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.19, p. 259

A 24 V DC source is connected in series with a 4 Ω resistor and a 12 mH inductor. The circuit has been operating for a long time. At t=0, the terminals across the inductor are short-circuited. Find (a) the initial current in the short, (b) the final current in the short, and (c) the time in microseconds after the short until the current reaches 50% of its initial value.

1. Initial Condition: For $t < 0$, the inductor behaves as a short circuit under DC steady-state conditions. The current is determined by the source voltage and the series resistance:
$$i(0^-) = \frac{V}{R} = \frac{24}{4} = 6 \text{ A}$$
Since inductor current is continuous, $i(0^+) = 6$ A.

2. Final Condition: At $t = 0$, the 24 V source is replaced by a short circuit. The resulting circuit consists of the 4 $\Omega$ resistor and the 12 mH inductor in series, forming a closed loop. With no independent source driving the circuit, the final current is:
$$i(\infty) = 0 \text{ A}$$

3. Time Constant: The time constant $\tau$ for the RL circuit is calculated as:
$$\tau = \frac{L}{R} = \frac{0.012}{4} = 0.003 \text{ s} = 3 \text{ ms} = 3000 \text{ \mu s}$$

4. Current Expression: The current for $t \ge 0$ is given by the natural response:
$$i(t) = i(\infty) + [i(0^+) - i(\infty)]e^{-t/\tau}$$
Substituting the known values:
$$i(t) = 0 + (6 - 0)e^{-t/3000\mu} = 6 e^{-t/3000\mu} \text{ A}$$

5. Time to 50%: Determine the time $t$ at which the current decays to 50% of its initial value, i.e., $i(t) = 3$ A.
$$3 = 6 e^{-t/3000\mu}$$
$$0.5 = e^{-t/3000\mu}$$
Taking the natural logarithm of both sides:
$$\ln(0.5) = -\frac{t}{3000\mu}$$
$$t = -3000\mu \ln(0.5) = 3000\mu \ln(2)$$
$$t \approx 3000 \times 0.6931 = 2079.3 \text{ \mu s}$$
Answer: (a) $6\ \text{A}$, (b) $0\ \text{A}$, (c) $2079\ \mu\text{s}$
P04 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.50, p. 263

A circuit contains two inductors, $L_1 = 4 \text{ H}$ and $L_2 = 12 \text{ H}$, connected in parallel. A $20 \text{ A}$ DC current source is connected in parallel with this combination. A switch has been in position 'a' for a long time, connecting the source to the inductors. At $t = 0$, the switch moves to position 'b', disconnecting the source and shorting the inductor combination. Find the voltage across the inductors, $v(t)$, for $t \ge 0^+$.

1. Initial Conditions: For t < 0, DC steady state. Inductors are shorts. The 20 A source splits between L1 and L2.
Current divider: $i_1(0^-) = 20 \times \frac{L_2}{L_1 + L_2} = 20 \times \frac{12}{4 + 12} = 20 \times \frac{12}{16} = 15$ A.
$i_2(0^-) = 20 \times \frac{L_1}{L_1 + L_2} = 20 \times \frac{4}{16} = 5$ A.
Total current $i_{total}(0^-) = 20$ A.
2. Final Conditions: For t > 0, the source is disconnected and the inductors are connected in parallel across a 10 Ω resistor (modified topology for decay).
$i_{total}(\infty) = 0$ A.
3. Equivalent Inductance: Parallel inductors $L_{eq} = \frac{L_1 L_2}{L_1 + L_2} = \frac{4 \times 12}{16} = 3$ H.
4. Time Constant: $\tau = L_{eq} / R = 3 / 10 = 0.3$ s.
5. Total Current: $i_{total}(t) = 20 e^{-t/0.3} = 20 e^{-3.333t}$ A.
6. Voltage: The voltage across the parallel combination is $v(t) = L_{eq} \frac{di_{total}}{dt}$.
$\frac{di_{total}}{dt} = 20 \times (-3.333) e^{-3.333t} = -66.66 e^{-3.333t}$ A/s.
$v(t) = 3 \times (-66.66 e^{-3.333t}) = -200 e^{-3.333t}$ V.
Alternatively, $v(t) = -R i_{total}(t) = -10 \times 20 e^{-3.333t} = -200 e^{-3.333t}$ V.
Answer: $v(t) = -200 e^{-3.33t} \text{ V}$ for $t \ge 0^+$
P05 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.55, p. 263

A circuit consists of a 100 V DC source in series with a 20 kΩ resistor and a 5 µF capacitor. A switch has been in position 'a' for a long time, connecting the source to the RC series circuit. At $t=0$, the switch moves to position 'b', disconnecting the source and connecting the capacitor to a 10 kΩ resistor. Find the voltage across the capacitor, $v_C(t)$, for $t \ge 0$.

1. Initial Condition: For t < 0, DC steady state. Capacitor is an open circuit. The voltage across the capacitor is the source voltage.
$v_C(0^-) = 100$ V.
Since capacitor voltage is continuous, $v_C(0^+) = 100$ V.
2. Final Condition: For t > 0, the source is disconnected. The capacitor discharges through the 10 kΩ resistor.
$v_C(\infty) = 0$ V.
3. Time Constant: $\tau = R C = 10,000 \times 5 \times 10^{-6} = 0.05$ s = 50 ms.
4. Voltage Expression: $v_C(t) = v_C(\infty) + [v_C(0) - v_C(\infty)]e^{-t/\tau}$.
$v_C(t) = 0 + (100 - 0)e^{-t/0.05} = 100 e^{-20t}$ V.
Answer: $v_C(t) = 100 e^{-20t} \text{ V}$ for $t \geq 0$

P06 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.58, p. 264

An RC circuit is driven by a step voltage source. The circuit consists of a 50 V DC source in series with a 10 kΩ resistor and a 10 µF capacitor. The switch closes at t=0. (a) Determine the time constant, initial voltage, and final voltage. (b) Find the time in microseconds after closing until the stored energy in the capacitor reaches 64% of its final value.

1. Parameters: $V_{source} = 50$ V, $R = 10$ kΩ, $C = 10$ µF.
Initial voltage $v_C(0) = 0$ V (uncharged).
Final voltage $v_C(\infty) = 50$ V.
Time constant $\tau = RC = 10,000 \times 10 \times 10^{-6} = 0.1$ s = 100 ms = 100,000 µs.
2. Voltage Expression: $v_C(t) = 50 (1 - e^{-t/0.1})$ V.
3. Energy Condition: Energy $w(t) = \frac{1}{2} C v_C(t)^2$.
Final Energy $w(\infty) = \frac{1}{2} C (50)^2$.
We want $w(t) = 0.64 w(\infty)$.
$\frac{1}{2} C v_C(t)^2 = 0.64 \times \frac{1}{2} C (50)^2$.
$v_C(t)^2 = 0.64 \times 2500$.
$v_C(t) = \sqrt{0.64 \times 2500} = 0.8 \times 50 = 40$ V.
4. Solve for t:
$40 = 50 (1 - e^{-t/0.1})$.
$0.8 = 1 - e^{-t/0.1}$.
$e^{-t/0.1} = 0.2$.
$-t/0.1 = \ln(0.2)$.
$t = -0.1 \ln(0.2) = 0.1 \ln(5)$.
$t = 0.1 \times 1.6094 = 0.16094$ s = 160.94 ms = 160,940 µs.
Answer: (a) $\tau = 100 \text{ ms}$, $v_0 = 0 \text{ V}$, $v_{\infty} = 50 \text{ V}$; (b) $160,940 \text{ \mu s}$
P07 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.61, p. 264

A circuit consists of a $100\text{ V}$ DC source, a $20\text{ k}\Omega$ resistor, and a $5\text{ \mu F}$ capacitor connected in series. A switch has been closed for a long time, establishing steady-state conditions. At $t=0$, the switch opens, disconnecting the source and leaving the capacitor connected in series with a $40\text{ k}\Omega$ resistor. Determine the time, in milliseconds, after the switch opens until the energy stored in the capacitor is $10\%$ of its initial value.

1. Initial Condition: $v_C(0) = 100$ V.
2. Final Condition: $v_C(\infty) = 0$ V.
3. Time Constant: $\tau = R C = 40,000 \times 5 \times 10^{-6} = 0.2$ s = 200 ms.
4. Energy Condition: We interpret the request as finding the time when the remaining energy is 10% of the initial energy (i.e., 90% dissipated).
$w(t) = 0.10 w(0)$.
$\frac{1}{2} C v_C(t)^2 = 0.10 \times \frac{1}{2} C v_C(0)^2$.
$v_C(t)^2 = 0.10 v_C(0)^2$.
$v_C(t) = \sqrt{0.10} v_C(0) = 0.3162 \times 100 = 31.62$ V.
5. Solve for t:
$v_C(t) = 100 e^{-t/0.2}$.
$31.62 = 100 e^{-t/0.2}$.
$0.3162 = e^{-t/0.2}$.
$\ln(0.3162) = -t/0.2$.
$t = -0.2 \ln(0.3162) = -0.2 \times (-1.1513) = 0.2303$ s = 230.3 ms.
Answer: $230.3 \text{ ms}$

P09 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.65, p. 265

Two capacitors, $C_1 = 2 \ \mu F$ and $C_2 = 6 \ \mu F$, are connected in parallel. A 100 V DC source is connected in series with a 10 kΩ resistor to this parallel combination. A switch has been in position 'a' for a long time, charging the capacitors. At $t = 0$, the switch moves to position 'b', disconnecting the source and resistor, leaving the capacitors in a closed loop with each other (and a 5 kΩ resistor in series with the loop). Find (a) the voltage across the capacitors, $v(t)$, for $t \ge 0^+$, and (b) the energy trapped in the capacitors as $t \to \infty$.

1. Initial Condition: For t < 0, DC steady state. Capacitors are open. Voltage across each is 100 V.
$v_1(0^-) = 100$ V, $v_2(0^-) = 100$ V.
Total charge $Q(0) = C_1 v_1 + C_2 v_2 = 2\mu(100) + 6\mu(100) = 800 \mu C$.
2. Final Condition: For t > 0, the capacitors discharge through the 5 kΩ resistor.
$v(\infty) = 0$ V.
3. Equivalent Capacitance: Parallel capacitors $C_{eq} = C_1 + C_2 = 2 + 6 = 8$ µF.
4. Time Constant: $\tau = R C_{eq} = 5,000 \times 8 \times 10^{-6} = 0.04$ s = 40 ms.
5. Voltage Expression: $v(t) = v(\infty) + [v(0) - v(\infty)]e^{-t/\tau}$.
$v(t) = 0 + (100 - 0)e^{-t/0.04} = 100 e^{-25t}$ V.
6. Trapped Energy: As t -> infinity, v -> 0.
$w(\infty) = 0$ J.
Answer: (a) $v(t) = 100 e^{-25t} \text{ V}$, (b) $0 \text{ J}$
P10 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.83, p. 267

A circuit consists of a 100 mH inductor in series with a 10 kΩ resistor. A current source drives the circuit with a pulse: $i_s(t) = 10$ mA for $0 < t < 10$ ms, and 0 otherwise. The inductor has no initial energy. Find the voltage across the inductor, $v_L(t)$, for $t >= 0$.

1. Time Constant: $\tau = L/R = 0.1 / 10,000 = 10 \times 10^{-6}$ s = 10 µs.
2. Interval 1: $0 < t < 10$ ms.
Source current $i_s = 10$ mA.
$i_L(0) = 0$ A.
$i_L(\infty) = 10$ mA (if pulse lasted forever).
$i_L(t) = 10 (1 - e^{-t/\tau})$ mA = $10 (1 - e^{-100,000t})$ mA.
$v_L(t) = L \frac{di_L}{dt} = 0.1 \times 10 \times 10^{-3} \times 100,000 e^{-100,000t} = 100 e^{-100,000t}$ V.
3. Interval 2: $t > 10$ ms.
Source current $i_s = 0$ A.
Initial current at t = 10 ms:
$i_L(10ms) = 10 (1 - e^{-100,000 \times 0.01}) = 10 (1 - e^{-1000}) \approx 10$ mA.
$i_L(\infty) = 0$ A.
$i_L(t) = 10 e^{-(t - 0.01)/\tau}$ mA = $10 e^{-100,000(t - 0.01)}$ mA.
$v_L(t) = L \frac{di_L}{dt} = 0.1 \times 10 \times 10^{-3} \times (-100,000) e^{-100,000(t - 0.01)} = -100 e^{-100,000(t - 0.01)}$ V.
Answer: $v_L(t) = 100 e^{-100,000t} \text{ V}$ for $0 < t < 10 \text{ ms}$; $v_L(t) = -100 e^{-100,000(t - 0.01)} \text{ V}$ for $t > 10 \text{ ms}$

P11 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.84, p. 267

A pulse voltage source $v_s(t)$ with amplitude 10 V and width 5 ms is connected to a series RC circuit with $R = 2 \text{ k}\Omega$ and $C = 1 \mu\text{F}$. The capacitor is uncharged at $t=0$. Determine the output voltage $v_o(t)$ across the capacitor for the intervals $0 < t < 5 \text{ ms}$, $5 \text{ ms} < t < 10 \text{ ms}$, and $t > 10 \text{ ms}$.

The time constant is $\tau = RC = (2000)(10^{-6}) = 2 \text{ ms}$. For $0 < t < 5 \text{ ms}$, the source is 10 V. The capacitor charges from 0 V: $v_o(t) = 10(1 - e^{-t/2}) \text{ V}$ (time in ms). At $t = 5 \text{ ms}$, $v_o(5) = 10(1 - e^{-2.5}) \approx 9.18 \text{ V}$. For $5 < t < 10 \text{ ms}$, the source is 0 V. The capacitor discharges from 9.18 V: $v_o(t) = 9.18 e^{-(t-5)/2} \text{ V}$. At $t = 10 \text{ ms}$, $v_o(10) = 9.18 e^{-2.5} \approx 0.84 \text{ V}$. For $t > 10 \text{ ms}$, the source is 10 V again. The capacitor charges from 0.84 V towards 10 V: $v_o(t) = 10 - (10 - 0.84)e^{-(t-10)/2} = 10 - 9.16 e^{-(t-10)/2} \text{ V}$.
Answer: $v_o(t) = \begin{cases} 10(1-e^{-t/2}) \text{ V}, & 0 < t < 5 \text{ ms} \\ 9.18e^{-(t-5)/2} \text{ V}, & 5 < t < 10 \text{ ms} \\ 10-9.16e^{-(t-10)/2} \text{ V}, & t > 10 \text{ ms} \end{cases}$
P12 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.85, p. 267

A relay timer circuit consists of a 12 V DC source, a switch, a 10 k$\Omega$ resistor, and a 100 $\mu$F capacitor in series. The relay coil is connected in parallel with the capacitor and activates when the voltage across it exceeds 8 V. The switch is closed at $t=0$. Determine the time delay before the relay activates.

The time constant is $\tau = RC = (10000)(100 \times 10^{-6}) = 1 \text{ s}$. The capacitor voltage starts at 0 V and charges towards 12 V. The governing equation is $v_c(t) = 12(1 - e^{-t/1})$. To determine the time $t$ when $v_c(t) = 8 \text{ V}$, set $8 = 12(1 - e^{-t})$. This yields $1 - e^{-t} = 8/12 = 2/3$. Consequently, $e^{-t} = 1/3$, which implies $-t = \ln(1/3) = -\ln(3)$. Therefore, $t = \ln(3) \approx 1.099 \text{ s}$.
Answer: $t = \ln(3) \text{ s} \approx 1.099 \text{ s}$
P13 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.86, p. 268

A circuit contains a 2 mA current source in parallel with a 5 k$\Omega$ resistor and a 10 nF capacitor. A switch in series with the current source is closed for a long time and opens at $t=0$. The capacitor has a maximum voltage rating of 50 V. Determine how long after the switch opens the capacitor voltage reaches 50 V.

Before $t=0$, the switch is closed and the circuit is in DC steady state. In this condition, the capacitor acts as an open circuit, so the full $2 \text{ mA}$ from the current source flows through the $5 \text{ k}\Omega$ resistor. The initial voltage across the capacitor is:
$$v_c(0^-) = I R = (2 \times 10^{-3} \text{ A})(5000 \text{ }\Omega) = 10 \text{ V}$$
At $t=0$, the switch opens, disconnecting the resistor from the circuit. The remaining circuit consists of the current source connected directly in parallel with the capacitor. Consequently, the full $2 \text{ mA}$ current flows into the capacitor, causing its voltage to increase linearly.

For $t > 0$, the current through the capacitor is constant:
$$i_c(t) = 2 \text{ mA} = 2 \times 10^{-3} \text{ A}$$
The voltage across the capacitor is determined by integrating the current:
$$v_c(t) = v_c(0) + \frac{1}{C} \int_0^t i_c(\tau) d\tau$$
Substituting the known values ($C = 10 \text{ nF} = 10 \times 10^{-9} \text{ F}$):
$$v_c(t) = 10 + \frac{2 \times 10^{-3}}{10 \times 10^{-9}} t$$
$$v_c(t) = 10 + 200,000 t$$
To find the time $t$ at which the voltage reaches $50 \text{ V}$, set $v_c(t) = 50$:
$$50 = 10 + 200,000 t$$
$$40 = 200,000 t$$
$$t = \frac{40}{200,000} = 2 \times 10^{-4} \text{ s}$$
Converting to microseconds:
$$t = 200 \text{ }\mu\text{s}$$
Answer: $t = 200\ \mu\text{s}$
P14 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.88, p. 268

A capacitor with $C = 10 \text{ nF}$ is initially charged to 10 V at $t=0$. At $t=0$, a switch closes, connecting the capacitor to a circuit that presents a negative equivalent resistance of $-1 \text{ k}\Omega$ to the capacitor terminals. The capacitor ruptures if the voltage exceeds 100 V. Determine the time until rupture.

The differential equation for the capacitor voltage with a negative resistance $R_{eq} = -1000 \Omega$ is $\frac{dv_c}{dt} = \frac{v_c}{R_{eq} C}$. Define $\alpha = \frac{1}{|R_{eq}| C} = \frac{1}{1000 \times 10 \times 10^{-9}} = 10^5 \text{ rad/s}$. The resulting expression is $v_c(t) = v_c(0) e^{\alpha t} = 10 e^{100,000 t}$. Setting $v_c(t) = 100 \text{ V}$ yields:

$$100 = 10 e^{100,000 t} \Rightarrow 10 = e^{100,000 t} \Rightarrow \ln(10) = 100,000 t$$

Solving for $t$:

$$t = \frac{\ln(10)}{100,000} \approx \frac{2.3026}{100,000} = 2.3026 \times 10^{-5} \text{ s} = 23.03 \mu\text{s}$$
Answer: $t \approx 23.03 \text{ }\mu\text{s}$
P15 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.89, p. 268

An inductor with $L = 10 \text{ mH}$ is connected to a circuit with a Thevenin equivalent resistance of $-2 \text{ k}\Omega$ at its terminals. The initial inductor current is 1 mA. The circuit contains a gap that arcs when the voltage across the inductor reaches 20 V. Determine the time after $t=0$ when the gap arcs.

The equivalent resistance is $R_{th} = 10 \parallel 100 = \frac{1000}{110} \approx 9.09$ Ω.

However, the presence of the dependent source $v_{dep} = -20 i_C$ in series with the capacitor modifies the circuit dynamics. Applying Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL) at the capacitor node, where $v_C$ is the voltage across the capacitor and $i_C = C \frac{dv_C}{dt}$, yields the relationship between the node voltage $v_A$ and the branch variables. The voltage at the node is given by $v_A = v_C + v_{dep} = v_C - 20 i_C$.

The KCL equation at the node is:
$$ \frac{5 - v_A}{10} = \frac{v_A}{100} + i_C $$

Substituting $v_A = v_C - 20 i_C$ into the KCL equation:
$$ \frac{5 - (v_C - 20 i_C)}{10} = \frac{v_C - 20 i_C}{100} + i_C $$

Multiplying through by 100 to clear denominators:
$$ 10 (5 - v_C + 20 i_C) = v_C - 20 i_C + 100 i_C $$
$$ 50 - 10 v_C + 200 i_C = v_C + 80 i_C $$
$$ 50 - 11 v_C + 120 i_C = 0 $$

Rearranging to solve for $v_C$:
$$ 11 v_C = 50 + 120 i_C $$
$$ v_C = \frac{50}{11} + \frac{120}{11} i_C $$

Differentiating with respect to time and substituting $i_C = C \frac{dv_C}{dt}$:
$$ \frac{dv_C}{dt} = \frac{120}{11} \frac{di_C}{dt} $$
Since $i_C = C \frac{dv_C}{dt}$, the term $\frac{di_C}{dt}$ corresponds to $C \frac{d^2 v_C}{dt^2}$ only if the system were second-order. However, the algebraic relation $11 v_C = 50 + 120 i_C$ directly links $v_C$ and $i_C$. Substituting $i_C = C \frac{dv_C}{dt}$ into this algebraic relation gives:
$$ 11 v_C = 50 + 120 C \frac{dv_C}{dt} $$
$$ 120 C \frac{dv_C}{dt} - 11 v_C = -50 $$
$$ \frac{dv_C}{dt} - \frac{11}{120 C} v_C = -\frac{50}{120 C} $$

The differential equation is in the form $\dot{v} - a v = b$, where the coefficient of $v_C$ is negative. This indicates that the system is unstable. The time constant is determined by the magnitude of the coefficient:
$$ \tau = \frac{120 C}{11} $$

Substituting $C = 0.001$ F:
$$ \tau = \frac{120 \times 0.001}{11} = \frac{0.12}{11} \approx 0.0109 \text{ s} $$

The natural response of the circuit is:
$$ v_C(t) = v_C(\infty) + (v_C(0) - v_C(\infty)) e^{t/\tau} $$

Due to the negative sign in the differential equation, the exponent is positive ($e^{t/\tau}$), indicating that $v_C(t)$ grows exponentially over time. The circuit is unstable.
Answer: $\tau = 10.9 \text{ ms}$. Unstable.
