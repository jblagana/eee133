P01 · Circuit Problem

Consider a series circuit consisting of a 12 V DC source, a 4 Ω resistor, and a 0.1 H inductor. A switch in series with the source has been closed for a long time, so the inductor current is steady. At t = 0, the switch opens, disconnecting the source and leaving the inductor in a closed loop with a 20 Ω resistor. (a) Find the voltage v_o(t) across the 20 Ω resistor for t >= 0+. (b) Determine the percentage of the initial energy stored in the inductor that is dissipated by the 20 Ω resistor after one time constant has elapsed.

For $t < 0$, the inductor acts as a short circuit. The current is $i_L(0^-) = \frac{12 \text{ V}}{4 \text{ } \Omega} = 3 \text{ A}$. Since inductor current is continuous, $i_L(0^+) = 3 \text{ A}$. For $t \ge 0$, the circuit is a source-free RL loop with $R_{eq} = 20 \text{ } \Omega$ and $L = 0.1 \text{ H}$. The time constant is $\tau = \frac{L}{R} = \frac{0.1}{20} = 0.005 \text{ s} = 5 \text{ ms}$. The current decays as $i_L(t) = 3 e^{-t/0.005} \text{ A}$. The voltage drop across the $20 \text{ } \Omega$ resistor in the direction of current flow is $v_o(t) = i_L(t) R = 3 e^{-200t} (20) = 60 e^{-200t} \text{ V}$. The initial energy is $w(0) = \frac{1}{2} L i_L(0)^2 = \frac{1}{2}(0.1)(3^2) = 0.45 \text{ J}$. The energy remaining at $t = \tau$ is $w(\tau) = \frac{1}{2} L i_L(\tau)^2 = \frac{1}{2} L (i_L(0) e^{-1})^2 = w(0) e^{-2}$. The energy dissipated is $w_{diss} = w(0) - w(\tau) = w(0)(1 - e^{-2})$. The percentage is $\frac{1 - e^{-2}}{1} \times 100\% = (1 - 0.1353) \times 100\% = 86.47\%$.
Answer: (a) $v_o(t) = 60 e^{-200t} \text{ V}$; (b) $86.47\%$
P02 · Circuit Problem

A circuit consists of a 10 V DC source in series with a 5 Ω resistor and a switch. The switch is open for a long time. At t = 0, the switch closes, connecting the source to a parallel combination of a 10 Ω resistor and a 0.2 H inductor. (a) Find the inductor current i_o(0+) and i_o(∞). (b) Find the expression for i_o(t) for t >= 0. (c) Determine the time in milliseconds when the inductor current reaches 0.9 A.

For t < 0, the switch is open, so no current flows through the inductor. $i_o(0^-) = 0 \text{ A}$. Thus, $i_o(0^+) = 0 \text{ A}$. For t >= 0, the circuit reaches a steady state where the inductor acts as a short circuit. The 10 Ω resistor is shorted out. The total resistance seen by the source is the series 5 Ω resistor. The steady-state current through the inductor is the total current: $i_o(\infty) = \frac{10 \text{ V}}{5 \text{ } \Omega} = 2 \text{ A}$. To find the time constant, we find the Thevenin resistance seen by the inductor. Turn off the source (short the 10 V source). The 5 Ω resistor is in parallel with the 10 Ω resistor. $R_{th} = 5 \parallel 10 = \frac{5 \times 10}{5 + 10} = \frac{50}{15} = \frac{10}{3} \text{ } \Omega$. The time constant is $\tau = \frac{L}{R_{th}} = \frac{0.2}{10/3} = 0.06 \text{ s} = 60 \text{ ms}$. The current expression is $i_o(t) = i_o(\infty) + [i_o(0^+) - i_o(\infty)] e^{-t/\tau} = 2 + (0 - 2) e^{-t/0.06} = 2(1 - e^{-16.67t}) \text{ A}$. To find the time when $i_o(t) = 0.9 \text{ A}$: $0.9 = 2(1 - e^{-t/0.06})$. $0.45 = 1 - e^{-t/0.06}$. $e^{-t/0.06} = 0.55$. $-t/0.06 = \ln(0.55) = -0.5978$. $t = 0.06 \times 0.5978 = 0.03587 \text{ s} = 35.87 \text{ ms}$.
Answer: (a) $i_o(0^+) = 0 \text{ A}$, $i_o(\infty) = 2 \text{ A}$; (b) $i_o(t) = 2(1 - e^{-16.67t}) \text{ A}$; (c) $35.87 \text{ ms}$
P03 · Circuit Problem

A circuit contains a 24 V DC source in series with a 12 Ω resistor and a switch. The switch has been closed for a long time, establishing a steady-state current. At t = 0, the switch opens, disconnecting the source and leaving the inductor L in a closed loop with a 6 Ω resistor. (a) Determine the value of inductance L such that the voltage v_o(t) across the 6 Ω resistor is 25% of its initial value v_o(0+) at t = 4 ms. (b) Calculate the value of v_o at t = 4 ms.

For t < 0, the inductor is a short. The current is $i_L(0^-) = \frac{24 \text{ V}}{12 \text{ } \Omega} = 2 \text{ A}$. Thus $i_L(0^+) = 2 \text{ A}$. For t >= 0, the circuit is a source-free RL loop with $R = 6 \text{ } \Omega$. The current decays as $i_L(t) = 2 e^{-t/\tau} \text{ A}$. The voltage across the 6 Ω resistor is $v_o(t) = i_L(t) R = 12 e^{-t/\tau} \text{ V}$. The initial voltage is $v_o(0^+) = 12 \text{ V}$. We require $v_o(4 \text{ ms}) = 0.25 v_o(0^+)$. So, $12 e^{-0.004/\tau} = 0.25(12)$. $e^{-0.004/\tau} = 0.25$. Taking the natural log: $-0.004/\tau = \ln(0.25) = -\ln(4) = -1.3863$. $\tau = \frac{0.004}{1.3863} = 0.002885 \text{ s}$. Since $\tau = L/R$, we have $L = \tau R = 0.002885 \times 6 = 0.01731 \text{ H} = 17.31 \text{ mH}$. The voltage at t = 4 ms is $v_o(4 \text{ ms}) = 0.25 \times 12 = 3 \text{ V}$.
Answer: (a) $L = 17.31 \text{ mH}$; (b) $v_o(4 \text{ ms}) = 3 \text{ V}$
P04 · Circuit Problem

A circuit consists of a 10 V DC source in series with a resistor R and a 10 mH inductor. A switch has been in position 1 (connecting the source) for a long time. At t = 0, the switch moves to position 2, disconnecting the source and shorting the inductor through the resistor R. Find the value of R such that 50% of the initial energy stored in the inductor is dissipated at t = 2 ms.

For $t < 0$, the inductor acts as a short circuit in steady state. The initial current is given by $i_L(0^-) = \frac{10 \text{ V}}{R}$. The initial energy stored in the inductor is:
$$ w(0) = \frac{1}{2} L \left( \frac{10}{R} \right)^2 $$
For $t > 0$, the inductor discharges through the resistor $R$. The current decays exponentially as:
$$ i_L(t) = \frac{10}{R} e^{-t/\tau} $$
where the time constant is $\tau = L/R$. The energy stored in the inductor at time $t$ is:
$$ w(t) = \frac{1}{2} L \left( \frac{10}{R} \right)^2 e^{-2t/\tau} $$
The condition that 50% of the energy is dissipated implies that 50% of the initial energy remains at time $t_0$:
$$ w(t_0) = 0.5 w(0) $$
Substituting the expressions for energy:
$$ \frac{1}{2} L \left( \frac{10}{R} \right)^2 e^{-2t_0/\tau} = 0.5 \left[ \frac{1}{2} L \left( \frac{10}{R} \right)^2 \right] $$
$$ e^{-2t_0/\tau} = 0.5 $$
Taking the natural logarithm of both sides:
$$ -\frac{2t_0}{\tau} = \ln(0.5) = -\ln 2 $$
$$ \tau = \frac{2 t_0}{\ln 2} $$
Given $t_0 = 2 \text{ ms} = 0.002 \text{ s}$:
$$ \tau = \frac{2(0.002)}{\ln 2} = \frac{0.004}{0.6931} = 0.005771 \text{ s} $$
Since $\tau = L/R$, the resistance $R$ is:
$$ R = \frac{L}{\tau} = \frac{0.01}{0.005771} = 1.7327 \text{ } \Omega $$
Answer: $R = 1.733 \ \Omega$
P05 · Circuit Problem

Consider a general source-free RL circuit with inductance L and resistance R. The initial current in the inductor is $I_g$. Derive an expression for R in terms of $I_g$, the fraction $\sigma$ of the initial energy dissipated in time $t_0$, and L. Note that the fraction of energy remaining is $1-\sigma$.

The initial energy stored in the inductor is $w(0) = \frac{1}{2} L I_g^2$. The current in a source-free RL circuit decays as $i_L(t) = I_g e^{-t/\tau}$, where $\tau = L/R$. The energy stored in the inductor at time $t$ is $w(t) = \frac{1}{2} L i_L(t)^2 = \frac{1}{2} L (I_g e^{-t/\tau})^2 = \frac{1}{2} L I_g^2 e^{-2t/\tau} = w(0) e^{-2t/\tau}$. The problem states that a fraction $\sigma$ of the initial energy is dissipated in time $t_0$. This means the energy remaining at $t_0$ is $w(t_0) = w(0) - \sigma w(0) = w(0)(1 - \sigma)$. Equating the expressions for $w(t_0)$: $w(0) e^{-2t_0/\tau} = w(0)(1 - \sigma)$. $e^{-2t_0/\tau} = 1 - \sigma$. Taking the natural logarithm of both sides: $-\frac{2t_0}{\tau} = \ln(1 - \sigma)$. $\frac{2t_0}{\tau} = -\ln(1 - \sigma) = \ln\left(\frac{1}{1 - \sigma}\right)$. $\tau = \frac{2t_0}{\ln\left(\frac{1}{1 - \sigma}\right)}$. Since $\tau = L/R$, we have $\frac{L}{R} = \frac{2t_0}{\ln\left(\frac{1}{1 - \sigma}\right)}$. Solving for R: $R = \frac{L \ln\left(\frac{1}{1 - \sigma}\right)}{2t_0}$.
Answer: $R = \frac{L \ln(1/(1-\sigma))}{2t_0}$
P06 · Circuit Problem

A circuit consists of a 5 V DC source in series with a 10 Ω resistor and a switch. The switch has been closed for a long time. At t = 0, the switch opens, disconnecting the source and leaving a 20 mH inductor in a closed loop with a 5 Ω resistor. Find the voltage v_o(t) across the 5 Ω resistor for t >= 0+.

For t < 0, the inductor acts as a short circuit. The current is determined by the source and the 10 Ω resistor: $i_L(0^-) = \frac{5 \text{ V}}{10 \text{ } \Omega} = 0.5 \text{ A}$. Thus, $i_L(0^+) = 0.5 \text{ A}$. For t >= 0, the circuit is a source-free RL loop with $R = 5 \text{ } \Omega$ and $L = 20 \text{ mH} = 0.02 \text{ H}$. The time constant is $\tau = \frac{L}{R} = \frac{0.02}{5} = 0.004 \text{ s} = 4 \text{ ms}$. The current decays as $i_L(t) = 0.5 e^{-t/0.004} = 0.5 e^{-250t} \text{ A}$. The voltage $v_o(t)$ across the 5 Ω resistor is $v_o(t) = i_L(t) R = 0.5 e^{-250t} (5) = 2.5 e^{-250t} \text{ V}$.
Answer: $v_o(t) = 2.5 e^{-250t} \text{ V}$
P07 · Circuit Problem

A circuit contains a 1 mA DC current source connected to a switch. For t < 0, the switch is in position a, connecting the source to a 1 kΩ resistor. At t = 0, the switch moves to position b, connecting the source to a parallel combination of two inductors (4 mH and 12 mH) and a 2 kΩ resistor. (a) Find the voltage v_o(t) across the 2 kΩ resistor for t >= 0+. (b) Calculate the total energy delivered to the 2 kΩ resistor. (c) Determine how many time constants are required to deliver 95% of this energy.

For t < 0, the inductors are open circuits (steady state DC). The current source flows through the 1 kΩ resistor. The voltage across the parallel branches (which are open) is $V = I R = 1 \text{ mA} \times 1 \text{ k} \Omega = 1 \text{ V}$. The inductor currents are zero: $i_{L1}(0^-) = 0, i_{L2}(0^-) = 0$. Thus $i_{L1}(0^+) = 0, i_{L2}(0^+) = 0$. For t >= 0, the source is connected to the parallel combination of the inductors and the 2 kΩ resistor. The equivalent inductance of the parallel inductors is $L_{eq} = \frac{4 \times 12}{4 + 12} = \frac{48}{16} = 3 \text{ mH}$. The circuit is a parallel RL circuit driven by a current source. The time constant is $\tau = \frac{L_{eq}}{R} = \frac{3 \text{ mH}}{2 \text{ k} \Omega} = 1.5 \text{ } \mu\text{s}$. The current through the inductor branch (total) is $i_L(t) = i_L(\infty) + [i_L(0^+) - i_L(\infty)] e^{-t/\tau}$. In steady state (t -> ∞), inductors are shorts, so all current flows through the inductors, and the voltage across the resistor is 0. $i_L(\infty) = 1 \text{ mA}$. $i_L(0^+) = 0$. So $i_L(t) = 1 (1 - e^{-t/1.5\mu}) \text{ mA}$. The voltage across the 2 kΩ resistor is $v_o(t) = (I_{source} - i_L(t)) R = (1 \text{ mA} - 1(1 - e^{-t/\tau}) \text{ mA}) (2 \text{ k} \Omega) = (e^{-t/\tau} \text{ mA}) (2 \text{ k} \Omega) = 2 e^{-t/1.5\mu} \text{ V}$. (b) Total energy delivered to the resistor is the integral of power: $W = \int_0^\infty \frac{v_o(t)^2}{R} dt = \int_0^\infty \frac{(2 e^{-t/\tau})^2}{2000} dt = \frac{4}{2000} \int_0^\infty e^{-2t/\tau} dt = \frac{4}{2000} \left[ \frac{-\tau}{2} e^{-2t/\tau} \right]_0^\infty = \frac{4}{2000} \frac{\tau}{2} = \frac{2 \tau}{2000}$. $\tau = 1.5 \times 10^{-6} \text{ s}$. $W = \frac{2 (1.5 \times 10^{-6})}{2000} = \frac{3 \times 10^{-6}}{2000} = 1.5 \times 10^{-9} \text{ J} = 1.5 \text{ nJ}$. (c) Energy delivered up to time t is $W(t) = \int_0^t \frac{v_o^2}{R} dt = \frac{4}{2000} \frac{\tau}{2} (1 - e^{-2t/\tau}) = W_{total} (1 - e^{-2t/\tau})$. We want $W(t) = 0.95 W_{total}$. $1 - e^{-2t/\tau} = 0.95$. $e^{-2t/\tau} = 0.05$. $-2t/\tau = \ln(0.05) = -2.9957$. $2t/\tau = 2.9957$. $t/\tau = 1.4978$. So approximately 1.5 time constants.
Answer: (a) $v_o(t) = 2 e^{-666.67t} \text{ V}$; (b) $1.5 \text{ nJ}$; (c) $1.5$ time constants
P08 · Circuit Problem

A circuit consists of a 10 V DC source in series with a 5 kΩ resistor (R1) and a switch. The switch has been open for a long time. At t = 0, the switch closes. The circuit also contains a 10 kΩ resistor (R2) in parallel with a 0.1 mF capacitor (C). R1 is in series with the parallel combination of R2 and C. (a) Find the current i1(0-) through R1 and i2(0-) through R2. (b) Find i1(0+) and i2(0+). (c) Explain why i1(0-) = i1(0+). (d) Explain why i2(0-) != i2(0+). (e) Find the expression for i1(t) for t >= 0. (f) Find the expression for i2(t) for t >= 0.

For $t < 0$, the switch is open. No current flows from the source, so $i_1(0^-) = 0 \text{ A}$. The capacitor is uncharged, so $v_C(0^-) = 0 \text{ V}$. The current through $R_2$ is $i_2(0^-) = v_C(0^-)/R_2 = 0 \text{ A}$.

(b) At $t = 0^+$, the switch closes. The capacitor voltage cannot change instantaneously, so $v_C(0^+) = v_C(0^-) = 0 \text{ V}$. The circuit consists of the source $V$ in series with $R_1$ and the parallel combination of $R_2$ and $C$. Since $v_C(0^+) = 0$, the voltage across $R_2$ is $0 \text{ V}$, resulting in $i_2(0^+) = 0 \text{ A}$. At $t = 0^+$, the capacitor acts as a short circuit, effectively shorting out $R_2$. The total resistance seen by the source is therefore $R_1 = 5 \text{ k}\Omega$. The total current is $i_1(0^+) = \frac{10 \text{ V}}{5 \text{ k}\Omega} = 2 \text{ mA}$.

(c) The capacitor voltage is continuous across the switching instant because the energy stored in a capacitor cannot change instantaneously. Thus, $v_C(0^-) = v_C(0^+)$.

(d) The current through the capacitor is discontinuous at $t = 0$. For $t < 0$, the circuit is open and the capacitor current is zero, so $i_C(0^-) = 0 \text{ A}$. For $t > 0$, the capacitor is connected to the source through $R_1$, and the initial current is determined by the source voltage and the series resistance, yielding $i_C(0^+) = 2 \text{ mA}$. Therefore, $i_C(0^-) \neq i_C(0^+)$.
Answer: (a) $i_1(0^-) = 0$, $i_2(0^-) = 0$; (b) $i_1(0^+) = 2 \text{ mA}$, $i_2(0^+) = 0$; (c) $v_C$ is continuous; (d) $i_C$ jumps; (e) $i_1(t) = 2 e^{-100t} \text{ mA}$; (f) $i_2(t) = 2 e^{-100t} \text{ mA}$
P09 · Circuit Problem

Consider the RC circuit shown in the circuit_spec. The switch has been closed for a long time, ensuring the capacitor is fully charged. At t = 0, the switch opens. The circuit consists of a 12 V DC source connected in series with a 4 kΩ resistor (R1) and a 6 kΩ resistor (R2). A 10 µF capacitor (C) is connected in parallel with R2. (a) Determine the initial voltage across the capacitor, v(0). (b) Determine the time constant, τ, for t > 0. (c) Determine the expression for v(t) for t ≥ 0. (d) Calculate the initial energy stored in the capacitor. (e) Determine the time required to dissipate 75% of the initial energy.

(a) For t < 0, the switch is closed and the circuit is in DC steady state. The capacitor acts as an open circuit. The voltage v(0) is the voltage across the 6 kΩ resistor, found using the voltage divider rule: $v(0) = 12 \text{ V} \times \frac{6 \text{ k}\Omega}{4 \text{ k}\Omega + 6 \text{ k}\Omega} = 12 \times \frac{6}{10} = 7.2 \text{ V}$. (b) For t > 0, the switch opens, disconnecting the voltage source and the 4 kΩ resistor. The capacitor discharges through the 6 kΩ resistor. The equivalent resistance is $R_{eq} = 6 \text{ k}\Omega$. The time constant is $\tau = R_{eq} C = (6 \times 10^3 \Omega)(10 \times 10^{-6} \text{ F}) = 0.06 \text{ s}$. (c) The voltage decays exponentially: $v(t) = v(0) e^{-t/\tau} = 7.2 e^{-t/0.06} \text{ V} = 7.2 e^{-16.67t} \text{ V}$. (d) The initial energy is $w(0) = \frac{1}{2} C v(0)^2 = \frac{1}{2} (10 \times 10^{-6}) (7.2)^2 = 5 \times 10^{-6} \times 51.84 = 259.2 \times 10^{-6} \text{ J} = 259.2 \text{ µJ}$. (e) The energy at time t is $w(t) = \frac{1}{2} C v(t)^2 = \frac{1}{2} C v(0)^2 e^{-2t/\tau} = w(0) e^{-2t/\tau}$. We want to dissipate 75% of the initial energy, meaning 25% remains: $w(t) = 0.25 w(0)$. Thus, $e^{-2t/\tau} = 0.25$. Taking the natural log: $-2t/\tau = \ln(0.25) = -\ln(4)$. So, $t = \frac{\tau}{2} \ln(4) = \frac{0.06}{2} \ln(4) = 0.03 \ln(4) \approx 0.03(1.386) = 0.0416 \text{ s} = 41.6 \text{ ms}$.
Answer: (a) $7.2 \text{ V}$; (b) $0.06 \text{ s}$; (c) $7.2 e^{-16.67t} \text{ V}$; (d) $259.2 \text{ \mu J}$; (e) $41.6 \text{ ms}$

P12 · Circuit Problem

The circuit in the circuit_spec has a switch that has been open for a long time. At t = 0, the switch closes. The circuit contains a 10 V DC source, a 2 kΩ resistor (R1) in series with the source, and an inductor L = 5 mH in parallel with a 4 kΩ resistor (R2). The output voltage v_o is defined across the inductor. Find v_o(t) for t ≥ 0+.

For $t < 0$, the switch is open. No current flows through the inductor, so $i_L(0^-) = 0$ A. Since inductor current is continuous, $i_L(0^+) = 0$ A. For $t > 0$, the switch closes. The Thevenin equivalent circuit seen by the inductor is determined by removing the inductor. The open-circuit voltage $V_{th}$ is the voltage across the terminals where the inductor was connected, which corresponds to the voltage across $R_2$. With the inductor removed (open circuit), the circuit consists of a series loop of $V$, $R_1$, and $R_2$.
$$V_{th} = V_{R2} = 10 \text{ V} \times \frac{4 \text{ k}\Omega}{2 \text{ k}\Omega + 4 \text{ k}\Omega} = 10 \times \frac{4}{6} = \frac{20}{3} \text{ V}$$
The Thevenin resistance $R_{th}$ is found by turning off the source (shorting $V$). $R_1$ and $R_2$ are in parallel:
$$R_{th} = R_1 || R_2 = \frac{2 \times 4}{2 + 4} \text{ k}\Omega = \frac{8}{6} \text{ k}\Omega = \frac{4}{3} \text{ k}\Omega$$
The time constant is
$$\tau = \frac{L}{R_{th}} = \frac{5 \times 10^{-3} \text{ H}}{\frac{4}{3} \times 10^3 \Omega} = \frac{5}{4/3} \times 10^{-6} \text{ s} = \frac{15}{4} \times 10^{-6} \text{ s} = 3.75 \text{ µs}$$
The final inductor current is
$$i_L(\infty) = \frac{V_{th}}{R_{th}} = \frac{20/3}{4/3} = 5 \text{ mA}$$
The inductor current is
$$i_L(t) = i_L(\infty) + [i_L(0) - i_L(\infty)] e^{-t/\tau} = 5 \text{ mA} + [0 - 5 \text{ mA}] e^{-t/3.75\mu} = 5(1 - e^{-266667t}) \text{ mA}$$
The voltage across the inductor is $v_o(t) = L \frac{di_L}{dt}$.
$$\frac{di_L}{dt} = 5 \text{ mA} \times 266667 e^{-266667t} = 1.333 \text{ A/s} e^{-266667t}$$
$$v_o(t) = (5 \times 10^{-3}) (1.333 \times 10^6) e^{-266667t} = 6665 e^{-266667t} \text{ V}$$
Alternatively, using the precise expression $i_L(t) = 0.005 (1 - e^{-t/\tau})$:
$$\frac{di_L}{dt} = 0.005 \frac{1}{\tau} e^{-t/\tau}$$
$$v_o(t) = L \frac{0.005}{\tau} e^{-t/\tau} = \frac{L}{\tau} 0.005 e^{-t/\tau} = R_{th} \times 0.005 e^{-t/\tau} = \frac{4000}{3} \times 0.005 e^{-t/\tau} = \frac{20}{3} e^{-t/\tau} \text{ V}$$
With $\tau = 3.75 \text{ µs}$:
$$v_o(t) = \frac{20}{3} e^{-t/3.75\mu} \text{ V} \approx 6.67 e^{-266667t} \text{ V}$$
Answer: $v_o(t) = \frac{20}{3} e^{-266667t} \text{ V}$
P13 · Circuit Problem

The circuit in the circuit_spec has a switch that has been open for a long time, so the capacitor is uncharged. At t = 0, the switch closes. The circuit consists of a 5 mA DC current source in parallel with a 10 kΩ resistor (R1). This combination is connected in series with a 20 kΩ resistor (R2) and a 0.5 mF capacitor (C). The output voltage v(t) is across the capacitor, and i(t) is the current flowing into the capacitor. Find (a) i(t) for t ≥ 0+ and (b) v(t) for t ≥ 0+.

For t < 0, the switch is open, so $v(0^-) = 0$ V and $i(0^-) = 0$ A. For t > 0, the switch closes. We find the Thevenin equivalent circuit seen by the capacitor. The current source (5 mA) in parallel with R1 (10 kΩ) can be converted to a voltage source in series with R1. $V_{th} = I \times R_1 = 5 \text{ mA} \times 10 \text{ k}\Omega = 50 \text{ V}$. The series resistance is $R_{th} = R_1 + R_2 = 10 \text{ k}\Omega + 20 \text{ k}\Omega = 30 \text{ k}\Omega$. The circuit is a series RC circuit with a 50 V source and 30 kΩ resistance. The time constant is $\tau = R_{th} C = (30 \times 10^3 \Omega)(0.5 \times 10^{-3} \text{ F}) = 15 \text{ s}$. The final voltage is $v(\infty) = V_{th} = 50 \text{ V}$. The voltage is $v(t) = v(\infty) + [v(0) - v(\infty)] e^{-t/\tau} = 50 + [0 - 50] e^{-t/15} = 50(1 - e^{-t/15}) \text{ V}$. The current is $i(t) = C \frac{dv}{dt} = (0.5 \times 10^{-3}) \frac{d}{dt} [50(1 - e^{-t/15})] = 0.5 \times 10^{-3} \times 50 \times \frac{1}{15} e^{-t/15} = \frac{25}{15} \times 10^{-3} e^{-t/15} = \frac{5}{3} \text{ mA} e^{-t/15} \approx 1.67 \text{ mA} e^{-t/15}$.
Answer: (a) $i(t) = \frac{5}{3} e^{-t/15} \text{ mA}$; (b) $v(t) = 50(1 - e^{-t/15}) \text{ V}$
P14 · Circuit Problem

The circuit in the circuit_spec has a switch that has been in position 'a' for a long time. At t = 0, the switch moves to position 'b'. In position 'a', the circuit consists of a 10 mA DC current source in parallel with a 5 kΩ resistor (R1) and a 100 nF capacitor (C). In position 'b', the capacitor is connected in series with a 15 kΩ resistor (R2) and a 5 kΩ resistor (R3) to ground (R2 and R3 are in series). Find (a) the initial capacitor voltage v_C(0), (b) the final capacitor voltage v_C(∞), (c) the time constant τ for t > 0, and (d) the time for v_C to reach zero.

(a) For $t < 0$, the switch is at position 'a'. The circuit is in DC steady state, and the capacitor acts as an open circuit. The current source ($10 \text{ mA}$) flows entirely through $R_1$ ($5 \text{ k}\Omega$). The voltage across the capacitor is equal to the voltage across $R_1$:
$$v_C(0) = I \times R_1 = 10 \text{ mA} \times 5 \text{ k}\Omega = 50 \text{ V}$$

(b) For $t > 0$, the switch is at position 'b'. The capacitor discharges through the series combination of $R_2$ and $R_3$. The final voltage is:
$$v_C(\infty) = 0 \text{ V}$$

(c) The equivalent resistance for $t > 0$ is:
$$R_{eq} = R_2 + R_3 = 15 \text{ k}\Omega + 5 \text{ k}\Omega = 20 \text{ k}\Omega$$
The time constant is:
$$\tau = R_{eq} C = (20 \times 10^3 \Omega)(100 \times 10^{-9} \text{ F}) = 2000 \times 10^{-6} \text{ s} = 2 \text{ ms} = 2000 \text{ µs}$$

(d) The voltage expression is:
$$v_C(t) = v_C(\infty) + [v_C(0) - v_C(\infty)] e^{-t/\tau} = 0 + (50 - 0) e^{-t/2000\mu} = 50 e^{-t/2000\mu} \text{ V}$$
The voltage $v_C(t)$ approaches zero asymptotically. In engineering practice, the time required for the voltage to effectively reach zero is approximated as four time constants ($4\tau$), at which point the voltage has decayed to 1% of its initial value.
$$t = 4\tau = 4 \times 2000 \text{ µs} = 8000 \text{ µs}$$
Answer: (a) $50\ \text{V}$; (b) $0\ \text{V}$; (c) $2000\ \mu\text{s}$; (d) $8000\ \mu\text{s}$ (approx. $1\%$ settling)
P15 · Circuit Problem

The circuit contains a switch that has been in position 'a' for a long time. At $t = 0$, the switch moves to position 'b'. In position 'a', the circuit consists of a $10\text{ mA}$ DC current source in parallel with a $20\text{ k}\Omega$ resistor ($R_1$) and a $100\text{ nF}$ capacitor ($C$). In position 'b', the capacitor is connected in parallel with a $10\text{ k}\Omega$ resistor ($R_2$). Determine $v_o(t)$ and $i_o(t)$ for $t \ge 0^+$, where $v_o$ is the voltage across the capacitor and $i_o$ is the current through $R_2$.

For t < 0, the switch is at 'a'. The circuit is in DC steady state. The capacitor acts as an open circuit. The current source (10 mA) flows through R1 (20 kΩ). The voltage across the capacitor is $v_o(0) = I \times R_1 = 10 \text{ mA} \times 20 \text{ k}\Omega = 200 \text{ V}$. For t > 0, the switch is at 'b'. The capacitor discharges through R2 (10 kΩ). The equivalent resistance is $R_{eq} = R_2 = 10 \text{ k}\Omega$. The time constant is $\tau = R_{eq} C = (10 \times 10^3 \Omega)(100 \times 10^{-9} \text{ F}) = 1000 \times 10^{-6} \text{ s} = 1 \text{ ms} = 1000 \text{ µs}$. The final voltage is $v_o(\infty) = 0 \text{ V}$. The voltage expression is $v_o(t) = v_o(0) e^{-t/\tau} = 200 e^{-t/0.001} = 200 e^{-1000t} \text{ V}$. The current through R2 is $i_o(t) = \frac{v_o(t)}{R_2} = \frac{200 e^{-1000t}}{10 \times 10^3} = 0.02 e^{-1000t} \text{ A} = 20 e^{-1000t} \text{ mA}$.
Answer: $v_o(t) = 200 e^{-1000t} \text{ V}$; $i_o(t) = 20 e^{-1000t} \text{ mA}$
