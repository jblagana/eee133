P01 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.4, p. 257

A circuit consists of a 12 V DC source, a 40 Ω resistor, and a 0.4 H inductor in series. A switch in series with the source has been closed for a long time and opens at $t = 0$. (a) Find the inductor current $i(t)$ for $t \ge 0^+$. (b) Determine the percentage of the initial energy stored in the inductor that is dissipated by the resistor after one time constant ($t = \tau$).

For t < 0, the switch is closed and the circuit is in DC steady state. The inductor acts as a short circuit. The current is $i(0^-) = \frac{V}{R} = \frac{12}{40} = 0.3$ A. Since inductor current cannot change instantaneously, $i(0^+) = 0.3$ A. For t >= 0, the source is disconnected, leaving a source-free RL circuit. The time constant is $\tau = \frac{L}{R} = \frac{0.4}{40} = 0.01$ s. The current decays as $i(t) = i(0^+)e^{-t/\tau} = 0.3e^{-100t}$ A. The energy stored in the inductor is $w(t) = \frac{1}{2}Li(t)^2$. The initial energy is $w(0) = \frac{1}{2}(0.4)(0.3)^2 = 0.018$ J. At $t = \tau$, $i(\tau) = 0.3e^{-1}$ A. The remaining energy is $w(\tau) = \frac{1}{2}L(0.3e^{-1})^2 = w(0)e^{-2}$. The dissipated energy is $w(0) - w(\tau) = w(0)(1 - e^{-2})$. The percentage dissipated is $(1 - e^{-2}) \times 100\% \approx (1 - 0.1353) \times 100\% = 86.47\%$.
Answer: (a) $i(t) = 0.3e^{-100t} \text{ A}$; (b) $86.47\%$
P02 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.5, p. 256

A circuit contains a 10 V DC source, a 50 Ω resistor, and a 0.5 H inductor in series. A switch in series with the source has been open for a long time and closes at $t = 0$. (a) Find the inductor current $i(0^+)$ and $i(\infty)$. (b) Find the expression for $i(t)$ for $t \ge 0$. (c) Calculate the time required for the current to reach 90% of its final value.

For t < 0, the switch is open, so no current flows. $i(0^-) = 0$ A. Thus, $i(0^+) = 0$ A. For t -> ∞, the inductor acts as a short circuit in DC steady state. The final current is $i(\infty) = \frac{V}{R} = \frac{10}{50} = 0.2$ A. The time constant is $\tau = \frac{L}{R} = \frac{0.5}{50} = 0.01$ s. The general solution for a step response is $i(t) = i(\infty) + [i(0^+) - i(\infty)]e^{-t/\tau}$. Substituting the values: $i(t) = 0.2 + [0 - 0.2]e^{-100t} = 0.2(1 - e^{-100t})$ A. To find the time when $i(t) = 0.9 i(\infty) = 0.18$ A, we set $0.2(1 - e^{-100t}) = 0.18$. This gives $1 - e^{-100t} = 0.9$, so $e^{-100t} = 0.1$. Taking the natural log: $-100t = \ln(0.1) = -\ln(10)$. Thus, $t = \frac{\ln(10)}{100} \approx \frac{2.3026}{100} = 0.02303$ s or 23.03 ms.
Answer: (a) $i(0^+) = 0 \text{ A}$, $i(\infty) = 0.2 \text{ A}$; (b) $i(t) = 0.2(1 - e^{-100t}) \text{ A}$; (c) $23.03 \text{ ms}$
P03 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.8, p. 257

A source-free RL circuit consists of a resistor R = 200 Ω and an inductor L. The switch has been closed for a long time, establishing an initial current, and opens at t = 0. The voltage across the inductor is measured to be 25% of its initial value at t = 4 ms. (a) Determine the value of L. (b) Find the voltage across the inductor at t = 8 ms as a percentage of the initial voltage.

In a source-free RL circuit, the inductor voltage is given by $v_L(t) = v_L(0^+)e^{-t/\tau}$, where $\tau = L/R$. We are given that $v_L(4\text{ms}) = 0.25 v_L(0^+)$. Therefore, $e^{-0.004/\tau} = 0.25$. Taking the natural logarithm of both sides: $-\frac{0.004}{\tau} = \ln(0.25) = \ln(1/4) = -\ln(4)$. Solving for $\tau$: $\tau = \frac{0.004}{\ln(4)} \approx \frac{0.004}{1.3863} \approx 0.002885$ s. Since $\tau = L/R$, we have $L = \tau R = 0.002885 \times 200 \approx 0.577$ H. For part (b), at $t = 8$ ms, which is $2\tau$, the voltage is $v_L(2\tau) = v_L(0^+)e^{-2}$. The ratio is $e^{-2} \approx 0.1353$. As a percentage, this is $13.53\%$.
Answer: (a) $L \approx 0.577 \text{ H}$; (b) $13.53\%$
P04 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.9, p. 257

A source-free RL circuit contains an inductor L = 40 mH and a resistor R. The initial current in the inductor is 2 A. Find the value of R such that 50% of the initial energy stored in the inductor is dissipated at t = 5 ms.

The energy stored in an inductor is proportional to the square of the current: $w(t) = \frac{1}{2}Li(t)^2$. The current in a source-free RL circuit decays as $i(t) = i(0)e^{-t/\tau}$. Thus, the energy decays as $w(t) = \frac{1}{2}L[i(0)e^{-t/\tau}]^2 = w(0)e^{-2t/\tau}$. We require that 50% of the initial energy is dissipated at $t = 5$ ms, which means 50% of the energy remains. So, $w(0.005) = 0.5 w(0)$. Substituting the decay equation: $w(0)e^{-2(0.005)/\tau} = 0.5 w(0)$. This simplifies to $e^{-0.01/\tau} = 0.5$. Taking the natural log: $-\frac{0.01}{\tau} = \ln(0.5) = -\ln(2)$. Solving for $\tau$: $\tau = \frac{0.01}{\ln(2)} \approx \frac{0.01}{0.6931} \approx 0.014427$ s. Since $\tau = L/R$, we have $R = L/\tau = \frac{0.040}{0.014427} \approx 2.773$ Ω.
Answer: R ≈ 2.773 Ω
P05 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.10, p. 257

Consider a general source-free RL circuit with inductance L and resistance R. The initial current is $I_0$. Derive an expression for R in terms of L, $I_0$, the fraction $\sigma$ of initial energy remaining, and time $t_0$, such that the energy remaining at $t_0$ is $\sigma$ times the initial energy.

The current in a source-free RL circuit is $i(t) = I_0 e^{-t/\tau}$, where $\tau = L/R$. The energy stored in the inductor at time t is $w(t) = \frac{1}{2}L i(t)^2 = \frac{1}{2}L I_0^2 e^{-2t/\tau} = w(0) e^{-2t/\tau}$. We are given that at $t = t_0$, the remaining energy is $\sigma w(0)$. Therefore, $w(t_0) = \sigma w(0)$. Substituting the energy expression: $w(0) e^{-2t_0/\tau} = \sigma w(0)$. Dividing by $w(0)$: $e^{-2t_0/\tau} = \sigma$. Taking the natural logarithm: $-\frac{2t_0}{\tau} = \ln(\sigma)$. Solving for $\tau$: $\tau = \frac{-2t_0}{\ln(\sigma)} = \frac{2t_0}{\ln(1/\sigma)}$. Since $\tau = L/R$, we have $R = L/\tau = \frac{L}{\frac{2t_0}{\ln(1/\sigma)}} = \frac{L \ln(1/\sigma)}{2t_0}$.
Answer: $R = \frac{L \ln(1/\sigma)}{2 t_0}$

P06 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.16, p. 258

A circuit consists of a 20 V DC source, a 100 Ω resistor, and a 50 mH inductor in series. A switch in series with the source has been closed for a long time and opens at $t = 0$. Find the voltage across the inductor, $v_L(t)$, for $t \ge 0^+$.

For t < 0, the circuit is in DC steady state with the switch closed. The inductor acts as a short circuit. The current is $i(0^-) = \frac{V}{R} = \frac{20}{100} = 0.2$ A. Thus, $i(0^+) = 0.2$ A. For t >= 0, the source is disconnected, and the inductor current decays through the resistor. The time constant is $\tau = \frac{L}{R} = \frac{0.050}{100} = 0.0005$ s. The current is $i(t) = 0.2 e^{-t/0.0005} = 0.2 e^{-2000t}$ A. The voltage across the inductor is $v_L(t) = L \frac{di}{dt}$. Differentiating the current: $\frac{di}{dt} = 0.2 (-2000) e^{-2000t} = -400 e^{-2000t}$ A/s. Therefore, $v_L(t) = 0.050 (-400 e^{-2000t}) = -20 e^{-2000t}$ V. Alternatively, using KVL on the source-free loop: $v_L(t) = -i(t)R = -(0.2 e^{-2000t})(100) = -20 e^{-2000t}$ V.
Answer: $v_L(t) = -20e^{-2000t} \text{ V}$
P07 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.20, p. 259

A circuit has a 5 mA DC current source connected to a parallel combination of two inductors (L1 = 100 mH and L2 = 50 mH) and a resistor R = 150 Ω. A switch has been in position 'a' (connecting the source) for a long time and moves to position 'b' (source-free loop) at t = 0. (a) Find the equivalent inductance Leq. (b) Find the total energy delivered to the resistor. (c) How many time constants are required to dissipate 95% of the total energy?

(a) The equivalent inductance of parallel inductors is $L_{eq} = \frac{L_1 L_2}{L_1 + L_2} = \frac{100 \times 50}{100 + 50} = \frac{5000}{150} = \frac{100}{3} \approx 33.33$ mH. (b) For t < 0, the inductors act as short circuits in DC steady state. The entire source current flows through the inductors. The initial current in the equivalent inductor is $i(0^-) = 5$ mA. The initial energy stored in the inductors is $w(0) = \frac{1}{2} L_{eq} i(0)^2 = \frac{1}{2} (0.03333) (0.005)^2 = \frac{1}{2} (0.03333) (25 \times 10^{-6}) \approx 4.167 \times 10^{-7}$ J. In the source-free loop, all this energy is eventually dissipated by the resistor. So, total energy delivered is $4.167 \times 10^{-7}$ J or 0.4167 µJ. (c) The energy decays as $w(t) = w(0) e^{-2t/\tau}$. We want the dissipated energy to be 95% of the total, which means the remaining energy is 5% of the initial. So, $w(t) = 0.05 w(0)$. This implies $e^{-2t/\tau} = 0.05$. Let $n = t/\tau$ be the number of time constants. Then $e^{-2n} = 0.05$. Taking the natural log: $-2n = \ln(0.05) = \ln(1/20) = -\ln(20)$. So, $2n = \ln(20) \approx 2.9957$. $n = \frac{2.9957}{2} \approx 1.498$. Thus, approximately 1.5 time constants are required.
Answer: (a) $L_{eq} = 33.33 \text{ mH}$; (b) $0.4167 \text{ \mu J}$; (c) $1.5$ time constants
P08 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.21, p. 259

A circuit consists of a 100 mA DC current source connected to a parallel combination of two branches. Branch 1 has a resistor $R_1 = 20 \text{ k}\Omega$. Branch 2 has a resistor $R_2 = 5 \text{ k}\Omega$ in series with a capacitor $C = 0.2 \text{ mF}$. A switch in series with the current source has been open for a long time and closes at $t = 0$. (a) Find the current $i_1(0^+)$ through $R_1$. (b) Find the current $i_2(0^+)$ through the capacitor branch. (c) Find the expression for $i_1(t)$ for $t \ge 0$.

For $t < 0$, the switch is open, so the capacitor is uncharged: $v_C(0^-) = 0$ V. At $t = 0^+$, the capacitor voltage cannot change instantaneously, so $v_C(0^+) = 0$ V. The capacitor acts as a short circuit at $t = 0^+$. The circuit consists of a current source of 100 mA feeding two parallel branches: $R_1 = 20$ k$\Omega$ and $R_2 = 5$ k$\Omega$ (since $C$ is a short). The equivalent resistance of the parallel combination is $R_{eq} = \frac{20 \times 5}{20 + 5} = \frac{100}{25} = 4$ k$\Omega$. The voltage across the parallel branches at $t = 0^+$ is $v(0^+) = I_{source} R_{eq} = 0.1 \times 4000 = 400$ V.

(a) The current through $R_1$ is $i_1(0^+) = \frac{v(0^+)}{R_1} = \frac{400}{20000} = 0.02$ A = 20 mA.

(b) The current through the capacitor branch is $i_2(0^+) = \frac{v(0^+)}{R_2} = \frac{400}{5000} = 0.08$ A = 80 mA.

(c) For $t \ge 0$, the capacitor charges. The time constant is $\tau = R_{th} C$, where $R_{th}$ is the Thevenin resistance seen by the capacitor. Deactivating the current source (open circuit), the resistance seen by $C$ is determined by looking into the terminals of $C$. The top terminal of $C$ is connected to $R_2$, which is connected to the top node. The bottom terminal of $C$ is connected to the bottom node. The top node is connected to $R_1$ and the current source (open). Looking from $C$, the path is through $R_2$ to the top node, and then through $R_1$ to the bottom node. Thus, $R_{th} = R_1 + R_2 = 20 + 5 = 25$ k$\Omega$.

The Thevenin voltage $V_{th}$ is the open-circuit voltage across the capacitor terminals. With $C$ removed, no current flows through $R_2$. The voltage at the top node is determined by the current source and $R_1$: $v_A = I_s R_1 = 0.1 \times 20000 = 2000$ V. Since no current flows through $R_2$, the voltage at the top terminal of $C$ is the same as $v_A$. Therefore, $V_{th} = 2000$ V.

The circuit for $t \ge 0$ is a series RC circuit with $V_{th} = 2000$ V, $R_{th} = 25$ k$\Omega$, and $C = 0.2$ mF. The time constant is $\tau = R_{th} C = 25000 \times 0.0002 = 5$ s. The capacitor voltage starts at 0 and approaches $V_{th} = 2000$ V:
$$v_C(t) = 2000 (1 - e^{-t/5}) \text{ V}$$

The current through the capacitor branch is:
$$i_2(t) = C \frac{dv_C}{dt} = 0.0002 \times 2000 \times \frac{1}{5} e^{-t/5} = 0.08 e^{-t/5} \text{ A}$$

The voltage across the parallel branches is:
$$v(t) = v_{R2} + v_C = i_2 R_2 + v_C = 0.08 e^{-t/5} \times 5000 + 2000 (1 - e^{-t/5}) = 400 e^{-t/5} + 2000 - 2000 e^{-t/5} = 2000 - 1600 e^{-t/5} \text{ V}$$

The current through $R_1$ is:
$$i_1(t) = \frac{v(t)}{R_1} = \frac{2000 - 1600 e^{-t/5}}{20000} = 0.1 - 0.08 e^{-t/5} \text{ A}$$
$$i_1(t) = 100 (1 - 0.8 e^{-0.2t}) \text{ mA}$$
Answer: (a) $20\ \text{mA}$; (b) $80\ \text{mA}$; (c) $i_1(t) = 100(1 - 0.8e^{-0.2t})\ \text{mA}$
P09 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.22, p. 259

A circuit consists of a 10 V DC source, a resistor R1 = 40 kΩ, and a capacitor C = 10 nF in series. A switch in series with the source has been closed for a long time and opens at t = 0. After opening, the capacitor discharges through a resistor R2 = 60 kΩ connected in parallel with the capacitor (the source and R1 are disconnected). (a) Find the initial voltage across the capacitor. (b) Find the time constant for t > 0. (c) Find the expression for the capacitor voltage v(t) for t >= 0. (d) Find the time required to dissipate 75% of the initial energy stored in the capacitor.

(a) For t < 0, the switch is closed and the circuit is in DC steady state. The capacitor acts as an open circuit. The voltage across the capacitor is equal to the source voltage. $v_C(0^-) = 10$ V. Thus, $v_C(0^+) = 10$ V. (b) For t > 0, the source and R1 are disconnected. The capacitor discharges through R2 = 60 kΩ. The time constant is $\tau = R_2 C = 60000 \times 10 \times 10^{-9} = 600 \times 10^{-6} = 0.6$ ms. (c) The voltage decays as $v(t) = v_C(0^+) e^{-t/\tau} = 10 e^{-t/0.0006} = 10 e^{-1666.7t}$ V. (d) The energy stored in the capacitor is $w(t) = \frac{1}{2} C v(t)^2 = \frac{1}{2} C v_C(0^+)^2 e^{-2t/\tau} = w(0) e^{-2t/\tau}$. We want to dissipate 75% of the initial energy, which means 25% of the energy remains. So, $w(t) = 0.25 w(0)$. This implies $e^{-2t/\tau} = 0.25$. Taking the natural log: $-\frac{2t}{\tau} = \ln(0.25) = -\ln(4)$. So, $\frac{2t}{\tau} = \ln(4)$. $t = \frac{\tau \ln(4)}{2} = \frac{0.6 \text{ ms} \times 1.3863}{2} \approx 0.4159$ ms.
Answer: (a) $10\ \text{V}$; (b) $0.6\ \text{ms}$; (c) $v(t) = 10e^{-1666.7t}\ \text{V}$; (d) $0.416\ \text{ms}$
P10 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.23, p. 259

A circuit consists of a 30 V DC source, a resistor $R_1 = 10 \text{ k}\Omega$, and a resistor $R_2 = 30 \text{ k}\Omega$ in series. A capacitor $C = 5 \text{ nF}$ is connected in parallel with $R_2$. A switch in series with the source has been closed for a long time and opens at $t = 0$. Find the voltage across the capacitor, $v_C(t)$, for $t \ge 0$.

For t < 0, the switch is closed and the circuit is in DC steady state. The capacitor acts as an open circuit. The voltage across the capacitor is the voltage across R2. Using the voltage divider rule: $v_C(0^-) = V_{source} \frac{R_2}{R_1 + R_2} = 30 \frac{30}{10 + 30} = 30 \frac{30}{40} = 30 \times 0.75 = 22.5$ V. Thus, $v_C(0^+) = 22.5$ V. For t >= 0, the switch opens, disconnecting the source and R1. The capacitor discharges through R2. The time constant is $\tau = R_2 C = 30000 \times 5 \times 10^{-9} = 150 \times 10^{-6} = 0.15$ ms. The voltage decays as $v_C(t) = v_C(0^+) e^{-t/\tau} = 22.5 e^{-t/0.00015} = 22.5 e^{-6666.7t}$ V.
Answer: $v_C(t) = 22.5e^{-6666.7t} \text{ V}$

📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.24, p. 259

A circuit consists of a $10\text{ V}$ DC source in series with a $5\text{ k}\Omega$ resistor and a switch. The switch has been open for a long time and closes at $t = 0$. The load consists of two capacitors connected in series: $C_1 = 2\text{ \mu F}$ and $C_2 = 6\text{ \mu F}$. Find the voltage across the equivalent capacitor, $v_o(t)$, for $t \ge 0$.

First, determine the equivalent capacitance of the series combination. For two capacitors in series, $C_{eq} = \frac{C_1 C_2}{C_1 + C_2}$. Substituting the values, $C_{eq} = \frac{2 \times 6}{2 + 6} \mu F = \frac{12}{8} \mu F = 1.5 \mu F$. The time constant of the RC circuit is $\tau = R C_{eq} = (5 \times 10^3 \Omega)(1.5 \times 10^{-6} F) = 7.5 \times 10^{-3} s = 7.5 ms$. The initial voltage across the equivalent capacitor is $v_o(0) = 0 V$ since the switch was open for a long time. The final voltage as $t \to \infty$ is the source voltage, $v_o(\infty) = 10 V$. The general solution for the step response is $v_o(t) = v_o(\infty) + [v_o(0) - v_o(\infty)] e^{-t/\tau}$. Substituting the values, $v_o(t) = 10 + [0 - 10] e^{-t/0.0075} = 10(1 - e^{-133.33t}) V$.
Answer: $$v_o(t) = 10\left(1 - e^{-133.33t}\right) \text{ V}$$
P12 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.49, p. 263

A circuit contains a $12$ V DC source connected to a parallel combination of a $4$ k$\Omega$ resistor and a $2$ H inductor. A switch in series with the source has been open for a long time and closes at $t = 0$. Find the inductor current $i_L(t)$ for $t \ge 0$.

Before t = 0, the switch is open, so no current flows through the inductor. Thus, $i_L(0) = 0 A$. For t > 0, the switch is closed. The inductor sees a Thevenin equivalent circuit. The Thevenin voltage is the source voltage, $V_{th} = 12 V$. The Thevenin resistance is the resistance in parallel with the inductor, which is just the 4 kΩ resistor since the voltage source is shorted for resistance calculation. So, $R_{th} = 4 k\Omega$. The time constant is $\tau = \frac{L}{R_{th}} = \frac{2 H}{4000 \Omega} = 0.5 \times 10^{-3} s = 0.5 ms$. The final current as $t \to \infty$ is $i_L(\infty) = \frac{V_{th}}{R_{th}} = \frac{12 V}{4000 \Omega} = 3 mA$. The expression for the current is $i_L(t) = i_L(\infty) + [i_L(0) - i_L(\infty)] e^{-t/\tau}$. Substituting the values, $i_L(t) = 3 + [0 - 3] e^{-t/0.0005} = 3(1 - e^{-2000t}) mA$.
Answer: $i_L(t) = 3(1 - e^{-2000t}) \text{ mA}$
P13 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Nils & Riedel 12e, Prob 7.54, p. 263

A $5 \text{ mA}$ current source is connected in parallel with a $10 \text{ k}\Omega$ resistor and a $0.5 \text{ \mu F}$ capacitor. A switch in series with the current source has been open for a long time and closes at $t = 0$. The capacitor is initially uncharged. (a) Find the capacitor voltage $v(t)$ for $t \ge 0$. (b) Find the current through the capacitor $i_C(t)$ for $t \ge 0$.

The voltage expression is $v(t) = v(\infty) + [v(0) - v(\infty)] e^{-t/\tau} = 50 + [0 - 50] e^{-t/0.005} = 50(1 - e^{-200t}) V$. (b) The current through the capacitor is $i_C(t) = C \frac{dv}{dt}$. Differentiating $v(t)$, we get $\frac{dv}{dt} = 50(200) e^{-200t} = 10000 e^{-200t} V/s$. Thus, $i_C(t) = (0.5 \times 10^{-6} F)(10000 e^{-200t}) = 5 \times 10^{-3} e^{-200t} A = 5 e^{-200t} mA$.

The voltage across the resistor in a source-free RL circuit is $v_R(t) = v_R(0) e^{-t/\tau}$. Given $v_R(0) = 10$ V and $v_R(10 \text{ ms}) = 3.68$ V, we have $3.68 = 10 e^{-10\text{ms}/\tau}$. Dividing by 10, $0.368 = e^{-10\text{ms}/\tau}$. Taking the natural logarithm, $\ln(0.368) \approx -1$. So $-1 = -10\text{ms}/\tau$, which gives $\tau = 10$ ms. For part (b), $\tau = L/R$. With $L = 5$ mH and $\tau = 10$ ms, $R = L/\tau = 5 \times 10^{-3} / 10 \times 10^{-3} = 0.5$ Ω. For part (c), if $R = 100$ Ω, then $i_L(0) = v_R(0) / R = 10 / 100 = 0.1$ A.
Answer: $\tau = 10\ \text{ms}$, $R = 0.5\ \Omega$, $i_L(0) = 0.1\ \text{A}$
P30 · Circuit Problem
📖 Adapted from Hayt 8e, Prob 8.6 #47, p. 316

A circuit contains a 2 mA DC current source in series with a switch that closes at t = 0. This branch is in parallel with a 100 Ω resistor and a 2 mH inductor. (a) Find the inductor current $i_L(0^-)$ and $i_L(0^+)$. (b) Find the inductor voltage $v_L(0^+)$. (c) Calculate the inductor current $i_L(50 \text{ ns})$.

For t < 0, the switch is open, so no current flows from the source. The inductor current is $i_L(0^-) = 0$ A. Since inductor current cannot change instantaneously, $i_L(0^+) = 0$ A. For t > 0, the switch closes. The circuit is a parallel RL circuit driven by a current source. The time constant is $\tau = L / R = 2 \times 10^{-3} / 100 = 20 \times 10^{-6}$ s = 20 µs. The final current is $i_L(\infty) = 2$ mA (all current goes through the inductor in DC steady state). The current response is $i_L(t) = i_L(\infty) + (i_L(0) - i_L(\infty))e^{-t/\tau} = 2 + (0 - 2)e^{-t/20\mu s} = 2(1 - e^{-50000t})$ mA. The inductor voltage is $v_L(t) = L \frac{di_L}{dt} = (2 \times 10^{-3}) \frac{d}{dt} (2 \times 10^{-3} (1 - e^{-50000t})) = (2 \times 10^{-3})(2 \times 10^{-3})(50000)e^{-50000t} = 0.2 e^{-50000t}$ V. At $t = 0^+$, $v_L(0^+) = 0.2$ V. At $t = 50$ ns, $i_L(50\text{ns}) = 2(1 - e^{-50000 \times 50 \times 10^{-9}}) = 2(1 - e^{-0.0025}) \approx 2(1 - 0.9975) = 2(0.0025) = 0.005$ mA = 5 µA.
Answer: $i_L(0^-) = 0$, $i_L(0^+) = 0$, $v_L(0^+) = 0.2 \text{ V}$, $i_L(50 \text{ ns}) = 5 \text{ \mu A}$
