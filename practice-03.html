P01 · Circuit Problem

A 100 µF capacitor is charged to 10 V. It is then disconnected from the source and connected to a 5 kΩ resistor. Calculate the time constant $\tau$ and the voltage across the capacitor at $t = 2\tau$.

1. Identify parameters: $C = 100 \, \mu\text{F} = 100 \times 10^{-6} \, \text{F}$, $R = 5 \, \text{k}\Omega = 5000 \, \Omega$.
2. Calculate time constant: $\tau = RC = (5000)(100 \times 10^{-6}) = 0.5 \, \text{s}$.
3. The voltage equation for source-free RC is $v(t) = V_0 e^{-t/\tau}$, with $V_0 = 10 \, \text{V}$.
4. At $t = 2\tau$: $v(2\tau) = 10 \cdot e^{-2} \approx 10 \cdot 0.1353 = 1.353 \, \text{V}$.
Answer: \tau = 0.5 \, \text{s}, \, v(2\tau) \approx 1.35 \, \text{V}
P02 · Circuit Problem

An inductor of 2 H carries a current of 4 A. It is then disconnected from the source and connected to a 10 Ω resistor. Find the initial voltage across the inductor and the time constant.

1. Identify parameters: $L = 2 \, \text{H}$, $R = 10 \, \Omega$, $I_0 = 4 \, \text{A}$.
2. Calculate time constant: $\tau = L/R = 2/10 = 0.2 \, \text{s}$.
3. The current equation is $i(t) = I_0 e^{-t/\tau} = 4 e^{-5t}$.
4. The voltage across the inductor is $v_L(t) = L \frac{di}{dt}$. Alternatively, using KVL for the loop: $v_L + v_R = 0 \Rightarrow v_L = -v_R = -iR$.
5. At $t=0^+$, $i(0^+) = 4 \, \text{A}$. So $v_L(0^+) = -(4)(10) = -40 \, \text{V}$.
Answer: v_L(0^+) = -40 \, \text{V}, \, \tau = 0.2 \, \text{s}
P03 · Circuit Problem

A series RC circuit consists of a 12 V DC source, a 1 kΩ resistor, and a 10 µF capacitor. The switch closes at $t=0$ and the capacitor is initially uncharged. Find the voltage across the capacitor at $t = 5 \, \text{ms}$.

1. Identify parameters: $V_s = 12 \, \text{V}$, $R = 1000 \, \Omega$, $C = 10 \, \mu\text{F} = 10 \times 10^{-6} \, \text{F}$.
2. Initial condition: $v(0) = 0 \, \text{V}$.
3. Final value (steady state): $v(\infty) = V_s = 12 \, \text{V}$ (capacitor acts as open circuit).
4. Time constant: $\tau = RC = (1000)(10 \times 10^{-6}) = 0.01 \, \text{s} = 10 \, \text{ms}$.
5. Step response formula: $v(t) = v(\infty) + [v(0) - v(\infty)]e^{-t/\tau} = 12 + (0 - 12)e^{-t/0.01} = 12(1 - e^{-100t})$.
6. At $t = 5 \, \text{ms} = 0.005 \, \text{s}$: $v(0.005) = 12(1 - e^{-100(0.005)}) = 12(1 - e^{-0.5})$.
7. $e^{-0.5} \approx 0.6065$. $v(0.005) = 12(1 - 0.6065) = 12(0.3935) \approx 4.72 \, \text{V}$.
Answer: v(5 ms) \approx 4.72 \, \text{V}
P04 · Circuit Problem

A series RL circuit consists of a 20 V DC source, a 5 Ω resistor, and a 1 H inductor. The switch closes at $t=0$ and the inductor current is initially zero. Find the current through the inductor at $t = 1 \, \text{s}$.

1. Identify parameters: $V_s = 20 \, \text{V}$, $R = 5 \, \Omega$, $L = 1 \, \text{H}$.
2. Initial condition: $i(0) = 0 \, \text{A}$.
3. Final value (steady state): $i(\infty) = V_s/R = 20/5 = 4 \, \text{A}$ (inductor acts as short circuit).
4. Time constant: $\tau = L/R = 1/5 = 0.2 \, \text{s}$.
5. Step response formula: $i(t) = i(\infty) + [i(0) - i(\infty)]e^{-t/\tau} = 4 + (0 - 4)e^{-t/0.2} = 4(1 - e^{-5t})$.
6. At $t = 1 \, \text{s}$: $i(1) = 4(1 - e^{-5})$.
7. $e^{-5} \approx 0.0067$. $i(1) = 4(1 - 0.0067) = 4(0.9933) \approx 3.97 \, \text{A}$.
Answer: i(1 s) \approx 3.97 \, \text{A}
P05 · Circuit Problem

In the circuit shown, the switch has been in position 1 for a long time. At $t=0$, it moves to position 2. Find $v(0^+)$ and $i(0^+)$ for the capacitor branch. Position 1 connects a 10 V source to a 2 kΩ resistor and a 5 µF capacitor. Position 2 connects the capacitor to a 5 kΩ resistor.

1. For $t < 0$ (Position 1): DC steady state. Capacitor is open circuit. No current flows through the 2 kΩ resistor.
2. Voltage across capacitor: $v(0^-) = 10 \, \text{V}$ (full source voltage).
3. Continuity: $v(0^+) = v(0^-) = 10 \, \text{V}$.
4. For $t > 0$ (Position 2): The capacitor is connected to the 5 kΩ resistor. The source is disconnected.
5. The circuit is a source-free RC loop. The voltage across the resistor is equal to the capacitor voltage (with opposite polarity if defined by passive sign convention for discharge current, but magnitude is $V_C$).
6. Current $i(0^+)$ flows from the positive plate of the capacitor through the resistor. $i(0^+) = v(0^+) / R_{discharge} = 10 \, \text{V} / 5 \, \text{k}\Omega = 2 \, \text{mA}$.
7. Direction: If $i$ is defined as flowing out of the positive terminal, $i(0^+) = 2 \, \text{mA}$. If defined as entering the positive terminal (charging convention), it would be $-2 \, \text{mA}$. Assuming standard discharge current direction out of +: $2 \, \text{mA}$.
Answer: v(0^+) = 10 \, \text{V}, \, i(0^+) = 2 \, \text{mA}
P06 · Circuit Problem

A 1 H inductor carries a current of 5 A. At $t=0$, it is connected to a parallel combination of a 10 Ω resistor and a 5 Ω resistor. Find the time constant of the circuit and the current at $t = 0.5 \, \text{s}$.

1. Identify parameters: $L = 1 \, \text{H}$, $I_0 = 5 \, \text{A}$.
2. Equivalent resistance: The inductor sees the two resistors in parallel. $R_{eq} = (10 \times 5) / (10 + 5) = 50/15 = 10/3 \, \Omega \approx 3.33 \, \Omega$.
3. Time constant: $\tau = L / R_{eq} = 1 / (10/3) = 0.3 \, \text{s}$.
4. Current equation: $i(t) = I_0 e^{-t/\tau} = 5 e^{-t/0.3} = 5 e^{-3.33t}$.
5. At $t = 0.5 \, \text{s}$: $i(0.5) = 5 e^{-0.5/0.3} = 5 e^{-1.667}$.
6. $e^{-1.667} \approx 0.1889$.
7. $i(0.5) = 5 \times 0.1889 \approx 0.945 \, \text{A}$.
Answer: \tau = 0.3 \, \text{s}, \, i(0.5) \approx 0.945 \, \text{A}
P07 · Circuit Problem

A series RC circuit has a 15 V source, a 2 kΩ resistor, and a 2 µF capacitor. The capacitor is initially charged to 5 V with polarity opposing the source. Find the expression for $v(t)$ for $t \ge 0$ and the time when $v(t) = 10 \, \text{V}$.

1. Parameters: $V_s = 15 \, \text{V}$, $R = 2000 \, \Omega$, $C = 2 \, \mu\text{F} = 2 \times 10^{-6} \, \text{F}$.
2. Initial condition: $v(0) = -5 \, \text{V}$ (opposing polarity).
3. Final value: $v(\infty) = 15 \, \text{V}$.
4. Time constant: $\tau = RC = (2000)(2 \times 10^{-6}) = 0.004 \, \text{s} = 4 \, \text{ms}$.
5. General solution: $v(t) = v(\infty) + [v(0) - v(\infty)]e^{-t/\tau} = 15 + [-5 - 15]e^{-t/0.004} = 15 - 20e^{-250t}$.
6. Find $t$ when $v(t) = 10 \, \text{V}$:
$10 = 15 - 20e^{-250t}$
$20e^{-250t} = 5$
$e^{-250t} = 0.25$
$-250t = \ln(0.25) = -1.3863$
$t = 1.3863 / 250 \approx 0.005545 \, \text{s} = 5.55 \, \text{ms}$.
Answer: v(t) = 15 - 20e^{-250t} \, \text{V}, \, t \approx 5.55 \, \text{ms}
P08 · Circuit Problem

An RL circuit consists of a 10 V source, a 4 Ω resistor, and a 0.5 H inductor in series. The switch closes at $t=0$. Find the voltage across the inductor at $t = 0^+$ and at $t = \tau$.

1. Parameters: $V_s = 10 \, \text{V}$, $R = 4 \, \Omega$, $L = 0.5 \, \text{H}$.
2. Time constant: $\tau = L/R = 0.5/4 = 0.125 \, \text{s}$.
3. Current equation: $i(t) = \frac{V_s}{R}(1 - e^{-t/\tau}) = 2.5(1 - e^{-8t})$.
4. Inductor voltage: $v_L(t) = L \frac{di}{dt}$.
$\frac{di}{dt} = 2.5 \cdot (-e^{-8t}) \cdot (-8) = 20 e^{-8t}$.
$v_L(t) = 0.5 \cdot 20 e^{-8t} = 10 e^{-8t} \, \text{V}$.
Alternatively, KVL: $v_L(t) = V_s - v_R(t) = 10 - i(t)R = 10 - 2.5(1 - e^{-8t})(4) = 10 - 10(1 - e^{-8t}) = 10e^{-8t}$.
5. At $t = 0^+$: $v_L(0^+) = 10 e^0 = 10 \, \text{V}$.
6. At $t = \tau = 0.125 \, \text{s}$: $v_L(\tau) = 10 e^{-8(0.125)} = 10 e^{-1} \approx 10(0.3679) = 3.68 \, \text{V}$.
Answer: v_L(0^+) = 10 \, \text{V}, \, v_L(\tau) \approx 3.68 \, \text{V}
P09 · Circuit Problem

A capacitor of 100 µF is charged to 20 V. It is discharged through a resistor. If the voltage drops to 7.36 V after 0.5 seconds, find the value of the resistor.

1. Source-free RC equation: $v(t) = V_0 e^{-t/\tau}$.
2. Given: $V_0 = 20 \, \text{V}$, $v(0.5) = 7.36 \, \text{V}$, $t = 0.5 \, \text{s}$, $C = 100 \, \mu\text{F} = 10^{-4} \, \text{F}$.
3. Substitute values: $7.36 = 20 e^{-0.5/\tau}$.
4. $e^{-0.5/\tau} = 7.36 / 20 = 0.368$.
5. Take natural log: $-0.5/\tau = \ln(0.368) \approx -1.0$.
6. $0.5/\tau = 1 \Rightarrow \tau = 0.5 \, \text{s}$.
7. Since $\tau = RC$, $R = \tau / C = 0.5 / 10^{-4} = 5000 \, \Omega = 5 \, \text{k}\Omega$.
Answer: R = 5 kΩ
P10 · Circuit Problem

In a series RL circuit, the current rises from 0 to 63.2% of its final value in 0.2 seconds. If the resistance is 10 Ω, find the inductance.

1. The current reaches 63.2% of its final value at $t = \tau$.
2. Given $t = 0.2 \, \text{s}$ corresponds to $\tau$, so $\tau = 0.2 \, \text{s}$.
3. For an RL circuit, $\tau = L/R$.
4. Given $R = 10 \, \Omega$.
5. $L = \tau \cdot R = 0.2 \cdot 10 = 2 \, \text{H}$.
Answer: L = 2 H
P11 · Circuit Problem

A circuit has a 12 V source, a 3 kΩ resistor, and a 4 µF capacitor in series. The switch closes at $t=0$. Determine the time required for the capacitor voltage to reach 9 V.

1. Parameters: $V_s = 12 \, \text{V}$, $R = 3000 \, \Omega$, $C = 4 \, \mu\text{F} = 4 \times 10^{-6} \, \text{F}$.
2. $v(0) = 0$, $v(\infty) = 12 \, \text{V}$.
3. $\tau = RC = 3000 \times 4 \times 10^{-6} = 0.012 \, \text{s} = 12 \, \text{ms}$.
4. Equation: $v(t) = 12(1 - e^{-t/0.012})$.
5. Set $v(t) = 9 \, \text{V}$:
$9 = 12(1 - e^{-t/0.012})$
$9/12 = 1 - e^{-t/0.012}$
$0.75 = 1 - e^{-t/0.012}$
$e^{-t/0.012} = 0.25$
$-t/0.012 = \ln(0.25) \approx -1.3863$
$t = 0.012 \times 1.3863 \approx 0.01664 \, \text{s} = 16.64 \, \text{ms}$.
Answer: t \approx 16.64 ms
P12 · Circuit Problem

An inductor of 4 H is connected to a 20 Ω resistor. The initial current is 8 A. Find the energy dissipated in the resistor during the first time constant.

1. Parameters: $L = 4 \, \text{H}$, $R = 20 \, \Omega$, $I_0 = 8 \, \text{A}$.
2. $\tau = L/R = 4/20 = 0.2 \, \text{s}$.
3. Current: $i(t) = 8 e^{-t/0.2} = 8 e^{-5t}$.
4. Power in resistor: $p(t) = i^2(t) R = (8 e^{-5t})^2 (20) = 64 e^{-10t} \cdot 20 = 1280 e^{-10t} \, \text{W}$.
5. Energy dissipated from $t=0$ to $t=\tau=0.2$:
$W = \int_0^{0.2} 1280 e^{-10t} dt$
$W = 1280 \left[ \frac{e^{-10t}}{-10} \right]_0^{0.2}$
$W = -128 \left[ e^{-2} - e^0 \right] = 128 (1 - e^{-2})$.
6. $e^{-2} \approx 0.1353$.
$W = 128 (1 - 0.1353) = 128 (0.8647) \approx 110.7 \, \text{J}$.
Answer: W \approx 110.7 J
P13 · Circuit Problem

A 50 µF capacitor is charged to 100 V. It is connected to a 20 kΩ resistor. Calculate the current at $t = 0^+$ and the power dissipated in the resistor at $t = \tau$.

1. Parameters: $C = 50 \, \mu\text{F} = 50 \times 10^{-6} \, \text{F}$, $R = 20 \, \text{k}\Omega = 20000 \, \Omega$, $V_0 = 100 \, \text{V}$.
2. $\tau = RC = 20000 \times 50 \times 10^{-6} = 1 \, \text{s}$.
3. Current equation: $i(t) = -\frac{V_0}{R} e^{-t/\tau} = -\frac{100}{20000} e^{-t} = -0.005 e^{-t} \, \text{A} = -5 e^{-t} \, \text{mA}$.
4. At $t = 0^+$: $i(0^+) = -5 \, \text{mA}$.
5. Power at $t = \tau = 1 \, \text{s}$:
$i(1) = -5 e^{-1} \approx -5(0.3679) = -1.8395 \, \text{mA}$.
$P = i^2 R = (-1.8395 \times 10^{-3})^2 \times 20000$.
$P = (3.384 \times 10^{-6}) \times 20000 \approx 0.0677 \, \text{W} = 67.7 \, \text{mW}$.
Answer: i(0^+) = -5 mA, P(\tau) \approx 67.7 mW
P14 · Circuit Problem

A series RL circuit has a 10 V source, 5 Ω resistor, and 2 H inductor. The switch closes at $t=0$. Find the total energy stored in the inductor at $t = 0.5 \, \text{s}$.

1. Parameters: $V_s = 10 \, \text{V}$, $R = 5 \, \Omega$, $L = 2 \, \text{H}$.
2. $\tau = L/R = 2/5 = 0.4 \, \text{s}$.
3. Final current $i(\infty) = 10/5 = 2 \, \text{A}$.
4. Current equation: $i(t) = 2(1 - e^{-t/0.4}) = 2(1 - e^{-2.5t})$.
5. At $t = 0.5 \, \text{s}$:
$i(0.5) = 2(1 - e^{-2.5(0.5)}) = 2(1 - e^{-1.25})$.
$e^{-1.25} \approx 0.2865$.
$i(0.5) = 2(1 - 0.2865) = 2(0.7135) = 1.427 \, \text{A}$.
6. Energy stored: $W_L = \frac{1}{2} L i^2 = 0.5 \times 2 \times (1.427)^2 = 1 \times 2.036 = 2.036 \, \text{J}$.
Answer: W_L \approx 2.04 J
P15 · Circuit Problem

Two capacitors, $C_1 = 10 \, \mu\text{F}$ and $C_2 = 20 \, \mu\text{F}$, are connected in series with a 5 kΩ resistor. The combination is initially charged to 30 V across the series pair. Find the time constant of the discharge circuit.

1. The capacitors are in series. The equivalent capacitance $C_{eq}$ is given by:
$\frac{1}{C_{eq}} = \frac{1}{C_1} + \frac{1}{C_2} = \frac{1}{10} + \frac{1}{20} = \frac{2}{20} + \frac{1}{20} = \frac{3}{20} \, \mu\text{F}^{-1}$.
$C_{eq} = \frac{20}{3} \, \mu\text{F} \approx 6.67 \, \mu\text{F}$.
2. The circuit is a first-order RC circuit with $C_{eq}$ and $R$.
3. $R = 5 \, \text{k}\Omega = 5000 \, \Omega$.
4. Time constant $\tau = R C_{eq} = 5000 \times \frac{20}{3} \times 10^{-6}$.
$\tau = \frac{100000}{3} \times 10^{-6} = \frac{0.1}{3} \approx 0.0333 \, \text{s} = 33.3 \, \text{ms}$.
Answer: \tau \approx 33.3 ms
